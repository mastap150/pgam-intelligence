# Santorini vs Mykonos vs Paros: Which Greek Island Should You Visit?

*By [Author] · [Date] · 9 min read*

The honest comparison of these three islands changed in 2026, and most guides have not
caught up. Santorini now enforces a hard cap of 8,000 cruise passengers a day, charges
arriving cruise visitors up to €20 a head in peak season, and has been formally classified
a "saturated zone" with strict limits on new hotel development.

That is not a footnote. It is the Greek state officially conceding that the island is over
capacity — and it should change how you weigh these three against each other.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Santorini** — the landscape no photograph prepares you for, and the crowds to match. Go
  for the caldera, stay outside Oia, and travel in shoulder season.
- **Mykonos** — the best nightlife and the best beach clubs in the Cyclades, and the highest
  prices in Greece. Go if that is the trip you want; skip it if it isn't.
- **Paros** — the one to pick if you want a Greek island rather than a landmark. Best
  balance of beaches, towns, food and cost.
- **If you can only pick one and you have never been:** Paros, then Santorini for two
  nights on the way out.

---

## What Changed in 2026

Worth understanding before you book anything, because it affects the day-to-day experience
more than any hotel choice.

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Cruise passenger cap, Santorini | **8,000 per day**, hard limit |
| Occupancy calculation | From 2026, ships count at **100% of berths** — a 3,000-berth ship uses 3,000 slots, not its actual manifest |
| Cruise levy, Santorini & Mykonos | **€20** per person, 1 June–30 Sept; **€12** in April–May and October; **€4** in winter |
| How it's charged | Once per port call, all ages, generally waived if you never disembark |
| Development | Santorini classed a "saturated zone" (May 2026) — new hotels capped at 75–100 beds |

Before the cap, day-visitor surges on Santorini reached 11,000 to 17,000 people. The cap
plus the 100%-occupancy rule cuts that meaningfully, and the practical effect is fewer
enormous ships on any given day.

**What it means for you:** Santorini in 2026 and beyond is less crowded on the worst days
than it was, but "less crowded than 17,000 cruise passengers" is not the same as quiet. The
cap does nothing about the people staying on the island, and Oia at sunset remains one of
the most congested square kilometres in Europe.

## Santorini — the Landmark

**Go for:** the caldera. It is a flooded volcanic crater ringed by cliffs with white towns
along the rim, and there is genuinely nothing else like it in the Mediterranean. The
photographs are not exaggerated; they are just cropped.

**The reality check:** Oia at sunset is a crowd-management exercise. People start staking
positions two hours out. The famous blue-domed shot is taken from a specific spot on a
walkway with a queue.

**How to do it properly:**

- **Stay in Imerovigli or Pyrgos, not Oia or Fira.** Imerovigli has the same caldera view
  with a fraction of the foot traffic. Pyrgos is inland, considerably cheaper, and the best
  food on the island.
- **Watch sunset from anywhere except Oia.** The sun sets over the whole west-facing rim.
  Imerovigli's path, the Skaros Rock trail, or a table in Firostefani all deliver the same
  event without the scrum.
- **Walk Fira to Oia** — about 10 km along the rim, three to four hours, and the best thing
  on the island. Start early.
- **Two nights is enough.** Three if you want a boat trip to the volcano.

**Costs are high** and rising — caldera-view rooms in August run several hundred euros a
night. The same room in Pyrgos, without the view, is a third of that.

---

## Mykonos — the Party

**Go for:** the beach clubs, the nightlife, and a town that is genuinely beautiful
underneath the crowds. Mykonos Town's whitewashed lanes are as good as anything in the
Cyclades, early in the morning before the day-trippers land.

**The reality check:** it is the most expensive island in Greece by a wide margin, and the
gap is not subtle. Sunbeds at the well-known beach clubs run into the hundreds of euros for
a pair. Drinks are priced accordingly. There is a long history of visitors being surprised
by bills, and the sensible defence is to ask prices before ordering, every time.

**Who should go:** people who want that specific trip. Mykonos does it better than anywhere
else in Greece and makes no apology for it.

**Who shouldn't:** anyone hoping to find a quiet traditional island underneath. It isn't
there in summer. Come in May or October and it partly is.

---

## Paros — the Balance

**Go for:** the island that does everything reasonably well and nothing punishingly. Two
good towns — Parikia and the harbour at Naoussa — proper beaches, a walkable interior, real
food at real prices, and enough going on in the evening without it being the point.

**The case for Paros as your one island:** it has the best ferry connections in the
Cyclades, which makes it the natural base for day trips — Antiparos is 10 minutes away,
Naxos is close, and Santorini is a straightforward hop. It costs meaningfully less than
either of the others. And it is a place where people live, which the other two increasingly
struggle to be in August.

**The trade-off:** no single landscape moment on the scale of the Santorini caldera. Paros
is a good week; Santorini is a great two days.

**Also worth knowing:** Paros has become notably more popular in recent years and is no
longer the secret it was a decade ago. Naoussa in August is busy. It is still not Mykonos.

## Island Hopping: How the Ferries Actually Work

All three sit on the same central Cyclades network, which is what makes combining them
straightforward.

- **Two ferry types.** High-speed catamarans are roughly twice the price and half the time;
  conventional ferries are slower, cheaper, far more stable in wind, and take cars.
- **Wind cancels boats.** The meltemi blows hard through July and August and high-speed
  services are the first cancelled. Never plan to reach your flight on a same-day ferry —
  build in a night in Athens or on the island you fly from.
- **Book ahead in peak season**, especially if you have a car. Foot passengers have more
  slack.
- **A sensible first-timer route:** Athens → Paros (4 nights) → Santorini (2 nights) → fly
  home from Santorini. One direction, no backtracking, and it ends on the landmark.

## When to Go

| Period | Conditions | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| April–May | 18–24 °C, sea still cold, everything opening | Excellent — cruise levy drops to €12 |
| June | Warm, sea swimmable, busy | Very good |
| July–August | 30 °C+, meltemi winds, peak crowds and prices | Avoid if you have a choice |
| September | Warm, warm sea, easing crowds | **Best month** |
| October | Mild, quiet, some closures late in the month | Excellent value |
| November–March | Many hotels and restaurants closed; ferries reduced | Santorini and Paros just about work; Mykonos doesn't |

**September is the answer** for most people: the sea is at its warmest, the crowds have
thinned, and prices drop from the August peak.

## Beyond the Big Three

If this comparison has made you suspect none of the three is quite what you want, that
instinct is probably right.

- **Naxos** — bigger, greener, working agricultural island with the best beaches in the
  Cyclades and a fraction of the attention. The strongest alternative on this list.
- **Milos** — extraordinary volcanic coastline, Sarakiniko in particular. Busier every year.
- **Folegandros, Sifnos, Serifos** — small, quiet, genuinely traditional. Sifnos is a food
  destination in its own right.
- **Crete** — not a Cyclades island at all but a place in its own right: mountains, gorges,
  Minoan sites, and a coastline that takes weeks. Better for two weeks than for a hop.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is there a tourist tax for Santorini and Mykonos?**
There is a cruise passenger levy, not a general tourist tax on stays. Cruise visitors
disembarking at Santorini or Mykonos pay €20 per person from 1 June to 30 September, €12 in
April, May and October, and €4 in winter. It is charged once per port call and generally
waived if you stay aboard. Greece also applies a separate per-night accommodation levy
across the country.

**Which Greek island is best for first-time visitors?**
Paros, for most people — it balances beaches, towns, food and cost better than either
Santorini or Mykonos, and it has the best ferry connections for day trips. Add two nights
in Santorini if you want the caldera, which is worth seeing once.

**How many days do you need in Santorini?**
Two to three. The island is small, the main sights — the caldera rim, Oia, the Fira–Oia
walk, a volcano boat trip — fit comfortably into that, and costs are high enough that
longer stays are hard to justify unless you are there to do nothing.

**Can you visit Santorini, Mykonos and Paros in one trip?**
Yes — they sit on the same central Cyclades ferry network and all three are connected
directly. Allow at least ten days to do it without spending most of the trip in transit, and
avoid scheduling a ferry on the day you fly home; summer winds cancel high-speed services
regularly.

---

## The Bottom Line

Paros is the island most people should actually book, Santorini is worth two nights for a
landscape nothing else matches, and Mykonos is worth it only if its specific brand of
expensive fun is what you came for. Go in September.
