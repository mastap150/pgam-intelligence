# A Realistic Travel Budget Template — Plan Any Trip Without Surprises

*By [Author] · [Date] · 7 min read*

Most travel budgets fail the same two ways: they count the flight and the hotel and treat
everything else as "spending money", and they carry no contingency.

Here is a structure that survives contact with an actual trip.

---

## The Quick Answer

**Five categories:** Fixed costs → Daily costs → Activity costs → Pre-trip costs →
Contingency.

- **Contingency should be 10–15%** of the total, and it is not optional.
- **The two costs people always forget:** getting to and from airports at both ends, and
  the money spent in the 48 hours around travel days.
- **Budget per day, not per trip**, for anything that scales with time.
- **Track the first three days** against the plan, then adjust the rest.

---

## The Five Categories

### 1. Fixed costs — paid once, regardless of trip length

- International flights
- Visas and entry fees
- Travel insurance
- Vaccinations
- Rail passes or car hire booked as a block
- Gear bought specifically for this trip

### 2. Daily costs — multiply by number of days

| Line | Notes |
|---|---|
| Accommodation | Per night, not per day — one fewer night than days |
| Food and drink | Be honest; this is where estimates fail |
| Local transport | Metro, buses, occasional taxis |
| Incidentals | Water, tips, laundry, SIM top-ups |

**The realistic-estimate trick:** take your best guess for food and add 30%. Almost everyone
underestimates it, because holiday eating is not home eating.

### 3. Activity costs — listed individually, not averaged

Museums, tours, permits, dives, guides, tickets. **List each one by name with its actual
price.** Averaging these is how budgets drift — the safari, the permit or the cruise is not a
"daily cost", it is a line item worth hundreds.

### 4. Pre-trip costs — the invisible category

- Airport transfers **at both ends** — routinely forgotten and routinely $50–$150 the pair
- Airport parking or long-stay
- Baggage fees
- Kennels, pet sitting, house sitting
- Currency exchange spread and foreign transaction fees
- The pre-trip shop — toiletries, adaptors, a bag

### 5. Contingency — 10–15%

**Not optional and not a slush fund.** It covers the missed connection, the cancelled ferry,
the day you are ill, the thing that broke, and the once-in-a-lifetime opportunity you did not
plan for.

**Trips with more moving parts need more:** 10% for a city break, 15%+ for multi-country or
remote travel where a single logistics failure costs a day and a rebooking.

## Setting the Daily Number

Work backwards from what you will actually do.

| Style | Typical daily range |
|---|---|
| Backpacker, Southeast Asia | $25–$45 |
| Mid-range, Southeast Asia | $50–$90 |
| Backpacker, Western Europe | $60–$90 |
| Mid-range, Western Europe | $130–$190 |
| Mid-range, USA / Japan | $150–$250 |

**Adjust for:** how much you eat out, whether you drink, whether you are travelling as a pair
(accommodation halves per person, food does not), and how fast you move — **travel days are
expensive days.**

---

## Tracking It Without Ruining the Trip

- **Log spending daily**, in a note or an app. Three minutes.
- **Check against plan after day three.** If you are 30% over, you have eleven days to adjust
  rather than a nasty surprise at the end.
- **Track by category**, not just total, so you can see *what* is over.
- **Do not budget to the last dollar.** A budget you resent is a budget you abandon.

## The Costs People Forget

A checklist. Almost every overrun is on this list.

- Airport transfers at **both** ends
- Foreign transaction fees and ATM charges
- Tips — significant in the US, on safari, and for guides and drivers generally
- Baggage fees on budget carriers, including the return leg
- Departure taxes and tourist levies paid on arrival
- Laundry on trips over a week
- The "last night" splurge, which everyone has
- Souvenirs and gifts
- Data or eSIM top-ups
- Vaccinations and antimalarials, which can run to hundreds

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much contingency should a travel budget have?**
10–15% of the total. Ten per cent for a straightforward city break, fifteen or more for
multi-country or remote trips where one logistics failure costs a day and a rebooking. It is
not a slush fund — it is the thing that stops the budget being wrong.

**How do you estimate a daily travel budget?**
Break it into accommodation, food, local transport and incidentals, estimate each separately,
then add roughly 30% to the food figure — that is the line almost everyone underestimates.
Multiply by the number of days, remembering you have one fewer night than days.

**What costs do people forget when budgeting a trip?**
Airport transfers at both ends, foreign transaction fees, tips, return-leg baggage fees,
departure taxes and tourist levies paid on arrival, laundry, and vaccinations. Together these
routinely add several hundred dollars to a trip nobody planned for.

**Should you budget per day or per trip?**
Both. Fixed costs, activities and pre-trip costs are per-trip line items; accommodation, food
and local transport are per-day and multiply. Averaging a big activity like a safari or a
permit into a daily rate is the most common way budgets drift.

---

## The Bottom Line

Use five categories rather than two, list every activity by name and price, remember the
airport transfers at both ends, and carry 10–15% contingency.
