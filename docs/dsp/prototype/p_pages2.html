                Attention

                The measure that is ours, and the one people ask about most

                What is the attention score made of?

                Three things, each measured separately on every impression and then averaged, weighted by the spend that produced them:

                • On screen — the television was on and your ad was the thing playing, rather than running behind a paused game or on a set that had been switched off at the wall.
                • Sound on — not muted. This is most of the gap between streaming TV and web video.
                • Watched to the end — the spot completed rather than being skipped or abandoned.

                The Attention page shows all three, per campaign.

                Is 77 good?

                Yes. The open web sits around 64 on the same three components, so a streaming buy that scores in the mid-to-high 70s is doing what you bought it to do. Below about 65 on a CTV campaign, something is wrong — usually the inventory mix rather than the creative.

                Judge the trend more than the level. A score that falls while spend rises is the earliest warning you get that a campaign is scaling into worse inventory.

                Why does a campaign show no score at all?

                It needs 48 hours on air before there are enough impressions to score. Until then it is excluded from the account average rather than counted as zero — counting it as zero would drag the number down for something that simply has not run yet.

                A dash on this product never means zero. It means not measured.

                Attention is high but I have no leads. What does that mean?

                That the media was bought well and the offer is not landing. Attention says people watched; it says nothing about whether they wanted the thing. When attention is high and results are thin, change the message, the offer or the landing page — not the targeting.

                The reverse is rarer and more urgent: leads holding up while attention falls usually means you are harvesting existing demand while the campaign quietly stops building any.

                Does a longer spot score better?

                On this account, yes — 30-second cuts average 77 against 74 for the 15. A longer spot has more chance of being abandoned, so the gap is not obvious in advance, but it runs the same direction on every campaign you have run.

                The cheaper impressions a 15 buys are not cheaper per lead once completion is accounted for. Worth testing a 30-second cutdown before scaling a 15.

                Can I see attention by app, or by time of day?

                Yes. Every row of Where it ran on Results carries its own attention score — by day, by market, by genre, by app and by device. Each cut rolls up to the same account figure of 77, so you can compare across them without the numbers arguing.

                The sharpest one is usually device: on this account, connected TV scores 79 while phone is 66 and desktop 61.

                Is this the same as viewability?

                No. Viewability asks whether an ad had the opportunity to be seen — pixels in the viewport for a minimum time. Attention asks whether the conditions for it actually being watched were present: on screen, audible, completed.

                A muted autoplay in a background tab can be perfectly viewable. It scores badly here, which is the point.

                How is attention actually measured?

                From signals the device and the player report back: screen state, audio state, and the quartile completion events in the video ad response. It is not a panel, not a survey and not modelled from a sample — it is scored on the impressions you actually bought.

                Refreshed hourly as delivery comes in. Figures for the last few hours are partial and will move as the rest of the day settles.

                What is attention not?

                It is not a measure of whether the campaign worked. A spot can be watched closely by people who were never going to call. Attention tells you the media was bought well; Results tells you whether it earned anything.

                Both moving the right way at once — which is the case here — is the state in which scaling is the obvious next move.

                Measurement and attribution

                Why the numbers are what they are

                Why do your numbers differ from Google Analytics?

                GA4 credits the last click before a purchase. There are no clicks on streaming television, so Attune matches a lead to an ad by view-through: the household saw the ad, then called or filled in a form within the attribution window.

                The two will never agree and neither is wrong — they are measuring different things. GA4 will systematically under-credit television, because the medium leaves no click to credit.

                How does a household get matched to a lead?

                By IP. The television that played the ad and the phone or laptop that later visited your site are usually on the same home network, so they share an address. That match is what turns an impression into an attributed visit.

                It is a household-level match, not a person-level one. If two people in a house both look you up, that is one household and two events.

                What is the attribution window, and can I change it?

                14 days by default: a call or a form fill counts if it happens within 14 days of the household seeing the ad. You can change it per campaign on Results, and the figure applied is always printed beside the numbers it produced.

                A shorter window is more conservative and will show fewer leads. Changing it re-attributes history rather than only affecting new delivery, so the whole series moves — which is why the window in force is stated rather than assumed.

                I can see events on the site tag but zero leads in Results. Why?

                The site tag reports every event from every source — organic, paid, direct, someone typing your name into a browser. Results only counts events it can attribute to an Attune campaign.

                Early in a flight it is normal to see traffic on one and nothing on the other. Attribution needs delivery before it can match anything.

                What counts as a lead?

                A phone call to your tracked number that lasted long enough to be a real conversation, or a form fill on a page carrying the site tag. Both are counted once, inside the attribution window. Your split is currently 68% calls.

                Can two campaigns both claim the same lead?

                No. A lead is credited once, to the last campaign that reached that household inside the window. The campaign totals on Results therefore add up to the account total exactly — if they did not, one of them would be counting someone else's work.

                Why does a week show spend but no leads?

                Because measurement was not connected yet. The first week of May on this account is exactly that: spend served, nothing counted. It is shown as a dash and excluded from the cost-per-lead maths rather than counted as a zero, which would make that week look catastrophic instead of unmeasured.

                How fresh is the data?

                Delivery updates hourly. The most recent few hours are partial — impressions settle behind live, so today's figures will rise for a while after you look at them.

                Leads lag further, by the length of the attribution window. Any campaign younger than 14 days has more leads coming that are not on the page yet.

                Money and pacing

                Prepaid, drawn in arrears, never in advance

                What happens when the wallet hits zero?

                Delivery stops and every campaign moves to Out of funds. Nothing is cancelled and nothing is lost; they resume automatically the moment the wallet is funded.

                The cost is the gap in the flight. A campaign that restarts after a break relearns its audience and is usually more expensive for a few days than it was before it stopped.

                Why is a campaign "under-pacing"?

                It is spending slower than its budget divided by its flight length. Usually the audience is too narrow, the bid is too low, or the wallet ran dry mid-flight.

                Under-pacing is not failure. It often means an efficient line has headroom — Sequential Retargeting is your cheapest source of leads and your most under-paced campaign at the same time. But the budget goes unspent unless something changes.

                Is a typical cost per lead $97?

                For local streaming television, $60–$150 is the usual band. It moves with how competitive your category is and how tight your radius is. Yours is $97.06 and falling.

                Compare it against your own history rather than the band. A number inside the band that is rising is worse news than a number above it that is coming down.

                What am I actually charged for?

                Impressions that served. Not impressions bid on, not impressions won and dropped. Delivery is drawn from the wallet weekly in arrears, after the fact — which is why the transaction list shows a draw dated after the week it covers.

                Is there a minimum, or a contract?

                No minimum and no commitment. Whatever is left in the wallet stays yours; you can stop a campaign at any point and the unspent balance is unaffected.

                Why does adding a campaign shorten my runway so much?

                Because runway is the whole account's, not one campaign's. A new campaign's daily pace is added to what is already burning — $12,000 a month is about $429 a day on top of the $2,326 already running, which takes seven days of cover down to six.

                The builder does that arithmetic on the review step as you type the budget, rather than after you launch.

                Campaigns and targeting

                What the choices actually do

                What is the PGAM Optimized Network?

                One line across all 53 destinations, with the mix rebalanced weekly toward whatever is producing leads for you — not toward whatever is cheapest to buy.

                It is not a black box. It only moves budget between destinations you are already eligible for, it never bids past your cap, and every week's mix is on the campaign's Where it ran panel. Switch to choosing channels yourself at any point and the mix freezes where the optimizer left it.

                Should I narrow the targeting?

                Usually not, and never by default. Every filter — age, income, daypart, channel, genre — is a hard exclusion. Each one shrinks the pool, raises the cost per result and slows down how fast the campaign learns.

                The one case where narrowing genuinely helps is device: connected TV scores 79 on attention while phone and desktop score 66 and 61. That is the rare filter that raises quality rather than just cutting volume.

                How big should my radius be?

                Big enough that the budget can be seen more than once. The reach bar in the builder shows how many times each household will see the ad at your budget — under about once a month, nobody remembers it, and you have bought a very wide silence.

                Your Q1 pilot ran at 8 miles and cost $142.86 a lead, the dearest campaign on the account. Everything since has run 12–25 miles and nothing has been above $107.

                Why is DMA targeting greyed out for some markets?

                Because we can only guarantee 38 of roughly 210 US markets today. DMA is applied through a crosswalk between Nielsen's market codes and the ad server's own metro ids, and the rest are not yet matched.

                An unmatched market would be accepted and then silently dropped — you would be targeting nothing and not know it. Being refused is better than being ignored, so the ones we cannot resolve are disabled rather than offered.

                What does dayparting cost me?

                Every hour you remove is inventory you cannot bid on. Narrowing the daypart grid is the commonest way a campaign quietly under-delivers.

                If you do daypart, follow the shape rather than fighting it: 62% of your delivery already lands between 5pm and 11pm, and weekends alone are 36% of the week.

                How long should a campaign run?

                Four weeks minimum for anything measured by leads — attribution needs delivery before it can show you anything, and a fortnight of data is mostly noise. Awareness wants eight weeks or more. An event campaign is the exception: it is time-boxed and front-loads delivery into the run-up, because impressions after the date are worthless.

                Creative

                Getting a spot on air

                What format does my video need to be?

                15 or 30 seconds, 1920×1080, MP4 or MOV, with audio. Broadcast-grade encoding is handled for you. Approval takes about one working day.

                I do not have a video. What now?

                We can make one from photographs of the business, your logo and a short script. It is not a substitute for a real production, but it is a great deal better than not being on television, and the same approval path applies.

                Why was my creative rejected?

                Almost always one of four things: no audio track, a resolution below 1080p, a claim that needs substantiation, or a phone number or URL on screen that does not match the account. The rejection names which.

                Can I run more than one video in a campaign?

                Yes, and you should. Delivery is split between them and the per-video figures appear on the campaign, so the comparison is made for you rather than requiring two campaigns and a spreadsheet.

                Reporting and exports

                Getting the numbers out

                Why does the same figure appear on three screens?

                Because there is one set of numbers underneath all of them. Every figure in Attune is a sum over a single weekly ledger — the dashboard, Results, the campaign pages, the wallet statements and the exports are all slices of it.

                That is deliberate. A statement and a report that can disagree is a support ticket waiting to happen.

                Can I schedule a report?

                Yes — daily, weekly or monthly, to any address. Scheduled reports carry the same figures as the screen they came from and state the attribution window applied, so a report opened three weeks later still says what it was measuring.

                Why is spend approximate in the Where it ran breakdowns?

                Because impressions are counted per row and cost is not. Spend in a breakdown is worked back from impressions at your account's blended rate. With one rate that is exact; across several it is close but not to the dollar.

                The exact figures are the totals and the per-campaign rows. We would rather say this than let you reconcile a breakdown against an invoice and lose an afternoon.

                Why can I not see leads by hour, or by app?

                Because attribution resolves to a campaign and a household, not to a moment or a placement. We know a household saw your ad and later called; we do not know which of the eleven impressions it saw that week was the one that did it.

                Anyone who splits leads by hour is modelling, not measuring. Impressions and attention are broken out that way because they genuinely are measured that way.

                Account and access

                People, permissions and getting help

                Can I add someone to the account?

                Yes, with three levels: owner, can edit campaigns, and can view only. Invites expire after seven days. Only an owner can add funds or change the business details.

                Can I white-label reports for a client?

                Yes — Attune branding comes off client-facing reports from Settings. The figures and the methodology notes stay, because a report that cannot explain itself is worth less than no report.
