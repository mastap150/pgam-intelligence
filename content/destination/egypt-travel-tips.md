# First-Time Egypt: What to Know Before You Visit the Pyramids & Beyond

*By [Author] · [Date] · 9 min read*

Egypt's headline attraction changed in November 2025. **The Grand Egyptian Museum opened on
1 November 2025**, and for the first time since Howard Carter found them in 1922, **all 5,398
objects from Tutankhamun's tomb are displayed together.**

That reshapes the Cairo half of any Egypt itinerary. Here is what else you need to know.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Grand Egyptian Museum:** book online, timed entry, roughly **$30** for foreign adults.
  Six daily slots.
- **Visa:** e-visa **$25** single / **$60** multi-entry online, or **$30** visa on arrival for
  citizens of 45+ countries. 30 days.
- **Passport validity:** six months beyond departure.
- **Ten days** covers Cairo, a Nile cruise and Abu Simbel comfortably.
- **The hassle is real.** Persistent, commercial, and manageable — see below.

---

## The Grand Egyptian Museum

Located near the Giza plateau rather than in central Cairo, and it is the largest
archaeological museum in the world dedicated to a single civilisation.

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| Opened | **1 November 2025** |
| Tutankhamun | All **5,398** artefacts together for the first time since 1922 |
| Ticket, foreign adult | ~**$30** (roughly 1,450 EGP) |
| Reduced (students 13–25, children 6–12, with ID) | ~**$15** (roughly 730 EGP) |
| Booking | **Online only**, via the official ticketing site |
| Entry | Timed, six daily slots: 9:00, 11:00, 13:00, 15:00, 17:00, 19:00 |

**Give it a full day.** People routinely allocate a morning and regret it.

**The old Egyptian Museum in Tahrir Square still exists** and retains a substantial
collection, though the headline pieces have moved. Worth a visit if you have the days, not if
you don't.

## The Ten-Day Shape

**Cairo (3–4 days)** — the Grand Egyptian Museum (a full day), the Giza pyramids and Sphinx,
Saqqara and Dahshur (the Bent and Red pyramids, far quieter than Giza), Islamic Cairo and
Khan el-Khalili, Coptic Cairo.

**Aswan (2 days)** — Philae Temple, the Nubian villages, a felucca on the Nile at sunset.

**Abu Simbel (1 day)** — three hours south of Aswan, and the most spectacular single monument
in Egypt. Go early.

**Nile cruise, Aswan to Luxor (3–4 nights)** — Kom Ombo, Edfu, and the river itself. Sailing
downstream from Aswan to Luxor is the conventional direction and the better one.

**Luxor (2 days)** — Karnak and Luxor temples on the east bank; the Valley of the Kings,
Hatshepsut's temple and the Colossi of Memnon on the west. **Balloon flight at dawn** over the
west bank if the budget allows.

## Visas and Entry

| Route | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **E-visa** | **$25** single, **$60** multi | Official Egypt e-visa portal, ~3 business days |
| **Visa on arrival** | **$30** | Cairo airport and other entry points, for citizens of 45+ countries including the US, UK, Canada, Australia and most of Europe |

Both allow a **30-day stay**. **Passport must be valid at least six months** beyond your
planned departure.

Use the official portal for the e-visa — as with most countries, there is a large ecosystem
of lookalike sites charging a multiple of the real fee.

## The Hassle Question, Honestly

This is the thing first-time visitors ask about most and that most guides skirt.

**What it is:** persistent commercial attention at every major site — camel rides, guides,
souvenirs, "free" gifts that are not free, and offers to photograph you with your own camera
followed by a demand for payment. It is a livelihood strategy, not aggression, and it is
concentrated at Giza, Khan el-Khalili and the Luxor west bank.

**What genuinely helps:**

- **Hire a licensed Egyptologist guide** for the major sites. Beyond the substantial
  improvement in what you understand, their presence deflects almost all of it.
- **A firm, friendly "la shukran"** (no, thank you) and continuing to walk. Stopping is read
  as interest.
- **Never accept anything placed in your hand.** The "gift" is the opening of a transaction.
- **Agree every price before**, including camel rides — and specifically agree the price to
  get *down* as well as up, which is a well-worn routine.
- **Carry small notes.** Tipping (*baksheesh*) is embedded in the culture, for small services
  everywhere. Budget for it rather than resenting it.

**It is manageable and it should not put you off.** But being told it does not exist is worse
preparation than knowing it does.

## When to Go

| Period | Conditions |
|---|---|
| **Oct–Apr** | The season. Warm days, cool nights. **Peak Dec–Jan** |
| May, Sept | Hot but workable, fewer people |
| **Jun–Aug** | 40 °C+ in Luxor and Aswan. Cheapest, genuinely punishing |

**Whenever you go, start at dawn.** Sites open early, and the difference between 7am and 11am
at Karnak is heat, light and crowds all at once.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you get tickets for the Grand Egyptian Museum?**
Online only, through the official ticketing site, with timed entry in six daily slots from
9:00 to 19:00. Foreign adult tickets are roughly $30, with a reduced rate of about $15 for
students aged 13–25 and children 6–12 with valid ID.

**Do I need a visa for Egypt?**
Most visitors do. An e-visa costs $25 for single entry or $60 for multiple, applied for
through the official portal and processed in about three business days. A visa on arrival at
$30 is available to citizens of 45+ countries including the US, UK, Canada, Australia and
most of Europe. Both allow 30 days, and your passport must be valid six months beyond
departure.

**How many days do you need in Egypt?**
Ten covers Cairo, Aswan, Abu Simbel, a Nile cruise and Luxor at a reasonable pace. A week is
possible if you cut the cruise to three nights or skip Abu Simbel.

**Is Egypt difficult for first-time visitors?**
The persistent commercial attention at major sites is real and catches people off guard.
Hiring a licensed guide for the main sites deflects most of it and substantially improves what
you take away. Beyond that, Egypt is well set up for tourism and the infrastructure works.

---

## The Bottom Line

Give the Grand Egyptian Museum a full day, hire a licensed guide for the major sites, start
every day at dawn, and go between October and April.
