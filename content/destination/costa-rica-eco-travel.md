# Costa Rica for Eco-Travellers: Where to Go, What to Do, Best National Parks

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

Costa Rica protects roughly a quarter of its land and has built a national brand on it, which
means two things: the conservation is genuinely real, and "eco" is also a marketing word
applied to a great many things that are not.

Here is which parks are worth the journey, and how to tell the difference.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Two regions, not five.** Roads are slow; distances deceive.
- **Corcovado** is the most biodiverse and the hardest to reach. **Monteverde** is the cloud
  forest. **Manuel Antonio** is the easiest and the busiest.
- **Look for CST certification** (Certificación para la Sostenibilidad Turística), the
  government scheme — not just green branding.
- **Guides find wildlife you will walk past.** This is the single best money you spend.
- **Best months:** December–April dry season; May–November green season is cheaper and
  wetter.

---

## The Parks Worth the Journey

**Corcovado (Osa Peninsula)** — described by National Geographic as one of the most
biologically intense places on Earth, and the only place in the country with a realistic
chance of tapirs and all four monkey species. **Entry requires a certified guide** and
advance booking. Hard to reach — that is why it is what it is.

**Monteverde Cloud Forest** — high-altitude cloud forest, quetzals, and the original canopy
walkways. Cool and wet year-round. The reserve itself caps daily numbers.

**Manuel Antonio** — small, beautiful, beaches inside the park, and easily the busiest.
Wildlife is habituated and easy to see. Go at opening, and book ahead — it has a daily cap
and closes one day a week.

**Tortuguero** — reachable only by boat or small plane, canals through rainforest, and
green turtle nesting roughly July to October. A genuinely different Costa Rica.

**Arenal / La Fortuna** — the volcano, hanging bridges, hot springs. The most developed
tourist area in the country and still worth it.

**Rincón de la Vieja** — volcanic, drier, geothermal features, far fewer people.

**Santa Rosa / Guanacaste** — tropical dry forest, an ecosystem far rarer than rainforest and
almost entirely overlooked.

## Telling Real Sustainability From a Sticker

"Eco-lodge" is unregulated. These are the signals that mean something:

- **CST certification** — Costa Rica's government-run Certification for Sustainable Tourism,
  graded in levels. Genuinely audited.
- **Local employment and ownership.** Ask. Lodges that employ and are run by people from the
  area answer this immediately.
- **Wildlife policy.** No feeding, no touching, no captive animals used as attractions.
  **Sloth "sanctuaries" that let you hold sloths are not sanctuaries.**
- **Specifics over adjectives.** Real operations will tell you about their water treatment,
  energy and waste. Marketing talks about "harmony with nature".

**A note on animal encounters:** anything advertising close contact with wild animals —
holding sloths, swimming with captive dolphins, monkeys that come to you for food — is a
welfare problem regardless of how it is branded. Habituating wildlife to humans and food
causes real harm, and Costa Rica has active campaigns against feeding.

## The Two-Region Rule

Costa Rica looks small and drives slowly. Mountain roads, single-lane bridges and rain mean
a 150 km journey can take four hours.

**Pick two regions for a 10-day trip:**

| Pairing | Works because |
|---|---|
| **Arenal + Monteverde** | Close together; volcano and cloud forest contrast well |
| **Monteverde + Manuel Antonio** | Cloud forest and Pacific coast |
| **Osa/Corcovado + Pacific coast** | The serious wildlife trip |
| **Tortuguero + Arenal** | Caribbean and central; very different feels |

**Do not attempt** Caribbean coast plus Osa Peninsula in ten days. They are opposite corners.

---

## Practicalities

- **Rent a 4x4** if you are going anywhere unpaved, which is most interesting places. Standard
  cars struggle in green season.
- **Guides:** $50–$100 for a half day and the best value in the country. A naturalist guide
  with a scope will show you a dozen things you would have walked straight past.
- **Costa Rica is not cheap** by Latin American standards — closer to southern Europe.
- **Tap water is generally drinkable** in most of the country, which is unusual for the
  region.
- **Book Corcovado and Manuel Antonio ahead.** Both cap daily entry.

## When to Go

| Period | Conditions |
|---|---|
| **Dec–Apr** | Dry season. Peak crowds and prices, best beach weather |
| **May–Nov** | Green season — rain most afternoons, lush, cheaper, fewer people |
| Sept–Oct | Wettest on the Pacific; **driest on the Caribbean** |

**Green season is underrated.** Rain typically comes in the afternoon, mornings are clear,
wildlife is active, and prices drop substantially. Roads are the real constraint.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which national park is best in Costa Rica?**
Corcovado for biodiversity — it is the richest and requires a certified guide and advance
booking. Monteverde for cloud forest and quetzals. Manuel Antonio is the easiest and most
accessible but also the busiest, with a daily cap and one closure day a week.

**How do you tell if a Costa Rican eco-lodge is genuine?**
Look for CST certification, the government's audited sustainability scheme. Ask about local
employment and ownership, and check the wildlife policy — no feeding, no touching, no captive
animals as attractions. Places offering close contact with wild animals are a welfare problem
regardless of branding.

**How many places can you visit in Costa Rica in 10 days?**
Two regions. Roads are slow — a 150 km drive can take four hours — so attempting more means
spending the trip driving. Arenal with Monteverde, or Monteverde with the Pacific coast, are
the standard pairings.

**Is Costa Rica expensive?**
Yes by Latin American standards — closer to southern European pricing than to neighbouring
countries. Budget $110–$180 per person per day mid-range, with accommodation and guided
activities being the main costs.

---

## The Bottom Line

Pick two regions, hire a naturalist guide at every park, look for CST certification rather
than green branding, and consider green season for lower prices and active wildlife.
