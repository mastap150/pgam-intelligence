# Sri Lanka in 10 Days: The Itinerary That Covers Everything Worth Seeing

*By [Author] · [Date] · 9 min read*

Sri Lanka is small — roughly the size of Ireland — and packs cultural sites, hill country,
national parks and beaches into distances you can drive in a morning. Ten days genuinely
covers it.

The one thing that catches people out is the weather. **Sri Lanka has two monsoons hitting
opposite coasts at opposite times of year**, which means there is no bad month to visit the
country, only a bad month for a specific beach.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Route:** Negombo → Sigiriya → Kandy → Ella (by train) → Yala or Udawalawe → south coast
  → Colombo.
- **Two monsoons.** South-west coast is best **December–March**; east coast is best
  **May–September**.
- **The Kandy–Ella train** is the single best thing in the country. Book reserved seats
  ahead.
- **Hire a car with a driver.** Roughly $50–$80 a day and standard practice, not a luxury.
- **Visa:** an ETA is required for most nationalities — terms have changed repeatedly, so
  check the current official position.

---

## The Two Monsoons

This decides your beach, and therefore your itinerary.

| Monsoon | Affects | Season |
|---|---|---|
| **Yala (south-west)** | West and south-west coasts, hill country | May–September |
| **Maha (north-east)** | East and north coasts | November–February |

**Which means:**

- **December–March:** go to the **south and west** coasts — Mirissa, Unawatuna, Bentota.
  Peak season, best weather, highest prices.
- **May–September:** go to the **east** coast — Arugam Bay, Trincomalee, Nilaveli. Arugam Bay
  is a serious surf destination in this window.
- **April and October:** inter-monsoon. Unsettled everywhere, cheapest, and perfectly
  workable if you are flexible.

**The cultural triangle and hill country are visitable year-round.** Rain in the hills tends
to be afternoon showers rather than washouts.

## The 10-Day Route

### Day 1: Arrive — Negombo

Fly into Colombo (CMB) and stay in **Negombo**, 15 minutes from the airport, rather than
driving two hours into Colombo tired. A beach town with a working fishing fleet.

### Days 2–3: The Cultural Triangle — Sigiriya

**Sigiriya** — a 200 m rock fortress with fifth-century frescoes and a lion-paw staircase.
Climb it at opening; it is exposed and gets brutally hot.

**Pidurangala**, the neighbouring rock, is cheaper, less crowded and gives you the view *of*
Sigiriya, which is arguably the better photograph.

**Also here:** Dambulla cave temples, and **Polonnaruwa**, an eleventh-century ruined capital
best seen by bicycle in the early morning.

**Optional:** **Anuradhapura**, older and larger than Polonnaruwa. Add a day if ancient
cities are your priority.

### Day 4: Kandy

The last capital of the Sinhalese kings, around a lake, with the **Temple of the Sacred Tooth
Relic** — the most important Buddhist site in the country. Attend a *puja* ceremony rather
than visiting between them.

**Nearby:** the Peradeniya botanical gardens.

### Days 5–6: The Hill Country — Ella

**Take the train from Kandy to Ella.** Six to seven hours through tea plantations and cloud
forest, and one of the best rail journeys in the world. **Reserve seats in advance** —
unreserved carriages are standing-room in season.

**In Ella:** Little Adam's Peak (easy, an hour), Ella Rock (harder, three hours), and the
Nine Arch Bridge. **Nuwara Eliya** and the tea estates are worth a stop en route.

**For the ambitious:** **Adam's Peak** — a pre-dawn pilgrimage climb of around 5,500 steps
to see the sunrise shadow. Season runs roughly December to May.

### Day 7: Safari — Yala or Udawalawe

**Yala** has the highest leopard density in the world and the crowds that come with it.
**Udawalawe** is quieter, less about leopards, and reliably excellent for elephants.

**Go at dawn.** Afternoon drives are hotter and less productive.

### Days 8–9: The South Coast

Season-dependent. **December–March:** Mirissa for whale watching (blue whales, roughly
November–April), Unawatuna for swimming, **Galle** for the Dutch colonial fort — walk the
ramparts at sunset.

**May–September:** swap the south for **Arugam Bay** on the east coast.

### Day 10: Colombo, and out

Half a day is enough — Pettah market, the Gangaramaya temple, Galle Face Green at sunset.

## Getting Around

**A car with a driver is the standard way to travel Sri Lanka**, not an indulgence. Roughly
$50–$80 per day including fuel, and it covers the whole trip. Distances look short and take
far longer than the map suggests.

**Take the train anyway for Kandy–Ella.** Have the driver meet you at the other end. Every
driver in the country expects this request.

**Tuk-tuks** within towns; agree the fare first or use an app.

## What It Costs

Per person per day.

| Style | Daily cost | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | $30–$50 | Guesthouses, local food, public transport |
| Mid-range | $70–$120 | Good hotels, driver shared between two, safaris |
| Comfortable | $200+ | Boutique and colonial-era hotels, private guiding |

**Add:** national park fees (roughly $25–$40 per person plus jeep hire), and the driver if
not already counted.

Sri Lanka sits between India and the Maldives on price — better value than most of Southeast
Asia at the top end, slightly more expensive at the bottom.

## What Nobody Tells You

- **It is hotter than the numbers suggest** — humidity does the work. Sigiriya at 11am is
  genuinely punishing.
- **Dress code at Buddhist sites** is enforced: shoulders and knees covered, shoes off, and
  never pose with your back to a Buddha image. This last one has caused real legal trouble
  for visitors.
- **Ten days is tight but real.** Twelve is more comfortable.
- **Book the Kandy–Ella train early** — reserved seats sell out weeks ahead in season.
- **Tap water is not drinkable.** Filter or buy.
- **Dengue is present.** Mosquito repellent matters here more than in most of the region.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is 10 days enough for Sri Lanka?**
Yes. The country is roughly the size of Ireland and the main sights — the cultural triangle,
Kandy, the hill country, a national park and a coast — fit into ten days with a driver.
Twelve days makes it comfortable rather than brisk.

**What is the best time to visit Sri Lanka?**
It depends which coast. December to March suits the south and west, May to September suits
the east. The cultural triangle and hill country work year-round. April and October fall
between the two monsoons and are unsettled but cheap.

**Do you need a visa for Sri Lanka?**
Most nationalities need an ETA. The terms, cost and application system have changed several
times in recent years, so check the current official position before booking rather than
relying on any secondary source.

**Is the Kandy to Ella train worth it?**
Yes — it is the single best experience in the country, six to seven hours through tea country
and cloud forest. Reserve seats in advance; unreserved carriages are standing-room in season.
Have your driver meet you at the far end.

## The Bottom Line

Ten days genuinely covers Sri Lanka, hire a car with a driver for all of it except the
Kandy–Ella train, and let the monsoon calendar pick your coast.
