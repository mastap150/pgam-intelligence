# The Europe Packing List That Won't Ruin Your Checked Bag Allowance

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

Most Europe packing lists are the same list. This one starts somewhere else, because the
border changed: **the EU's Entry/Exit System has been fully operational at every Schengen
external border since 10 April 2026**, and it is adding real time at arrival.

That affects what you carry and how you plan your connections more than any packing cube
will. Then we get to the clothes.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Carry on only.** Seven days or seventy, the list barely changes.
- **EES is live** — biometric registration at Schengen entry. Allow more time.
- **ETIAS is not live yet.** Targeted for 2027; ignore anyone selling you one now.
- **Two pairs of shoes**, one worn. Cobblestones punish bad choices.
- **Pack for laundry, not for days.** Four days of clothes and a wash covers any trip length.

---

## The Border, First

### EES — live now

The **Entry/Exit System** replaced passport stamping for non-EU nationals. It registers your
**face and fingerprints** on entry to the Schengen area, and it began a phased rollout on
12 October 2025 before becoming fully operational on **10 April 2026**.

**What it means in practice:**

- **Your first entry takes longer** — biometric enrolment happens at the border.
- **Queues have lengthened** at major airports since rollout, and this is well documented.
- **Allow more connection time** than you would have pre-2026, particularly at the first
  Schengen airport on your itinerary.
- Subsequent entries are faster once you are enrolled.

**Practical packing consequence:** keep your passport accessible, don't check the bag with
your onward documents in it, and treat a 90-minute Schengen connection as tight rather than
comfortable.

### ETIAS — not yet

**ETIAS is delayed.** The EU has withdrawn its "last quarter of 2026" wording, a 2026 launch
is no longer considered feasible, and the current target is **2027** — with April 2027 the
earliest realistic date and a revised timeline expected from the EU in autumn 2026.

**Why it slipped:** ETIAS legally and technically requires EES to exist first, and EES only
completed its rollout in April 2026.

**What to do:** nothing. There is a substantial industry of sites selling ETIAS
"pre-registration" for a fee. **The scheme does not exist yet and there is nothing to buy.**
When it launches it will be a low-cost official application, not something you need an agent
for.

## The Core Principle

**Pack for four days and do laundry.** A two-week trip and a two-month trip need the same
bag. Almost everyone over-packs by planning an outfit per day.

**Carry-on only** removes checked bag fees, baggage delay risk, and the wait at the carousel.
On a multi-city European trip with budget-airline hops, it is worth more than any single
other decision.

## The List

### Clothing — the whole thing

| Item | Quantity | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| T-shirts / tops | 4 | Merino if you can — wears longer between washes |
| Shirt or blouse | 1–2 | For dinners and churches with dress codes |
| Trousers / jeans | 2 | One worn, one packed |
| Shorts or skirt | 1–2 | Summer only |
| Light jumper or fleece | 1 | Year-round. Evenings are cool even in July |
| Packable waterproof | 1 | Non-negotiable, any season |
| Underwear / socks | 5 each | Merino socks if walking a lot |
| Swimwear | 1 | If relevant |
| Something smarter | 1 outfit | Some restaurants and opera houses still care |

**Winter adds:** thermal base layer, proper coat, hat, gloves, scarf.

### Shoes — two pairs, one worn

1. **Comfortable walking shoes**, broken in. You will walk 15–20 km a day without noticing.
2. **One smarter or lighter pair** — sandals in summer, something you can wear to dinner
   otherwise.

**No more than two.** Shoes are the heaviest and bulkiest thing in any bag, and cobblestones
end trips for people in new footwear.

### Electronics

- **Type C plug adaptor** — covers most of continental Europe. **Type G** for the UK and
  Ireland, **Type J** for Switzerland, **Type L** for parts of Italy.
- **Power bank** — under 100 Wh, in your **cabin bag only**; airlines increasingly restrict
  where these can be carried and used.
- **A multi-port charger** rather than one per device.
- **Phone**, and an eSIM rather than roaming or a physical SIM.

### Documents

- Passport, valid **at least three months beyond your departure** from Schengen — many
  travellers assume six and some assume none; three is the Schengen rule.
- Travel insurance details, saved **offline**.
- Cards from two different networks. Some European terminals reject one and take the other.
- Photos of everything in cloud storage.

### Toiletries and medical

- Under 100 ml each in a clear bag if flying carry-on.
- **Buy the bulky items on arrival** — shampoo and sunscreen exist in Europe and cost less
  than the luggage space.
- Any prescription medication **in original packaging**, with a copy of the prescription.
- Blister plasters. The single most useful item on this list.

---

## What to Leave Behind

- **A different outfit per day.** Nobody is looking.
- **More than two pairs of shoes.**
- **A full-size towel** — accommodation provides them; a small microfibre one covers beaches.
- **Money belts worn outside clothing.** They mark you as a tourist and are slower than a
  zipped pocket.
- **Guidebooks.** Download the chapters.
- **Hairdryers, travel irons, kettles.** Accommodation has them or you can live without.
- **A second bag "just in case."** You will fill it.

## Season by Season

| Season | The addition that matters |
|---|---|
| **Spring (Mar–May)** | Waterproof and layers. Weather swings hard |
| **Summer (Jun–Aug)** | Sun protection, a refillable bottle, and something covering shoulders and knees for churches |
| **Autumn (Sept–Nov)** | Waterproof, warmer mid-layer. The best season and the wettest in the north |
| **Winter (Dec–Feb)** | Real coat, thermals, waterproof boots. Southern Europe is mild but wet |

**The church rule catches people out** in summer: many cathedrals in Italy, Spain and Greece
enforce covered shoulders and knees, and a scarf in the day bag solves it.

[MILES BOX: Carry-on-only travel pairs unusually well with award tickets — most airline
partner awards in economy include a cabin bag but charge for checked luggage on low-cost
partner segments. Packing to cabin size means you can take the cheapest award routing without
adding a bag fee that erodes the redemption's value.]

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need ETIAS to travel to Europe?**
Not yet. ETIAS has been delayed past its 2026 target and is now expected in 2027, with April
2027 the earliest realistic launch and a revised EU timeline due in autumn 2026. Sites
currently selling ETIAS registration are selling something that does not exist — wait for the
official scheme.

**What is EES and does it affect me?**
The Entry/Exit System registers non-EU travellers' faces and fingerprints on entering the
Schengen area, replacing passport stamping. It has been fully operational at every Schengen
external border since 10 April 2026 and has lengthened queues at major airports. Allow extra
time for your first entry and for connections at your first Schengen airport.

**Can you do a two-week Europe trip with carry-on only?**
Yes, and the list is the same as for a one-week trip. Pack four days of clothing and do
laundry once. The saving is not just the bag fee — it is the flexibility on budget-airline
hops and not waiting at carousels between cities.

**How long does my passport need to be valid for Europe?**
For the Schengen area, at least three months beyond your intended date of departure, and it
must have been issued within the previous ten years. Many travellers assume six months, which
is the rule for a number of other destinations but not for Schengen.

---

## The Bottom Line

Pack four days of clothes, two pairs of shoes and a waterproof into a cabin bag, allow extra
time at your first Schengen border for biometric checks, and do not buy an ETIAS from anyone.
