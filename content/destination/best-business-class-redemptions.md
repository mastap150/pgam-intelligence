# The Best Business Class Redemptions Using Points (Current Sweet Spots)

*By [Author] · [Date] · 9 min read*

A sweet spot is a route where an airline's award chart has not caught up with what the seat
actually costs. They are worth a great deal — often $3,000 to $7,000 of value for 50,000 to
90,000 points — and they disappear without warning.

**British Airways repriced much of its chart in December 2025**, raising many routes 10–25%
and hitting BA-operated long-haul hardest. **Air France and KLM move to a three-tier award
fare structure from 8 September 2026.** That is the pattern: charts change, and the article
you are reading is a snapshot.

Everything below was accurate at the time of writing. **Verify before you transfer points** —
transfers are almost always irreversible.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Hold transferable points**, not airline miles. It is the only real hedge against
  devaluation.
- **Never transfer speculatively.** Confirm award space first, then transfer.
- **The best value is in premium cabins.** Economy redemptions are usually worse than cash.
- **Availability, not price, is the constraint.** Book the moment space appears.

---

## Six Sweet Spots That Still Work

Rates are one-way in business class unless stated, and are current at time of writing.

### 1. ANA "The Room" via Virgin Atlantic Flying Club — from 52,500 points

Widely considered among the best business class products in the sky, bookable through Virgin
Atlantic from the western United States at around **52,500 points one-way**.

**Why it works:** Virgin Atlantic prices ANA on its own partner chart, well below what the
seat commands.

**Transfer partners:** Amex Membership Rewards, Chase Ultimate Rewards, Citi ThankYou,
Marriott Bonvoy — an unusually wide set, which makes the points easy to assemble.

**The catch:** ANA award space is genuinely hard to find and is released in limited windows.

### 2. Turkish Miles&Smiles — Star Alliance business, US to Europe, 45,000

Turkish prices partner Star Alliance business — ANA, Lufthansa, Swiss, United — at around
**45,000 one-way** transatlantic, against $3,500–$5,000 in cash.

**The catch:** Turkish's booking systems and customer service are the weak point. Budget
patience.

### 3. Aeroplan — Star Alliance transatlantic, 65,000–75,000

Air Canada's programme prices Star Alliance business transatlantic at roughly
**65,000–75,000**, against $3,500–$5,500 cash.

**Why it's good:** a distance-based chart, generous stopover rules, and a strong search
engine that actually shows partner space.

### 4. Alaska Mileage Plan — Cathay Pacific to Asia, 50,000

Cathay business to Asia at around **50,000 points**, against $4,500–$7,000 in cash. One of
the strongest value ratios available.

### 5. United MileagePlus — ANA to Japan, 88,000

Higher in points than the Virgin route but far easier to find and book, at roughly
**88,000** against $5,500–$9,000 cash. The pragmatic version of sweet spot #1.

### 6. Avianca LifeMiles — intra-Europe 25,000, US–Europe 63,000

Lufthansa and Swiss business within Europe at around **25,000 one-way**, and transatlantic
at about **63,000**. LifeMiles runs frequent transfer bonuses from Amex — sometimes 25–30% —
which cuts the effective cost meaningfully.

**The catch:** LifeMiles has a mixed reputation for booking reliability. Do not hold a large
balance.

## The Rule That Matters More Than Any Chart

**Hold transferable currencies, not airline miles.**

Amex Membership Rewards, Chase Ultimate Rewards, Citi ThankYou and Capital One miles can move
to many airline programmes. Airline miles sitting in one programme cannot move anywhere.

When British Airways repriced in December 2025, anyone holding Avios in Avios took the hit.
Anyone holding transferable points moved to a different programme instead — and notably,
partner redemptions through Iberia Plus and Qatar Privilege Club were largely unaffected by
that same devaluation, which is exactly the kind of escape route flexibility buys.

**The corollary: never transfer speculatively.** Transfers are one-way and almost never
reversible. Find and hold the award space first, transfer second. Points transferred into a
programme you then cannot use are stranded.

## How to Actually Find Award Space

1. **Search the partner programme's engine, not the operating airline's.** Aeroplan and
   United both display Star Alliance partner space well.
2. **Search one passenger at a time.** Two business seats on the same flight are far rarer
   than one; if a search for two returns nothing, search for one.
3. **Be flexible by a few days.** Award space is released in blocks and moves.
4. **Look at the extremes of the booking window** — space is often released about 355 days
   out, and again in the final two weeks as unsold seats are dumped.
5. **Use an award-search alert service** if you fly premium cabins regularly. Manual
   searching does not scale.

## What Points Are Actually Worth

A useful sanity check rather than a precise science.

| Redemption | Typical value per point |
|---|---|
| Economy award vs cheap cash fare | 0.8–1.2¢ — **usually not worth it** |
| Business class, standard chart | 3–5¢ |
| Business class, a sweet spot above | **5–10¢** |
| Cash-back or portal booking | ~1¢ — the baseline to beat |

**The discipline:** if a redemption returns less than about 1.5¢ per point, pay cash and keep
the points. Most people burn points badly on economy tickets they could have bought.

**The counter-argument worth acknowledging:** points have no value until used, and programmes
devalue. A redemption you actually take at 2¢ beats a theoretical 8¢ you never book.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best use of credit card points?**
Long-haul business or first class on a partner sweet spot. That is where the gap between
award price and cash price is widest — 5 to 10 cents per point, against roughly 1 cent for
cash-back or portal bookings. Economy awards are usually worse value than simply buying a
cheap fare.

**Should I transfer points before finding award space?**
No. Transfers to airline programmes are almost always irreversible, and points transferred
into a programme where you then cannot find space are stranded. Confirm the award seat is
available, then transfer, then book — usually within the same session.

**How do I protect points against devaluation?**
Hold transferable currencies — Amex Membership Rewards, Chase Ultimate Rewards, Citi
ThankYou, Capital One — rather than miles sitting in a single airline programme. When one
programme reprices, as British Airways did in December 2025, flexible points can move
elsewhere while airline miles cannot.

**How many points do you need for business class to Europe?**
Currently from around 45,000 one-way through Turkish Miles&Smiles for Star Alliance partner
business, or roughly 63,000 via Avianca LifeMiles and 65,000–75,000 via Aeroplan. Verify
before transferring — these figures change without notice.

---

## The Bottom Line

Keep your points transferable, find the seat before you move anything, and spend them on
long-haul business class where a point is worth five times what it is worth anywhere else.
