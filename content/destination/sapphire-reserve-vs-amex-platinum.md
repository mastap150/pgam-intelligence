# Chase Sapphire Reserve vs Amex Platinum: Which Card Is Worth It?

> **Not financial advice.** Card terms, fees and credits change frequently. Verify current
> terms with the issuer before applying. These cards only make sense if you clear the balance
> in full every month.

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

Both cards were restructured recently and both now carry fees that look startling: **the
Sapphire Reserve at $795 a year and the Amex Platinum at $895.**

Neither is worth it as a travel card in the ordinary sense. Both can be worth it as a bundle
of credits you would have spent anyway — and that "would have anyway" is the entire question.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Sapphire Reserve: $795/year.** Points transfer to **Hyatt at 1:1**, the best hotel
  transfer in the ecosystem.
- **Amex Platinum: $895/year.** The strongest airport lounge access available, and Fine Hotels
  + Resorts.
- **Both are credit bundles.** Do the arithmetic on the credits you will genuinely use, not
  the headline total.
- **Chase if you value flexibility and Hyatt.** Amex if you value lounges and hotel status.
- **Neither, if you carry a balance.** Interest destroys any value either card produces.

## The Fees and What Offsets Them

| | Sapphire Reserve | Amex Platinum |
|---|---|---|
| **Annual fee** | **$795** | **$895** |
| Hotel credit | Up to $500 via Chase's The Edit (up to $250 per prepaid booking), plus up to $250 on prepaid Chase Travel bookings with select brands | $600 in prepaid Amex Travel bookings at Fine Hotels + Resorts or The Hotel Collection |
| Dining credit | Up to $300 at Sapphire Reserve Exclusive Tables ($150 biannually) | — |
| Transport | — | $200 Uber Cash ($15/month plus $20 in December), plus up to $120 for Uber One |
| Entertainment | — | $300 digital entertainment credit |
| Global Entry / TSA PreCheck | Up to $120 every four years | Comparable credit |

**The critical caveat, and the reason most people overpay:** these credits are heavily
conditional. They are tied to specific booking platforms, specific hotel collections, specific
restaurant programmes, and in Amex's case they are largely **monthly** — an unused month is
gone forever.

**Do this arithmetic before applying:** go through each credit and ask "would I have spent
this money, on this platform, anyway?" Count only the ones where the honest answer is yes.
If that total is below the annual fee, the card is costing you money.

## Where Each One Actually Wins

### Chase Sapphire Reserve

- **Ultimate Rewards transfer to World of Hyatt at 1:1** — the strongest single transfer
  relationship in points, turning a ~1¢ flexible point into a ~1.6¢ Hyatt point at par. If
  you use Hyatt, this alone can justify the card.
- **Broadly useful transfer partners** across airlines and hotels.
- **Points redeemable through Chase Travel** at an uplift, which gives a floor value that does
  not require finding award space.
- **Strong travel protections** — trip delay and cancellation cover that is genuinely usable.

### Amex Platinum

- **Lounge access is not close.** Centurion Lounges plus Priority Pass plus Delta Sky Clubs on
  Delta flights is the best network attached to any card.
- **Fine Hotels + Resorts** — property credits, upgrades and late checkout that have real
  cash value on expensive stays.
- **Hotel elite status** granted with the card at both Hilton and Marriott.
- **Membership Rewards transfer partners**, including the airline programmes behind most of
  the sweet spots in our redemptions guide.

## Which One, Honestly

**Take the Sapphire Reserve if:** you want maximum points flexibility, you stay at Hyatt, you
value trip protections, and you would rather have a lower fee with credits that are less
fiddly.

**Take the Amex Platinum if:** you fly often enough that lounges genuinely change your travel,
you stay in expensive hotels where Fine Hotels + Resorts benefits pay out, and you will
actually use monthly credits.

**Take neither if:** you fly two or three times a year, you do not book premium hotels, you
would not otherwise use the credit platforms, or you ever carry a balance. **For most people,
a no-fee or mid-fee card earning the same transferable currency is the better answer** — you
get the points ecosystem without the fee.

**A note on holding both:** people do, and the credits are largely non-overlapping. That is
$1,690 a year in fees, which needs a great deal of genuine usage to justify. Do the
arithmetic twice.

## The Things That Matter More Than the Comparison

**Clear the balance every month.** Interest on a carried balance exceeds any rewards value
these cards produce, immediately and by a wide margin. If you carry a balance, neither card is
for you.

**Never spend to hit a bonus.** Meet spend requirements with spending you were going to do
anyway. Manufactured spending violates card terms and gets accounts closed.

**Check eligibility rules** before applying — both issuers restrict repeat sign-up bonuses,
and application rules differ between them.

**Credits are use-it-or-lose-it.** Set calendar reminders for monthly credits or accept that
you will not capture them, and adjust the arithmetic accordingly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the Chase Sapphire Reserve worth $795?**
Only if you genuinely use the credits — up to $500 in hotel credits through Chase's platform,
up to $250 more on select brands, and up to $300 in dining credits — and if you value Ultimate
Rewards, particularly the 1:1 transfer to World of Hyatt. Count only credits you would have
spent anyway; if that total is under the fee, it is costing you money.

**Is the Amex Platinum worth $895?**
It depends almost entirely on lounge use and hotel spending. The lounge network is the best
attached to any card, and Fine Hotels + Resorts benefits have real value on expensive stays.
The credits are largely monthly and expire unused, so it rewards people who will actually
engage with them.

**Which is better, Sapphire Reserve or Amex Platinum?**
Chase for points flexibility, trip protections and the Hyatt transfer; Amex for lounges, hotel
status and premium hotel benefits. For infrequent travellers, neither — a no-fee card earning
the same transferable currency captures most of the value without the fee.

**Should I get a premium travel card if I carry a balance?**
No. Interest on a carried balance exceeds the rewards value these cards generate by a
substantial margin. Clear the balance in full every month, or use neither.

---

## The Bottom Line

Both are credit bundles rather than travel cards. Add up only the credits you would genuinely
have used, compare that to $795 or $895, and if the sum does not clear the fee, take a no-fee
card on the same points ecosystem instead.
