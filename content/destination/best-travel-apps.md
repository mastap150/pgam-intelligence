# The Travel Apps Worth Having — Organised by What They Solve

*By [Author] · [Date] · 7 min read*

App round-ups date faster than anything else in travel writing. Apps get acquired, change
their pricing, break their free tier, or quietly stop working in the country you need them in.

So this is organised by **the problem**, with the category and the selection criteria first —
because when the specific app you installed stops working, what you need is to know what to
replace it with.

---

## The Quick Answer

The five categories that actually matter, in order:

1. **Offline maps** — the single most important travel app category.
2. **Connectivity (eSIM)** — replaced roaming and SIM cards entirely.
3. **Document and booking storage** — offline, and not only in email.
4. **Translation with offline packs** — download before you land.
5. **Local transport and ride-hailing** — country-specific, install on arrival.

---

## 1. Offline Maps — Install Before You Fly

**The problem:** you land, have no data, and cannot find your accommodation.

**What to look for:**

- **Genuine full offline download** — the whole region, not cached tiles that expire.
- **Offline search and routing**, not just a viewable map.
- **Walking and public transport directions offline**, which many apps drop.
- **Saved pins that sync** so you can plan on a laptop and use on a phone.

**Do this before you leave:** download the region, drop pins for your accommodation, the
airport and anywhere booked, and check the map opens in airplane mode. That last step catches
the apps that only *claimed* to download.

## 2. Connectivity — eSIMs Changed This

**The problem:** roaming charges, or hunting for a SIM shop at 11pm.

**eSIMs** let you buy a local or regional data plan and activate it before you land, keeping
your home number active on your physical SIM.

**What to check:**

- **Your phone supports eSIM** and is carrier-unlocked. Verify before you travel.
- **Which network** the plan actually uses in-country — coverage varies enormously and the
  cheapest plan often rides the worst network.
- **Data only vs data and calls.** Most travel eSIMs are data-only, which is usually fine.
- **Regional plans** are better value than country-by-country for multi-stop trips.

**Where a local physical SIM still wins:** long stays, and countries where local carriers
undercut travel eSIMs substantially — much of Asia and Latin America.

## 3. Documents and Bookings

**The problem:** you need a booking reference in a place with no signal.

**What to look for:** an app that **stores confirmations offline**, ideally parsing them from
email automatically, with everything in one timeline.

**The manual fallback that never fails:** a single PDF or note with every booking reference,
address in the local language, and emergency contacts, saved offline on your phone *and*
somewhere in the cloud. Do this regardless of which app you use.

## 4. Translation

**The problem:** menus, signs, pharmacies.

**What to look for:**

- **Offline language packs** — download before arrival.
- **Camera translation** for menus and signs, which is the feature that earns its place.
- **Conversation mode** for actual back-and-forth.

**Realistic expectations:** camera translation on printed text is excellent. Handwriting and
stylised signage remain poor.

## 5. Local Transport

**The problem:** the global ride-hailing app you use at home does not operate here.

**These are country-specific** and there is no universal answer — a different app dominates in
Southeast Asia, in China, in Latin America and in the Middle East. **Ask your accommodation on
arrival**, or check before you fly, and install it while you still have wifi.

**Also worth having:** the local transit authority's own app in major cities, which usually
beats the global mapping apps on live service information.

## The Two Categories Most People Miss

**Flight tracking with delay prediction.** Knowing your inbound aircraft is already late, an
hour before the airline announces it, is the difference between rebooking calmly and queueing.
Look for apps that track the specific incoming airframe.

**Currency conversion offline.** Trivial-sounding, genuinely useful when negotiating or
working out whether a taxi price is absurd. Must work with no signal.

---

## Housekeeping That Matters More Than Any App

- **Download everything before you fly.** Maps, translation packs, boarding passes, playlists.
- **Airplane-mode test.** Open every app you are relying on with data off, at home, before you
  go.
- **Power bank.** Every app on this list is useless at 0%.
- **Screenshot the critical things.** A screenshot works when an app will not load.
- **Two-factor authentication:** make sure you can receive codes abroad. Being locked out of
  your own email because the code goes to a number you cannot receive on is a genuinely common
  and miserable travel failure.

That last point is the single most under-appreciated item here. Sort it before you leave.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the most important travel app?**
An offline maps app with genuine full-region download, offline search and offline routing.
Everything else has a workaround; arriving somewhere with no data and no map does not.

**Are eSIMs better than local SIM cards?**
For most trips, yes — you buy and activate before landing and keep your home number active.
Local physical SIMs still win for long stays and in countries where local carriers undercut
travel eSIM pricing substantially, which includes much of Asia and Latin America.

**Do I need a paid travel app subscription?**
Rarely. The free tiers of the main categories cover most travel. The exception is offline
maps, where a one-off paid app with genuinely complete offline routing can be worth it if you
travel often.

**What should I do before I fly?**
Download offline maps and translation packs, save bookings offline, install the local
transport app, and test everything in airplane mode at home. Then check you can receive
two-factor authentication codes abroad.

---

## The Bottom Line

Get offline maps and an eSIM sorted before you fly, keep a plain offline document with every
booking reference, and test the lot in airplane mode before you leave the house.

accuracy problem. Second, app round-ups are the fastest-rotting content in travel: apps get
acquired, break their free tiers, or stop operating in specific countries, and a list of
fifteen names is wrong within a year.

**If commercial wants the named listicle** — and there is a real argument for it, since that

a visible "last verified" date.
