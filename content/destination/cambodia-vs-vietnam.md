# Cambodia or Vietnam First? How to Choose and How to Combine Both

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

Most people asking this question do not actually have to choose. The two countries share a
long border, the overland and river crossings are straightforward, and three weeks covers
both properly.

If you genuinely only have time for one, the answer depends on whether you want a country or
a monument.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Vietnam** is the bigger, more varied trip — 2–3 weeks on its own.
- **Cambodia** is smaller and more concentrated — 7–10 days covers it well.
- **Angkor is the single greatest sight in Southeast Asia.** If you go to the region and skip
  it, you will regret it.
- **Combine them:** Vietnam north to south, then overland or by river into Cambodia.
- **Three weeks** is the natural length for both.

---

## Head to Head

| | Vietnam | Cambodia |
|---|---|---|
| Time needed | 2–3 weeks | 7–10 days |
| Variety | High — mountains, coast, deltas, cities | Lower, more concentrated |
| Headline sight | No single one; the country is the point | **Angkor** |
| Food | Outstanding, regionally distinct | Good, less internationally known |
| Cost | Slightly higher | Slightly lower |
| Infrastructure | Better — rail, domestic flights, roads | Improving; buses and short flights |
| Crowds | Concentrated in Hoi An, Ha Long | Concentrated in Siem Reap |
| Best for | A full country trip | A focused week, or a leg of a longer trip |

---

## Choose Vietnam If

You want a **country rather than a sight**. Vietnam gives you a 1,650 km spine with genuinely
different regions at each end — Hanoi's old quarter, the karst bays, hill country at Sapa,
imperial Hue, Hoi An, the Mekong Delta and Ho Chi Minh City.

The food alone justifies the trip, and it changes materially every few hundred kilometres.
Infrastructure is better: cheap domestic flights, a functioning rail spine, and the
Hue–Da Nang coastal train, which is one of the best short journeys in Asia.

## Choose Cambodia If

You want **Angkor**, and you have a week.

Angkor is not one temple. It is a vast temple city spread across many square kilometres, and
three days there is the minimum to see it properly — sunrise at Angkor Wat, the faces at
Bayon, the strangler figs at Ta Prohm, and the further temples that most day-trippers never
reach.

Cambodia also asks something of visitors that Vietnam does not, quite: **the Khmer Rouge
period is recent, and the sites in Phnom Penh — Tuol Sleng and Choeung Ek — are among the
most affecting places you can visit anywhere.** They are not optional context; they are why
the country is the way it is. Give Phnom Penh two days and go to both.

---

## The Combined Trip — 3 Weeks

The natural shape, north to south and then west.

1. **Hanoi** (3 nights) — old quarter, street food.
2. **Lan Ha Bay** (2) — the quieter alternative to Ha Long.
3. **Hue and Hoi An** (5) — take the coastal train between them.
4. **Ho Chi Minh City** (3) — War Remnants Museum, Cu Chi.
5. **Mekong Delta** (1–2) — Can Tho and the dawn floating markets.
6. **Cross into Cambodia** — bus to Phnom Penh, or the river route.
7. **Phnom Penh** (2) — Tuol Sleng, Choeung Ek, the riverfront.
8. **Siem Reap** (3–4) — Angkor.

**Fly home from Siem Reap or Phnom Penh.** Book an open-jaw ticket at the start; backtracking
costs days.

**The reverse order works too** and is better if you want to end on Vietnam's food and coast
rather than on Angkor.

## Crossing the Border

**By land:** the Bavet–Moc Bai crossing between Ho Chi Minh City and Phnom Penh is the busiest
and most straightforward, served by frequent through-buses taking roughly six to eight hours
including formalities.

**By river:** boats run the Mekong between Chau Doc in Vietnam and Phnom Penh — slower, more
expensive, and considerably more interesting.

**Visas:** Cambodia offers an e-visa and a visa on arrival for most nationalities; Vietnam
uses its e-visa system. **Sort the Cambodian visa before you reach the border** if you are
crossing overland — the process at land crossings is where travellers most often report
being asked for unofficial extra fees.

**Practical:** carry small US dollars. Both countries use them informally, and Cambodia
particularly so.

**Add for Cambodia:** the Angkor pass, sold as one-day, three-day and seven-day tickets. The
three-day is the right choice for most visitors and is valid across a longer window, so you
do not have to use the days consecutively — confirm current pricing and validity when booking.

## When to Go

| Period | Both countries |
|---|---|
| **Nov–Feb** | Dry and cooler. **Peak season** |
| Mar–May | Very hot, especially April in Cambodia |
| Jun–Oct | Wet season — green, cheap, afternoon downpours. Angkor's moats are full, which is when it photographs best |

**Note the regional split in Vietnam** — the centre floods September to November, which is
excellent weather in Hanoi. See the Vietnam guide for the full breakdown.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I visit Cambodia or Vietnam first?**
If you are doing both, Vietnam first works well — travel north to south and cross into
Cambodia from Ho Chi Minh City, ending at Angkor. Reversing it is equally valid and ends the
trip on Vietnam's food and coast instead.

**How many days do you need in Cambodia?**
Seven to ten. Three to four days at Angkor, two in Phnom Penh for Tuol Sleng and Choeung Ek,
and time for the coast or Battambang if you have it.

**Can you travel overland between Vietnam and Cambodia?**
Yes. The Bavet–Moc Bai crossing between Ho Chi Minh City and Phnom Penh is the main route,
with frequent through-buses taking six to eight hours. Mekong river boats between Chau Doc
and Phnom Penh are slower and more interesting. Arrange the Cambodian visa in advance if
crossing by land.

**Is Angkor Wat worth it?**
Yes, and it is worth more time than most people give it. Angkor is a temple city covering many
square kilometres, not a single monument — three days is the minimum to see it properly, and
the temples beyond the main circuit are the ones most visitors never reach.

---

## The Bottom Line

Treat them as one three-week trip rather than a choice: Vietnam for the country, Cambodia for
Angkor and for the recent history that explains it, and cross overland at Moc Bai.
