# The Complete Bali Travel Guide: Regions, Costs, What to Skip

*By [Author] · [Date] · 10 min read*

Bali is small — you can drive across it in three hours — and the single most common mistake
is treating it as one destination. Canggu and Amed have almost nothing in common. Choosing
the wrong base is what produces the "Bali was overrated" review.

This guide covers the mandatory tourist levy, which region actually matches what you want,
what things cost, and what is genuinely worth skipping.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Pay the tourist levy before you land.** IDR 150,000 (about $10) per person, once per
  trip, via the official Love Bali platform.
- **Pick one or two regions, not five.** Traffic is the constraint, not distance.
- **Canggu** for cafés and surf, **Ubud** for interior and culture, **Uluwatu** for cliffs
  and beaches, **the east and north** for quiet.
- **Best months:** April–June and September–October.
- **Budget $40–$70/day** comfortably; far less is possible.

---

## Before You Go: the Tourist Levy

Foreign visitors must pay a **IDR 150,000 per-person levy** to enter Bali, paid once per
trip through the official **Love Bali** platform. Indonesian citizens are exempt.

**Pay it online before you travel** and keep the QR voucher. Paying on arrival is possible
but means a queue after a long flight, and the airport process has been inconsistent. The
platform has had app issues at various points, so allow a little time rather than doing it
in the taxi.

This is separate from your visa. Most nationalities use a visa on arrival or e-VOA for
tourism — check your own country's current terms, as Indonesian visa rules change with some
frequency.

## The Regions — Pick One or Two

### Canggu — cafés, surf, remote workers

The centre of gravity for the digital-nomad and young-traveller scene. Excellent coffee,
good beach breaks, a dense restaurant scene, and traffic that has become genuinely difficult.

**Go if:** you want social energy, surfing, gyms and co-working.
**Don't if:** you came for quiet or for Balinese culture. Canggu is an international scene
that happens to be in Bali.

### Ubud — interior, culture, rice terraces

Inland, cooler, greener. Temples, the monkey forest, rice terraces, yoga, and the best
craft traditions on the island. Also very busy, and the centre is thick with traffic.

**Go if:** you want culture, walking, and a base for the interior.
**Stay slightly out** — Penestanan, Sayan or north toward Tegallalang — rather than in the
centre.

### Uluwatu and the Bukit — cliffs and the best beaches

The southern peninsula. Dramatic limestone cliffs, the best beaches on the island, serious
surf, and the sunset kecak dance at Uluwatu temple.

**Go if:** beaches are the point. **Don't if:** you want to move around — it is a long
drive from everywhere else.

### Sanur and the east — calm, families, older travellers

Sanur is flat, calm-watered and unfashionable, which is exactly why it works for families.
Further east, **Amed** and **Candidasa** are quiet, with the best diving and snorkelling on
the island.

### The north — Munduk, Lovina

Mountains, waterfalls, coffee plantations, and almost no crowds. Cooler and wetter. Two
nights here is the best antidote to southern Bali traffic.

### Where not to base yourself

**Kuta and Legian.** Included for honesty: this is the old package-tourism strip, and it is
not what most people picture when they imagine Bali. Fine for a first night near the
airport; not a holiday.

## What to Skip

- **The Bali swing and most "Instagram" attractions** — paid photo spots with queues.
- **Tanah Lot at sunset** — spectacular temple, overwhelming crowds. Go at dawn instead.
- **The Gates of Heaven** at Lempuyang — the "reflection" is a mirror held under a phone,
  and the queue can run for hours.
- **Kuta**, as above.
- **Trying to see the whole island from one base.** Google Maps times are optimistic in
  Bali by a wide margin.

---

## The Honest Bit: Consider Leaving Bali

The best decision many Bali trips make is not spending the whole time in Bali.

- **Nusa Penida** — 45 minutes by fast boat. Dramatic coastline, Kelingking beach, manta
  rays. Now busy, still worth it. Roads are rough.
- **The Gili Islands** — off Lombok, no motorised traffic, excellent snorkelling. Gili Air
  for balance, Meno for quiet, Trawangan for nightlife.
- **Lombok** — bigger, quieter, and Rinjani for serious trekking.
- **Java** — Yogyakarta, Borobudur and Prambanan, and the volcanoes at Bromo and Ijen. A
  short flight and a completely different trip.

**A good two-week shape:** Ubud (4) → Uluwatu or Canggu (4) → Nusa Penida or the Gilis (4)
→ buffer (2).

## What It Costs

Per person per day.

| Style | Daily cost | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Backpacker | $25–$40 | Hostels, warungs, scooter hire |
| Mid-range | $40–$70 | Good guesthouse or private villa share, mixed dining, driver days |
| Comfortable | $100–$200 | Villa with pool, restaurants, spa |
| Luxury | $300+ | Bali does high-end genuinely well and comparatively cheaply |

**Add:** the IDR 150,000 levy, and roughly $30–$50 for a full-day private driver — which is
the single best value purchase on the island and much safer than a scooter if you are not
already an experienced rider.

**A note on scooters:** they are cheap, ubiquitous, and the leading cause of tourist injury
in Bali. Most travel insurance will not cover you without the correct licence. Check your
policy wording before you rent one, not after.

## When to Go

| Period | Conditions | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Apr–Jun** | Dry, warm, quieter than peak | **Best overall** |
| Jul–Aug | Dry season peak; busiest and most expensive | Good weather, heavy crowds |
| **Sept–Oct** | Dry, warm, thinning crowds | **Best value** |
| Nov–Mar | Wet season — humid, afternoon downpours, fewer visitors | Cheapest; still very usable |

**Wet season is more usable than people assume.** Rain tends to come as heavy afternoon
bursts rather than all-day drizzle, and prices drop substantially. January and February are
the wettest.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do you have to pay a tourist tax to enter Bali?**
Yes. Foreign visitors pay a levy of IDR 150,000 per person, charged once per trip, through
the official Love Bali platform. Indonesian citizens are exempt. Pay online before you
travel and keep the QR voucher — it is separate from your visa.

**Where should I stay in Bali for the first time?**
Split your time: Ubud for the interior, temples and rice terraces, then either Uluwatu for
beaches and cliffs or Canggu for cafés, surf and social scene. Avoid trying to see the whole
island from one base — traffic makes distances far longer than they look.

**How many days do you need in Bali?**
Ten to fourteen. That allows two bases on Bali itself plus a few days on Nusa Penida or the
Gili Islands, without the trip becoming a series of long drives.

**Is Bali expensive?**
No, by international standards. Mid-range travel runs about $40–$70 per person per day
including accommodation, food and transport, and backpacking is comfortably under that.
High-end villas and restaurants cost a fraction of comparable quality in Europe or Australia.

---

## The Bottom Line

Pay the levy before you fly, pick two regions rather than five, and spend part of the trip
off Bali entirely — Nusa Penida or the Gilis are what most people remember afterwards.
