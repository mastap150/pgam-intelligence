# 11 Quieter Temples in Kyoto That Escape the Tourist Crowds

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

Kyoto has over 1,600 Buddhist temples and roughly a dozen of them absorb almost all the
visitors. The arithmetic is in your favour: walk twenty minutes from Kiyomizu-dera or take a
bus north, and you can have a fourteenth-century garden largely to yourself.

Two rules first, because they matter more than any list.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Arrive at opening.** Most temples open at 8:00 or 9:00; the buses arrive at 10:00.
- **Go north and west.** The crowds concentrate in eastern Higashiyama and Arashiyama.
- **Autumn and cherry blossom seasons** overwhelm even the quiet places. Winter is empty.
- **Many temples close by 16:00 or 17:00.** Plan the day around closing, not opening.

---

## The Two Rules

**Timing beats location.** Kiyomizu-dera at 6:30am — the approach streets are open and empty —
is a better experience than an obscure temple at noon. Fushimi Inari at 7am is transformed.
If you only take one thing from this article, it is that Kyoto rewards early starts more than
almost any city.

**Direction matters.** Eastern Higashiyama and Arashiyama are where the coaches go. **Northern
Kyoto, Ohara and the western hills** see a fraction of the traffic for comparable quality.

---

## The Eleven

### Northern Kyoto

**1. Shisen-dō** — a poet's retreat with a small, precise garden and a *shishi-odoshi* (the
bamboo deer-scarer) providing the only sound. Sit on the veranda.

**2. Manshu-in** — a Tendai temple with a dry landscape garden, near Shisen-dō and even
quieter. Excellent in autumn.

**3. Enkō-ji** — small, with a bamboo grove and a maple garden that rivals anything in the
city in November, at a fraction of the crowd.

**4. Kōtō-in** — a sub-temple within the large Daitoku-ji complex, reached along a maple
avenue. Daitoku-ji as a whole is an under-visited walled world of sub-temples; most visitors
never go in.

### Ohara — an hour north

**5. Sanzen-in** — moss gardens, small stone jizō figures among the moss, and a genuinely
rural setting. The single best half-day trip from central Kyoto.

**6. Hōsen-in** — nearby, with a framed garden view designed to be seen from a seated
position inside, and matcha included in the entry.

### Western Kyoto

**7. Gio-ji** — a tiny thatched hut in a moss garden in the Arashiyama hills, ten minutes'
walk beyond the bamboo grove and almost always empty.

**8. Adashino Nenbutsu-ji** — thousands of small stone Buddhas gathered from unmarked graves.
Unsettling and quietly remarkable.

**9. Daikaku-ji** — a former imperial villa with a large pond, north of the Arashiyama
crowds.

### Central and southern

**10. Tōfuku-ji** — famous in autumn and empty the rest of the year. The modernist dry gardens
by Shigemori Mirei are outstanding and worth a visit in any season.

**11. Sennyū-ji** — an imperial mortuary temple in the southeastern hills, close to Tōfuku-ji
and almost entirely overlooked.

## Practical Notes

- **Entry fees** run roughly ¥300–¥600 per temple. Some sub-temples charge separately.
- **Many close at 16:00 or 17:00**, earlier in winter. The last entry is usually 30 minutes
  before.
- **Cash.** Some smaller temples still do not take cards.
- **Shoes off** where indicated, and socks are appreciated.
- **Special openings:** many temples open interiors or gardens for limited seasons only,
  particularly in spring and autumn. Check what is open when you go.
- **Buses over trains** for northern Kyoto — the subway does not reach most of this list. A
  day pass is worth it.
- **Photography** is often prohibited inside halls. The signage is clear; the rule is taken
  seriously.

## A Quiet Day Itinerary

**Morning:** Fushimi Inari at 7:00, before anyone. Down by 9:00.
**Late morning:** Tōfuku-ji and Sennyū-ji, ten minutes apart.
**Afternoon:** bus north to Daitoku-ji and Kōtō-in.
**Alternative full day:** Ohara — Sanzen-in and Hōsen-in, with lunch in the village.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you avoid crowds at Kyoto temples?**
Arrive at opening — most temples open at 8:00 or 9:00 and the coaches arrive around 10:00 —
and head north or west rather than to eastern Higashiyama and Arashiyama. Even the famous
sites are transformed before 8am.

**Which temples in Kyoto are least crowded?**
Sanzen-in and Hōsen-in in Ohara, Shisen-dō and Manshu-in in the northern hills, Gio-ji beyond
the Arashiyama bamboo grove, and the sub-temples of the Daitoku-ji complex, which most
visitors walk past entirely.

**How much do Kyoto temples cost to enter?**
Roughly ¥300–¥600 each, with some sub-temples charging separately. A day visiting five
temples including a bus pass runs about ¥3,000–¥4,000.

**When is the quietest time to visit Kyoto?**
Winter, outside the New Year period — cold, clear and genuinely empty. Avoid the cherry
blossom window in late March and early April and the autumn colour season in November, both
of which fill even the quiet temples.

---

## The Bottom Line

Start at 7am, go north instead of east, and give Ohara a full half-day — it is the closest
Kyoto comes to how the city is imagined.
