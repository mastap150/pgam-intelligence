# The Amalfi Coast Road Trip: Route, Stops, Parking Reality & What to Skip

*By [Author] · [Date] · 9 min read*

Before you plan an Amalfi Coast road trip, one fact should shape the entire decision:
**rental cars are banned from the coast road on alternating days through the summer.** The
*targhe alterne* system has run since 2022, it applies specifically to hire cars, and people
still arrive not knowing about it.

This guide covers the rule, the route, where you can actually park, and the honest case for
not driving at all.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Targhe alterne:** on odd-numbered dates, odd-numbered plates are barred; on even dates,
  even plates are barred. Roughly June–September, **10:00–18:00**, along the full SS163 from
  Vietri sul Mare to Positano.
- **It applies to rental cars.** Residents, taxis, buses, scooters and motorbikes are exempt.
- **Parking is the real constraint**, not the driving.
- **For most people the ferry is the better answer** between May and September.
- **Best months to drive:** April, May, October.

---

## The Driving Restriction, Explained

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| What | Alternating number-plate ban (*targhe alterne*) |
| Where | The full SS163 Amalfi Drive, Vietri sul Mare → Positano |
| When | Summer season, roughly June–September |
| Hours | **10:00–18:00** |
| Rule | Odd plate / odd date = barred. Even plate / even date = barred |
| Applies to | **Rental cars** |
| Exempt | Residents, taxis, buses, scooters, motorcycles |

The rule exists because the SS163 is a two-lane cliff road built for far less traffic than it
now carries, and a single badly-judged passing manoeuvre can stop the entire coast for an
hour.

**How this ruins itineraries:** you collect a hire car in Naples on the 14th, drive down
fine, and discover on the 15th that your plate is barred until six in the evening. Check
your plate number at pickup and plan around it, or pick a rental period where it doesn't
bite.

The exact calendar is confirmed each year before summer, so verify the current dates rather
than assuming last year's.

---

## Should You Drive At All?

**Drive if:** you are travelling in April, May or October; you are staying inland or above
the coast; you want the Sorrento peninsula, Paestum and the mountains as well as the coast
towns; or you have mobility needs that make ferries and buses impractical.

**Don't drive if:** you are visiting in July or August; you are staying in Positano or
Amalfi town itself; or your itinerary is three coastal towns and a beach.

**The honest recommendation for most summer visitors:** base yourself in one town, use the
ferries, and hire a driver for the one day you want to go inland. The coast road is
genuinely spectacular and genuinely miserable to drive in August, and you cannot look at the
view while driving it anyway.

---

## The Route

The SS163 runs about 50 km from Vietri sul Mare in the east to Positano in the west, then
on to Sorrento. It takes 90 minutes with no traffic and three to four hours in summer.

**Drive it west to east** — Sorrento toward Salerno. You end up on the inside lane, away
from the drop, which makes the whole experience considerably less demanding.

### The stops worth making

**Positano** — the vertical town in every photograph. Beautiful, extremely crowded, and
built on a staircase. Parking is expensive and limited; expect €5–€10 an hour in the garages.

**Praiano** — between Positano and Amalfi, much quieter, and the best sunset on the coast.
An underrated place to stay.

**Furore** — the fjord, a narrow inlet under a bridge. Easy to drive straight past; there is
no real parking.

**Amalfi** — the working town of the coast, with a striking cathedral and the largest
harbour. Busier than it looks in photos.

**Atrani** — a five-minute walk from Amalfi and almost untouched by comparison. One of the
best short detours in Italy.

**Ravello** — inland and 350 m up, with the two garden villas (Rufolo and Cimbrone) and the
best views on the entire coast. **The single stop to prioritise if you only make one.**

**Vietri sul Mare** — the ceramics town at the eastern end. Good place to end.

### What to skip

- **The Emerald Grotto** — a lesser Capri Blue Grotto with a queue.
- **Driving to Positano's centre** — you cannot. Park above and walk down; remember you
  will walk back up.
- **Capri as a day trip by car** — impossible. Cars are heavily restricted; go by ferry.

## Parking: the Real Problem

Parking, not driving, is what actually breaks Amalfi Coast itineraries.

- **Expect €5–€10 per hour** in the main towns, and more in Positano in August.
- **Garages fill by mid-morning** in high season.
- **Book accommodation with parking included** — this is worth paying a premium for and
  routinely saves more than it costs.
- **Street parking is colour-coded:** blue lines are paid, white free (rare), yellow
  residents only. Yellow lines are enforced.
- **Ravello has a large garage** at the entrance to town, which is the easiest parking on
  the coast.

---

## The Alternative: Ferries and Buses

**Ferries** run April to October between Sorrento, Positano, Amalfi, Minori, Maiori,
Salerno and Capri. They are faster than the road in summer, considerably more pleasant, and
the coast looks better from the water. This is the right answer for most summer trips.

**SITA buses** run the full coast road year-round, cheaply. They are also crowded, and they
stop everywhere. Fine for one or two hops, wearing as a primary mode.

**Private drivers** cost roughly €300–€500 a day for the coast. Split between four, for one
inland day, that is defensible.

## Where to Stay

| Base | Best for | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Positano** | The iconic stay | Most expensive; endless stairs |
| **Praiano** | Value and quiet, central position | Fewer restaurants |
| **Amalfi** | Ferry connections, walkability | Busy, less dramatic setting |
| **Ravello** | Views, calm, gardens | Up a mountain — a car or bus each way |
| **Sorrento** | Transport links, price | Not actually on the Amalfi Coast |
| **Salerno** | Cheapest, real city, train station | 40 minutes from the good bit |

**The under-used option is Salerno** — a proper working city with a mainline station, ferry
terminal and normal prices, 40 minutes from Amalfi.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can you drive the Amalfi Coast in a rental car?**
Yes, but not on every day in summer. The *targhe alterne* system bars rental cars from the
SS163 on alternating days by plate number — odd plates on odd dates, even on even —
between roughly 10:00 and 18:00 during the summer season, along the full road from Vietri
sul Mare to Positano. Residents, taxis, buses, scooters and motorcycles are exempt.

**Is driving the Amalfi Coast difficult?**
It is narrow, winding and busy rather than technically hard. Driving west to east keeps you
on the inside lane away from the drop, which helps considerably. Buses take up more than
their lane on the corners, and the real difficulty in summer is traffic volume and parking
rather than the road itself.

**What's the best base on the Amalfi Coast?**
Praiano for balance of price, quiet and position; Amalfi for ferry connections and
walkability; Ravello for views and calm. Salerno is the value option — a real city with a
train station, 40 minutes away.

**How many days do you need on the Amalfi Coast?**
Four to five. That allows two or three coastal towns at a reasonable pace, a day in Ravello,
and a day trip to Capri or Pompeii without the trip becoming a series of transfers.

---

## The Bottom Line

Check your rental plate against the *targhe alterne* calendar before you build the
itinerary, drive it in May or October if you are going to drive it at all, and take the
ferries in August.
