# How to Travel for 6+ Months: Visas, Money, Health & What Nobody Warns You About

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

Long trips rarely end because of the dramatic things. They end because of a visa
miscalculation, an insurance policy that turned out to have a duration cap, a bank account
frozen for suspicious activity, or straightforward exhaustion around month four.

The admin is the hard part. Here it is.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Visas are the binding constraint.** Count days precisely; Schengen's 90-in-180 rule
  catches people constantly.
- **Tell your bank before you go**, hold accounts at two institutions, and carry a backup
  card separately.
- **Long-stay insurance is a different product** from holiday cover — most annual policies cap
  individual trips at 30–45 days.
- **Tax residency does not pause** because you left. Check your own country's rules.
- **Slow down.** Month four is where fast travellers burn out.

---

## Visas — Count the Days Precisely

**Schengen: 90 days in any rolling 180.** This is the single most misunderstood rule in
travel. It is not 90 days per entry and not 90 days per calendar period — it is a rolling
window, and you must be able to look back 180 days from any given date and count 90 or fewer.
Use a calculator; do not estimate.

**Note the new border reality:** the EU's Entry/Exit System is now fully operational at every
Schengen external border and records entries and exits biometrically. Informal overstaying
that previously depended on inconsistent stamping is no longer viable.

**Elsewhere:**

- **Visa runs** — leaving and re-entering to reset a clock — are tolerated in some countries
  and explicitly cracked down on in others. Rules change; check current practice, not what a
  forum said two years ago.
- **Digital nomad visas** now exist in a substantial number of countries, offering legitimate
  longer stays with income requirements. Terms vary enormously and change frequently.
- **Onward-travel proof** is requested at check-in more often than at borders. A refundable
  ticket solves it.
- **Passport validity and blank pages** — six months' validity is the common requirement, and
  some countries want two consecutive blank pages.

## Money and Banking

- **Two banks, two cards, kept separately.** A single frozen card ends a trip.
- **Tell every institution you are travelling** and keep the notification updated as you move.
- **Choose cards with no foreign transaction fees** — over six months this is a serious
  amount.
- **Keep a home address** for banking and cards. Many institutions will close accounts without
  one.
- **Watch for account closures triggered by inactivity or by "residence"** — some banks close
  accounts if they believe you have moved abroad.
- **Keep a cash buffer** you do not touch.

## Insurance — a Different Product

**Holiday policies will not cover you.** Annual multi-trip policies typically cap any single
trip at **30 to 45 days** — a limit people discover when claiming, not when buying.

**What you need:**

- **Long-stay or nomad cover** designed for continuous travel, usually purchased monthly.
- **Adequate medical evacuation** — $250,000+.
- **Check the activity list** for anything you might do over six months, which is more than
  you would list for a two-week holiday.
- **Understand what happens if you return home mid-trip** — some policies void on re-entry to
  your home country.

**Also:** think about health cover *at home* if your national scheme has residency conditions.
Some suspend entitlement after a period abroad.

## Tax and Legal Admin

**This is the section people skip and should not.**

- **Leaving the country does not end tax residency.** Rules vary by nationality; some
  countries tax on citizenship regardless of where you live, others on day-count tests. **Get
  advice specific to your own situation** — this article cannot give it.
- **You may become tax resident somewhere else** by staying too long. Day-count thresholds
  are commonly around 183 days.
- **Keep a home address** for tax, banking, licences and voting.
- **Mail:** a forwarding service or a trusted person. Official correspondence does not stop.
- **Driving licences, passports and prescriptions expire** mid-trip. Check every expiry date
  against your intended return.
- **Power of attorney** — if something at home needs signing, someone must be able to.

## The Human Part

**Month four is when it gets hard.** This is consistent enough across long-term travellers to
be treated as a planning assumption rather than a personal failing.

**What actually helps:**

- **Slow down.** The single most common mistake is moving too fast for too long. A week per
  place minimum, and month-long stays where you can.
- **Have a base occasionally.** Rent somewhere for a month. Cook. Do laundry. Join a gym.
  Routine is restorative in a way that another new city is not.
- **Keep a routine that travels** — running, reading, writing, a language you are learning.
- **Stay in touch deliberately.** Scheduled calls, not vague intentions. Drift is real.
- **Accept the flat days.** Not every day in a remarkable place is remarkable. Sitting in a
  café doing nothing is a legitimate use of a Tuesday.
- **Have a rough end point**, even a loose one. Open-ended can become unmoored.

---

## Practicalities

- **Pack lighter than for a two-week trip.** Six months and two weeks need the same bag; you
  will do laundry either way.
- **Digitise everything** — passport, insurance, prescriptions, qualifications — into
  encrypted cloud storage plus an offline copy.
- **Prescription medication:** carry a doctor's letter, in original packaging, and research
  availability of your medication in the countries you are going to. Some common medications
  are controlled substances elsewhere.
- **Vaccinations** — plan a schedule with a travel clinic well ahead; some require multiple
  doses over months.
- **Two-factor authentication** must work on a number or device you will still have.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How does the Schengen 90/180 rule work?**
You may spend 90 days in any rolling 180-day period in the Schengen area. It is not per entry
or per calendar year — from any given date, look back 180 days and count. Use an official
calculator rather than estimating, and note that the EU's biometric Entry/Exit System now
records all entries and exits.

**Does normal travel insurance cover a six-month trip?**
Usually not. Annual multi-trip policies typically cap any single trip at 30 to 45 days, which
travellers often discover only when claiming. Long-stay or nomad-specific cover is a separate
product, usually bought monthly, and should include at least $250,000 of medical evacuation.

**Do you still pay tax if you travel for a year?**
Very possibly. Leaving does not automatically end tax residency, rules differ enormously by
nationality, and you can become tax resident elsewhere by exceeding a day-count threshold —
often around 183 days. Get advice specific to your own citizenship and circumstances.

**What is the hardest part of long-term travel?**
Consistently, around month four — a mix of fatigue, decision overload and distance from
routine. The reliable fix is slowing down: longer stays, occasionally renting somewhere for a
month, and keeping some routine that travels with you.

---

## The Bottom Line

Sort the visa arithmetic, the long-stay insurance and the tax question before you go, keep two
banks and a home address, and plan to move more slowly than you think you want to.
