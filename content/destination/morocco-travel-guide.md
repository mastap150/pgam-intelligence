# Morocco Travel Guide: Marrakech, the Sahara, Essaouira & What to Expect

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

Morocco is three hours from most of Europe and feels considerably further, which is most of
its appeal. It also has the steepest learning curve of any easily-reached destination: medina
navigation, negotiation as a default, and a desert-tour industry with enormous quality
variance.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Ten days:** Marrakech → Atlas → Sahara → Fes → Chefchaouen, or a shorter loop.
- **Fes has the better medina.** Marrakech has the better everything else.
- **The Sahara from Marrakech is a three-day commitment**, not an overnight. Merzouga has the
  proper dunes.
- **Stay in a riad.** It is the defining Moroccan accommodation and costs less than a hotel.
- **Negotiate everything**, cheerfully. The first price is an opening.

---

## The Route

### Marrakech — 3 days

The Jemaa el-Fnaa square, the souks, the Bahia Palace, the Saadian Tombs, the Majorelle
Garden. The **Ben Youssef Madrasa** is the most beautiful interior in the city and the most
often skipped.

**Stay in a riad inside the medina.** Traditional courtyard houses turned into small hotels —
quiet and cool inside, chaotic outside the door, which is the correct Marrakech experience.

### The Atlas Mountains — 1–2 days

**Imlil** for walking, and the Toubkal massif if you want a serious climb (that needs a guide
and proper planning). The **Ourika Valley** is an easy day trip from Marrakech.

### The Sahara — 3 days minimum

This is the trip most people get wrong.

**Merzouga (Erg Chebbi)** has the tall classic dunes and is roughly a **two-day drive each
way** from Marrakech over the Tizi n'Tichka pass and through the Dades and Todra gorges.
**Zagora** is closer but its dunes are notably smaller and less impressive.

**A "two-day desert trip" from Marrakech means one night and a great deal of minibus.** Three
days is the honest minimum, and the drive itself — gorges, kasbahs, Aït Benhaddou — is a
substantial part of the value.

**Tour quality varies enormously.** Read recent reviews specifically for group size, vehicle
condition and how much time is actually spent at the dunes rather than driving.

### Fes — 2–3 days

**The better medina** — around 9,000 lanes, largely unchanged, and genuinely disorienting.
The tanneries, the Al-Attarine and Bou Inania madrasas, the Al-Qarawiyyin complex.

**Hire a guide for the first half-day.** Not because you cannot manage, but because you will
see and understand ten times more, and it settles the constant offers of unofficial guidance.

### Chefchaouen — 1–2 days

The blue town in the Rif mountains. Small, photogenic, and much calmer than the imperial
cities. A good decompression stop.

### Essaouira — 1–2 days

Atlantic, windy, fortified, and by far the most relaxed city in Morocco. Three hours from
Marrakech and a genuine change of pace.

## Medina Rules

- **You will get lost.** Accept it. Offline maps help but do not fully work in covered lanes.
- **Unofficial guides are constant.** A firm "no thank you" and continuing to walk works.
  Someone who leads you somewhere will expect payment.
- **"That way is closed" is almost always untrue** — a standard redirection toward a shop.
- **Agree a price before any service**, including a photograph with anyone or anything.
- **Negotiate cheerfully.** It is expected, it is social, and the opening price assumes it.
  Roughly a third to half of the first ask is a common landing point.
- **Carry small notes.**
- **Dress modestly** — shoulders and knees covered. Enforced by convention, not law, but it
  changes the amount of attention you attract.

## What It Costs

Per person per day.

| Style | Daily cost |
|---|---|
| Budget | $35–$60 |
| Mid-range | $80–$140 |
| Comfortable | $200+ |

**Riads** run $50–$150 a night and are usually better value than hotels at the same price.
**A three-day Sahara tour** runs $120–$300 depending on group size and vehicle. **A car with
a driver** is roughly $80–$120 a day and worth considering over a group tour if there are
three or four of you.

## When to Go

| Period | Conditions |
|---|---|
| **Mar–May** | **Best** — warm, comfortable in the desert, green in the Atlas |
| Jun–Aug | 40 °C+ inland. Essaouira and the coast stay cool |
| **Sept–Nov** | **Best** — warm, clear, fewer people |
| Dec–Feb | Cool days, cold nights, snow in the Atlas. Desert nights are genuinely cold |

**Ramadan** changes daytime opening hours and the rhythm of the cities substantially. It is a
fascinating time to visit and a difficult one if you have not planned for it — check dates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many days do you need in Morocco?**
Ten to fourteen for a proper loop covering Marrakech, the Sahara, Fes and one coastal or
mountain stop. A week works for Marrakech, the Atlas and Essaouira without the desert, since
Merzouga alone needs three days.

**Is a Sahara desert tour from Marrakech worth it?**
Yes, if you give it three days. Merzouga has the tall dunes and is a two-day drive each way,
and the journey through the gorges and kasbahs is a real part of the experience. Two-day
tours mean one night and a great deal of minibus; Zagora is closer but its dunes are much
smaller.

**Is Fes or Marrakech better?**
Fes has the more remarkable medina — larger, older and less altered. Marrakech has better
food, nightlife, day trips and connections, and is the easier first stop. Most itineraries
include both.

**Should you negotiate prices in Morocco?**
Yes, in souks and for most services — the first price assumes it, and doing so cheerfully is
expected and social rather than confrontational. Fixed-price shops and restaurants are the
exception.

---

## The Bottom Line

Give the Sahara three days or skip it, stay in riads, hire a guide for your first half-day in
the Fes medina, and go in spring or autumn.
