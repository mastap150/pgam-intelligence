# Hotel Loyalty Programs Ranked: Where Your Points Are Worth the Most

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

Hotel points are not comparable across programmes, and the gap is much wider than most people
assume. **A World of Hyatt point is worth roughly twice a Marriott Bonvoy point and about
three times a Hilton Honors point.**

That single fact should change how you choose a programme, which card you put spending on,
and which points you burn first. Here is the ranking, why it holds, and the cases where it
flips.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Hyatt is the most valuable point**, by a wide margin — around 1.6¢ against Marriott's
  0.7¢ and Hilton's 0.5¢.
- **The reason is the award chart.** Hyatt still uses a fixed chart; the others price
  dynamically against cash rates.
- **A cheap point is not a bad programme.** Hilton points are worth less individually and are
  earned far faster.
- **2026 was a year of change** — Hyatt overhauled its chart in May, Wyndham in September,
  and Marriott has been quietly increasing pricing since July.

| Programme | Value per point | Chart type |

| **World of Hyatt** | **~1.6¢** | Fixed award chart |
| Wyndham Rewards | ~1.0¢ | Largely fixed |
| Marriott Bonvoy | ~0.7¢ | Dynamic |

| Choice Privileges | ~0.6¢ | Largely fixed |
| Hilton Honors | ~0.5¢ | Dynamic |

**Read this correctly.** Point value alone does not rank the programmes — it ranks the
points. Hilton points are worth a third of Hyatt's and you earn a great many more of them per
dollar. What matters is value per dollar spent, and value per dollar of the *stays you
actually take*.

---

## Why Hyatt Wins on Value

**A fixed award chart.** Hyatt prices standard properties at **5,000–45,000 points per
night**, with Category 7 luxury at **30,000–50,000**. That price does not move with the cash
rate.

The consequence: when a hotel's cash rate spikes — a conference, a festival, a peak weekend —
the points price does not follow. That is where outsized value appears, and it is
structurally impossible in a dynamic programme.

**Marriott, Hilton and IHG price dynamically**, meaning the points cost tracks the cash rate.
That caps the value of a point at roughly whatever the programme's internal conversion is,
and it removes the possibility of a genuine bargain.

**The trade-off:** Hyatt is much smaller. Fewer properties, thinner coverage outside major
cities and resort destinations. A programme with the best points and no hotel where you are
going is worth nothing.

---

## When the Ranking Flips

**Choose Hilton or Marriott if:**

- **Footprint matters more than value.** They are enormous, and in most cities you will find
  one. Hyatt frequently has nothing.
- **You want status you will actually use.** Both have accessible mid-tier status with
  meaningful breakfast and upgrade benefits.
- **You stay for work** and the earning volume is high enough that a lower-value point still
  produces plenty.

**Choose IHG if:** you value the fourth-night-free style benefits and stay in its
mid-market brands frequently.

**Choose Hyatt if:** you can plan stays around its footprint and you want the highest value
per point.

**The honest general answer:** for most leisure travellers, **earn transferable points and
decide later.** Chase Ultimate Rewards transfers to Hyatt at 1:1, which is widely considered
one of the best transfer relationships in the whole ecosystem, precisely because it converts
a flexible point into the most valuable hotel point at par.

## Getting Value Out of Hotel Points

1. **Redeem where cash rates are high.** Points value is the ratio of cash price to points
   price — the same 20,000-point night is a great redemption in Tokyo in cherry blossom week
   and a poor one in a suburban business hotel in February.
2. **Check the free-night-award threshold.** Many programmes cap card-earned free night
   certificates at a point value; topping up with points is often allowed and often worth it.
3. **Do not transfer airline miles into hotel points.** The rates are consistently poor. The
   flow works the other way, if at all.
4. **Watch for award chart changes.** Three major programmes moved during 2026 alone. If you
   are holding a large balance, holding is a decision with risk attached.
5. **Burn, don't hoard.** Same discipline as airline miles. Points depreciate through
   devaluation; they do not appreciate.

## On Status

Hotel status is generally easier to obtain and more immediately useful than airline status.

**What is genuinely valuable:**

- **Free breakfast** — often the single most valuable benefit, worth $20–$40 a day for two.
- **Late checkout** — underrated, particularly on the last day of a trip.
- **Upgrades** — real but inconsistent, and dependent on availability.

**What usually is not:**

- **Bonus point earning** at low tiers — the maths rarely justifies steering stays for it.
- **Status earned by credit card spending** unless you were going to do that spending anyway.

**The one that matters:** if you get free breakfast and late checkout reliably, mid-tier
status has paid for itself on almost any trip.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which hotel loyalty program has the most valuable points?**
World of Hyatt, at roughly 1.6¢ per point against Marriott Bonvoy's 0.7¢, IHG's 0.6¢ and
Hilton Honors' 0.5¢. The reason is structural: Hyatt still uses a fixed award chart, so
points prices do not rise with cash rates, while the others price dynamically.

**Is Hilton Honors bad because its points are worth less?**
No. Point value and programme quality are different things. Hilton points are worth less
individually and are earned much faster per dollar, and Hilton's footprint is far larger than
Hyatt's. The right comparison is value per dollar spent on the stays you actually take.

**What is the best way to earn hotel points?**
Transferable credit card points rather than committing to one chain — particularly Chase
Ultimate Rewards, which transfers to World of Hyatt at 1:1 and turns a flexible point into
the most valuable hotel point at par.

**Should I hold onto hotel points or use them?**
Use them. Hotel programmes devalue and three major ones changed their award pricing during
2026 alone. Points do not appreciate, and a redemption you actually take beats a better one
you never book.

---

## The Bottom Line

Hyatt points are worth roughly twice Marriott's and three times Hilton's because Hyatt still
prices from a fixed chart — but earn transferable points rather than committing to any chain,
and spend them where cash rates are highest.
