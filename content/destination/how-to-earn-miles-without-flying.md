# How to Earn Airline Miles Without Flying: The Complete Guide

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

Most people with a lot of miles did not get them by flying. Flying is a slow way to earn —
a transatlantic economy round trip might return 8,000–12,000 miles, and a business class
award to Europe costs 45,000 or more. At that rate you would fly five times to earn one
redemption.

The miles come from the ground. Here is where, ranked by how much they actually produce.

---

## The Quick Answer

1. **Credit card sign-up bonuses** — by far the largest single source.
2. **Everyday spending** on the right card.
3. **Transfer bonuses and promotions** — free uplift on points you already hold.
4. **Shopping portals, dining and hotel programmes** — small but genuinely free.

**The rule that governs all of it:** earn **transferable** points, not airline-specific
miles.

---

## Why Transferable Points Beat Airline Miles

This is the foundational decision and it determines whether everything else works.

**Transferable currencies** — American Express Membership Rewards, Chase Ultimate Rewards,
Citi ThankYou, Capital One — move to many different airline and hotel programmes.
**Airline miles sitting in one programme cannot move anywhere.**

**Why that matters:** award charts get devalued without notice. When British Airways repriced
much of its chart in December 2025, raising many routes 10–25%, anyone holding Avios took the
hit. Anyone holding transferable points moved to a different programme instead.

Flexibility is the only real hedge against devaluation, and it costs nothing to have.

## 1. Sign-Up Bonuses — the Big One

A single card bonus commonly exceeds what years of flying would earn. This is not a marginal
technique; it is the whole game for most people.

**How to do it sensibly:**

- **Meet the spend requirement with spending you were going to do anyway.** Time an
  application before a period of known expense — an annual insurance premium, a tax bill
  where the fee is worth it, a planned purchase.
- **Never spend money you would not otherwise spend** to hit a bonus. The maths never works,
  and this is where people lose more than they gain.
- **Read the eligibility rules.** Most issuers restrict repeat bonuses on the same product,
  and some limit how many cards you can open in a period.
- **Count the annual fee** against the bonus honestly, including whether you will use the
  credits that offset it.

**The hard prerequisite:** this only works if you clear the balance in full every month.
Interest on a carried balance destroys the value of any points earned, immediately and by a
wide margin. If you carry a balance, this entire category is not for you and the rest of this
article is the better place to spend your attention.

---

## 2. Everyday Spending

Slower, but it compounds and it requires no ongoing effort.

- **Match the card to the category.** Most cards pay bonus multiples on specific spending —
  groceries, dining, travel, transit. Using the right card for each is where the difference
  is.
- **Put recurring bills on it** — utilities, subscriptions, insurance — so the earning is
  automatic.
- **Watch for fees on the big-ticket items.** Tax payments, rent and tuition often carry
  processing fees that exceed the points earned; occasionally, during a bonus period, they
  do not.
- **Add authorised users** where household spending can be consolidated onto one earning
  account.

---

## 3. Transfer Bonuses — Free Uplift

Transferable programmes periodically run transfer bonuses to specific partners — commonly
**20–30%**, and sometimes more.

Transferring 50,000 points during a 30% bonus gives you 65,000 miles. That is a free 15,000
miles for waiting.

**How to use them:**

- **Do not chase a bonus into a programme you have no use for.** A 30% bonus on stranded
  miles is worth nothing.
- **Confirm the award space first, then transfer.** This is the single most important rule in
  points and it applies here more than anywhere — transfers are almost always irreversible.
- **Some programmes run bonuses predictably enough** to be worth waiting a few weeks for if
  your travel is not imminent.

**A worked example of why this matters:** Avianca LifeMiles prices Lufthansa and Swiss
business class within Europe at around 25,000 one-way and transatlantic at about 63,000, and
runs frequent Amex transfer bonuses. A 25% bonus turns 50,000 Amex points into 62,500
LifeMiles — a transatlantic business class seat for what is effectively 50,000 flexible
points.

## 4. The Small Stuff That Adds Up

Genuinely free, individually minor, collectively meaningful.

- **Shopping portals** — click through your airline or card programme's portal before buying
  online and earn miles per dollar on top of your card's earning. Costs nothing, takes ten
  seconds, and is the most consistently overlooked source.
- **Dining programmes** — register your card, earn miles at participating restaurants
  automatically.
- **Hotel programmes** — earn hotel points on stays, many of which transfer to airlines
  (usually at poor rates, so prefer redeeming them for hotels).
- **Car hire and travel bookings** through partner links.
- **Surveys and promotions** — low return per hour; skip unless you enjoy them.

---

## What to Avoid

- **Buying miles speculatively.** Occasionally worth it for a specific booking you have
  already confirmed. Never as a general strategy.
- **Manufactured spending schemes.** They violate card terms, get accounts shut down, and
  the tactics circulating publicly are usually already dead.
- **Hoarding.** Programmes devalue. Points have no value until used, and a redemption you
  actually take at 2¢ beats a theoretical 8¢ you never book.
- **Chasing status you will not use.** Elite status earned through spending is rarely worth
  the spending required unless you travel often.
- **Any of this while carrying a balance.** Worth repeating.

## What They're Worth

| Redemption | Typical value per point |
|---|---|
| Cash back or portal booking | ~1¢ — the baseline to beat |
| Economy award vs a cheap cash fare | 0.8–1.2¢ — usually not worth it |
| Business class, standard chart | 3–5¢ |
| Business class, a partner sweet spot | **5–10¢** |

**The discipline:** if a redemption returns under about 1.5¢ per point, pay cash and keep the
points for something better.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the fastest way to earn airline miles?**
Credit card sign-up bonuses, by a wide margin. A single bonus commonly exceeds what several
years of flying would earn. The condition is that you meet the spend requirement with
spending you would have done anyway, and clear the balance in full each month.

**Should I earn airline miles or transferable points?**
Transferable points — Amex Membership Rewards, Chase Ultimate Rewards, Citi ThankYou, Capital
One. They can move to many airline programmes, which means a devaluation in one programme
does not strand you. Airline miles sitting in a single programme cannot be moved.

**Are shopping portals worth using?**
Yes. They cost nothing, take seconds, and stack on top of what your card already earns.
Individually small, but it is the most consistently overlooked free earning source available.

**Is it worth buying miles?**
Rarely as a strategy. It can make sense to top up a balance for a specific award you have
already found and confirmed, where the cost per mile is well below the value of the
redemption. Buying speculatively, in the hope of a future use, generally loses.

---

## The Bottom Line

Earn transferable points rather than airline miles, treat sign-up bonuses as the main event
and everyday spending as the compounding background, wait for transfer bonuses when you can —
and never carry a balance to do any of it.
