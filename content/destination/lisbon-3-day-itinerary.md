# The Perfect 3-Day Lisbon Itinerary

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

Lisbon is built on seven hills and the itineraries that fail are the ones that ignore
topography. Three days is right for the city; a fourth belongs to Sintra, which is not a
half-day trip no matter what anyone tells you.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Day 1:** Baixa, Chiado, Bairro Alto. **Day 2:** Alfama and Graça. **Day 3:** Belém.
- **Sintra needs a full day** — add it as day four, not as an afternoon.
- **Tram 28 is famously queued.** Take tram 12 or ride 28 from Campo de Ourique instead.
- **Walk downhill, ride uphill.** The funiculars and the Elevador exist for a reason.
- **City tax:** €4 per person per night, capped at seven nights.

---

## Day 1 — Baixa, Chiado, Bairro Alto

Start in **Praça do Comércio**, the great riverfront square, and walk north through the
grid of **Baixa** — rebuilt on a plan after the 1755 earthquake, which is why it is the only
flat, straight part of central Lisbon.

Up into **Chiado** for bookshops and coffee at Brasileira, then **Bairro Alto**, which is
quiet and shuttered by day and entirely different after 10pm.

**Miradouro de São Pedro de Alcântara** for the first of the viewpoints. Lisbon's
*miradouros* are the organising feature of the city and you should collect them.

**Evening:** dinner in Bairro Alto or Cais do Sodré, then **fado**. Book a small house
rather than a dinner-show; the difference is enormous.

## Day 2 — Alfama and Graça

The oldest part of the city, and the part the earthquake spared. A medieval street plan on a
hill, which means you will get lost. That is the correct experience.

- **São Jorge Castle** — go at opening. The view is the point.
- **Sé Cathedral** and the streets below it.
- **Miradouro das Portas do Sol** and **Santa Luzia**, next to each other.
- **Feira da Ladra** flea market if it is Tuesday or Saturday.
- **Graça** above Alfama — less visited, better priced, and the **Miradouro da Senhora do
  Monte** is the highest and quietest of them.

**On tram 28:** it runs through Alfama and the queue at Martim Moniz regularly exceeds an
hour. **Board at Campo de Ourique** at the other end of the line, or take **tram 12**, which
covers a shorter version of the same route with a fraction of the crowd.

## Day 3 — Belém

West along the river, and easily a full day.

- **Mosteiro dos Jerónimos** — the finest Manueline building in Portugal. Book ahead; the
  queue is real.
- **Torre de Belém** — photograph it, decide whether the interior queue is worth it (usually
  not).
- **Padrão dos Descobrimentos** — the discoveries monument, with a viewing deck.
- **MAAT** — contemporary art and architecture on the waterfront.
- **Pastéis de Belém** — the original custard tarts, from the bakery that has made them since
  1837. Eat in rather than queueing at the takeaway window.

**Getting there:** tram 15 from Praça da Figueira, or the train from Cais do Sodré.

## Day 4 — Sintra, if you have it

**Do not try to do Sintra in an afternoon.** It is a UNESCO landscape of palaces in the hills
40 minutes by train from Rossio, and the sites are spread out and slow to move between.

**The realistic plan:** pick two. **Pena Palace** (book a timed slot, go at opening) plus
either **Quinta da Regaleira** (the initiation well, the gardens) or the **Moorish Castle**.
Trying for four means seeing the inside of a bus.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is 3 days enough for Lisbon?**
For the city itself, yes — Baixa and Chiado, Alfama, and Belém each fill a day comfortably.
Add a fourth day for Sintra, which cannot be done properly as an afternoon trip.

**Is tram 28 worth it in Lisbon?**
The route is genuinely lovely; the queue at Martim Moniz often exceeds an hour. Board at the
Campo de Ourique end instead, or take tram 12, which covers a shorter version of the same
route with far fewer people.

**Do you pay a tourist tax in Lisbon?**
Yes — €4 per person per night, capped at seven nights. Airbnb collects it automatically;
hotels usually add it at checkout.

**How do you get to Sintra from Lisbon?**
Train from Rossio station, around 40 minutes, running frequently. Once there the sites are
spread across hills — use the local bus or a tuk-tuk, and plan on two palaces, not four.

---

## The Bottom Line

Give each of the three central areas a full day, walk downhill and ride uphill, and give
Sintra a day of its own or leave it for next time.
