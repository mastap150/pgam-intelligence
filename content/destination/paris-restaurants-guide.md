# Where Parisians Actually Eat: A Paris Restaurant Guide

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

The difference between eating badly and eating extraordinarily well in Paris is not budget.
It is knowing which streets to avoid, how to read a menu, and booking two weeks earlier than
feels necessary.

This guide is deliberately about **areas and method** rather than a list of named
restaurants. Restaurant lists date within a year; the method does not.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Avoid anywhere with a photo menu, a tout outside, or a view of a monument.**
- **Book two to three weeks ahead** for anything well regarded. Paris runs on reservations.
- **Eat where the menu is short and handwritten** — it means the kitchen shops daily.
- **Lunch formules** cost roughly half of dinner at the same restaurant.
- **The best eating areas are the 11th, 10th, 9th and 20th**, not the 1st, 7th or 8th.

---

## How to Read a Paris Restaurant

**Good signs**

- A **short menu**, often handwritten or on a chalkboard, changing daily or weekly.
- **Ardoise** (slate) specials.
- A crowded room at 1pm on a weekday, full of people not carrying luggage.
- **Menu only in French.** Not snobbery — it means the customers are French.
- The words *bistrot*, *cave à manger* (wine bar with real food), or *néo-bistrot*.

**Bad signs**

- Photographs of the food.
- A menu in six languages.
- Someone outside inviting you in.
- "Touristic menu."
- Open continuously from 11am to 11pm. Real kitchens close between services.

**The single most reliable rule:** walk two streets away from any monument before you eat.

## Where to Eat, by Arrondissement

| Area | What it is | Go for |
|---|---|---|
| **11th** (Oberkampf, Charonne) | The centre of modern Paris dining | Néo-bistrots, natural wine |
| **10th** (Canal Saint-Martin) | Young, informal | Small plates, coffee, bakeries |
| **9th** (South Pigalle) | Confident and busy | Bistrots, cocktails |
| **20th** (Belleville, Ménilmontant) | Diverse, cheaper | North African, Chinese, Vietnamese |
| **5th / 6th** | Classic Left Bank | Old-school bistrots — pick carefully |
| **1st, 7th, 8th** | Monuments | **Eat elsewhere** |

**Belleville and the 13th** are where Paris's best Chinese and Vietnamese food is, and they
are almost entirely absent from English-language coverage of the city.

## Categories Worth Knowing

**Bouillons** — enormous, cheap, historic brasseries serving classic French food at prices
that look like a mistake. Originally workers' canteens. They do not take reservations, the
queues move, and they are genuinely good value rather than merely cheap.

**Boulangeries** — the annual *meilleure baguette de Paris* competition is a real thing and
the winner is announced each spring. Following the current and recent winners is a reliable
way to find excellent bread anywhere in the city.

**Marchés** — the covered and street markets. Marché d'Aligre in the 12th is the best
combination of quality, price and atmosphere.

**Cave à manger** — a wine shop that serves food, usually small plates. Often the best value
serious eating in the city.

## The Reservation Problem

Paris runs on reservations to a degree that surprises visitors.

- **Two to three weeks ahead** for anything with a reputation. Longer for the very best.
- **Most bookings now go through online platforms**; some smaller places still take phone
  calls only, in French.
- **Lunch is far easier to book than dinner** and costs less for the same kitchen.
- **Many kitchens close Sunday and Monday**, and August sees widespread closures for the
  annual holiday.
- **Turning up at 7pm** works more often than people think — that is early by Parisian
  standards and tables are frequently free before the 8:30 rush.

## What It Costs

| Meal | Typical price |
|---|---|
| Bakery lunch (sandwich, pastry, coffee) | €8–€12 |
| Bouillon dinner | €15–€25 |
| Lunch formule at a good bistrot | €20–€30 |
| Dinner at the same bistrot | €45–€70 |
| Tasting menu, well-regarded kitchen | €90–€200 |

**Service is included** by law — *service compris*. Rounding up or leaving a few euros for
good service is normal; 20% is not expected and marks you out.

**Coffee standing at the bar** costs meaningfully less than at a table, and less again on the
terrace. The price list shows all three.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How far in advance should you book restaurants in Paris?**
Two to three weeks for anything well regarded, and longer for the most sought-after kitchens.
Lunch is considerably easier to book than dinner and costs roughly half at the same
restaurant.

**How do you avoid tourist-trap restaurants in Paris?**
Walk two streets away from any monument, and avoid anywhere with photographs on the menu,
a menu in multiple languages, someone touting outside, or continuous service from morning to
late evening. Look instead for a short handwritten menu that changes regularly.

**Do you tip in Paris?**
Service is included by law. Rounding up the bill or leaving a few euros for good service is
normal and appreciated; a 20% tip is not expected.

**Which arrondissement is best for food in Paris?**
The 11th is the centre of modern Paris dining, with the 10th, 9th and 20th close behind. The
1st, 7th and 8th are where the monuments are and where the food is worst relative to price.

---

## The Bottom Line

Eat in the 11th rather than near the Louvre, book two weeks ahead or go at 7pm, and choose the
restaurant with the shortest menu on the street.
