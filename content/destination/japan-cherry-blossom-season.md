# Japan Cherry Blossom Season: Best Spots and Exact Timing by Region

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

The planning problem with cherry blossom season is simple and unavoidable: **peak bloom
lasts about a week in any given place, and nobody can tell you when it will be until roughly
two months beforehand.** Flights and hotels for late March in Japan need booking long before
that.

The way around it is not prediction. It is building an itinerary that moves, so that
whichever week the bloom lands, you are somewhere it is happening.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Bloom moves south to north** over roughly two months — Kyushu in late March, Tokyo and
  Kyoto late March to early April, Hokkaido late April into May.
- **Full bloom (*mankai*) is about a week after first bloom**, and lasts a few days to a week
  depending on wind and rain.
- **Forecasts start publishing in January** and update through February and March.
- **Book flights and hotels early, keep internal travel flexible.** That is the whole
  strategy.
- **Late March to early April** is the safest single window for Tokyo and Kyoto.

---

## How the Season Actually Works

The Japanese forecast tracks *somei yoshino*, the dominant cultivated variety, which blooms
almost simultaneously across a region because the trees are clones.

| Stage | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Kaika** (開花) | First bloom — a few flowers open on the benchmark tree |
| **Mankai** (満開) | Full bloom, typically **about a week after kaika** |
| Peak viewing | Roughly mankai plus 2–5 days |
| End | Petals fall; heavy rain or wind can end it in a day |

**The whole window in one city is 7–10 days**, and the genuinely spectacular part is shorter.

**Forecasts publish from January**, refine through February and March, and become reasonably
reliable about two weeks out — which is far too late to book international flights at a
sensible price.

## Regional Timing

Indicative, based on typical years. Warm winters pull dates earlier; cold ones push later.

| Region | Typical first bloom | Typical full bloom |
|---|---|---|
| Okinawa | Late January (different variety) | February |
| Fukuoka, Kyushu | Mid–late March | Late March |
| Hiroshima | Late March | Late March–early April |
| Osaka, Kyoto | Late March | **Early April** |
| Tokyo | Mid–late March | **Late March–early April** |
| Kanazawa, Japanese Alps | Early April | Mid April |
| Sendai, Tohoku | Mid April | Mid–late April |
| Hirosaki, Aomori | Late April | Late April |
| Sapporo, Hokkaido | Late April–early May | **Early May** |

**The two-month spread is your insurance.** If Tokyo blooms early and you miss it, Tohoku is
two weeks behind and reachable in a few hours.

## Where to See It

### Tokyo

- **Meguro River** — a canal lined with trees, lit at night. The best urban blossom in Japan.
- **Shinjuku Gyoen** — many varieties, so a longer overall season. Entry fee keeps it calmer.
- **Ueno Park** — the classic *hanami* party scene. Loud, crowded, genuinely fun.
- **Chidorigafuchi** — the moat by the Imperial Palace, with rowing boats under the branches.

### Kyoto

- **Philosopher's Path** — a canal walk lined with trees. Extremely busy by mid-morning.
- **Maruyama Park** — Kyoto's main hanami park, with a famous weeping cherry lit at night.
- **Arashiyama** — blossom against the hills; go at dawn.
- **Daigo-ji** — a temple with an exceptional display and fewer visitors than the central
  sites.

### Beyond the obvious

- **Hirosaki Castle, Aomori** — widely considered the finest blossom site in Japan, and
  visited by a fraction of Kyoto's numbers. Late April.
- **Yoshino, Nara** — tens of thousands of trees on a mountainside, blooming in bands up the
  slope.
- **Kanazawa Castle and Kenroku-en** — mid-April, less crowded than the Tokyo–Kyoto corridor.

## How to Plan Around an Unknown Date

**The strategy: book the ends, keep the middle loose.**

1. **Book international flights 4–8 months out.** Prices for late March and early April rise
   sharply and availability tightens.
2. **Book Tokyo and Kyoto accommodation early** — these sell out and prices roughly double.
   Choose flexible-cancellation rates.
3. **Do not pre-book internal travel.** Shinkansen seats can be reserved days ahead; leaving
   them open lets you chase the bloom.
4. **Target late March to early April** if you must pick one window — it covers Tokyo and
   Kyoto in most years.
5. **Build in a northern escape.** If the bloom came early, Tohoku or Kanazawa are behind.
   If it is late, Kyushu is ahead.
6. **Watch the forecasts from January** and adjust the internal route, not the trip.

**On the JR Pass:** blossom itineraries often involve chasing north or south at short notice,
which is exactly when a rail pass earns its cost. Note the pass rose in price on 1 October

Per person per day, mid-range, excluding international flights.

**Golden Week** (late April into early May) overlaps Hokkaido's bloom and is a domestic
travel peak — book much further ahead or avoid.

## Hanami: How It's Actually Done

*Hanami* — flower viewing — is a picnic, not a walk.

- **Groups reserve space with tarps** early in the morning, often sending the most junior
  colleague at dawn to sit on it all day.
- **Convenience stores sell hanami food and drink** in season, and this is entirely normal
  and correct.
- **Evening viewing (*yozakura*)** with lit trees is a genuinely different experience from
  daytime and considerably less crowded at the smaller sites.
- **Take your rubbish with you.** Public bins are scarce and this matters.
- **Alcohol in parks is legal and normal** in most hanami spots, though some parks restrict
  it — check signage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When is cherry blossom season in Japan?**
It moves south to north over roughly two months. Kyushu blooms in late March, Tokyo and
Kyoto from late March into early April, the Japanese Alps and Tohoku in mid-to-late April,
and Hokkaido in early May. Full bloom in any one place lasts about a week.

**How accurate are cherry blossom forecasts?**
Forecasts begin publishing in January and refine through February and March, becoming
reasonably reliable roughly two weeks out — which is far later than you can sensibly book
international flights. Plan a route that can move rather than trying to predict a date.

**Where is the best place to see cherry blossoms in Japan?**
Hirosaki Castle in Aomori is widely considered the finest display in the country and sees a
fraction of Kyoto's crowds, though it blooms later, in late April. For the classic
experience, Kyoto's Philosopher's Path and Maruyama Park, or Tokyo's Meguro River at night.

**Is Japan expensive during cherry blossom season?**
Accommodation roughly doubles in Tokyo and Kyoto and sells out months ahead. Food and
transport costs are unchanged. Budget ¥25,000–¥40,000 per person per day mid-range, against
¥15,000–¥25,000 in a normal shoulder season.

## The Bottom Line

Book flights and city hotels months ahead for the late-March-to-early-April window, leave
your internal travel unbooked, and watch the forecasts from January so you can move toward
the bloom rather than hoping it comes to you.
