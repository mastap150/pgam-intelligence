# Cape Town Travel Guide: Where to Stay, What to Do, and What to Skip

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

Cape Town has one planning rule that overrides everything else: **do Table Mountain on the
first clear day, whenever that is.** The cableway closes in high wind and cloud, and visitors
who save it for the last day routinely miss it entirely.

Build the whole trip that way — a list of weather-dependent things and a list of
weather-proof ones, and decide each morning.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Table Mountain on the first clear morning.** Not on a fixed day.
- **Stay in the City Bowl, Sea Point or Camps Bay.** Each suits a different trip.
- **Hire a car.** The peninsula and winelands need one, and it improves safety at night.
- **Five days minimum**; a week with the winelands.
- **Best months:** October–April, with February and March the warmest and least windy.

---

## The Weather Rule

Cape Town's weather is fast-changing and the wind is a genuine factor — the south-easter,
locally called the Cape Doctor, blows hard through summer.

**Weather-dependent, do on a clear calm day:**
Table Mountain cableway, Lion's Head at sunrise, Cape Point and the peninsula drive, beaches,
boat trips.

**Weather-proof, save for a bad day:**
District Six Museum, Zeitz MOCAA, the Bo-Kaap, wine tasting in Constantia or Stellenbosch,
Robben Island (though the ferry itself is sea-dependent), restaurants.

**Check the cableway's own status page each morning.** It closes regularly and posts
in advance.

---

## What to Do

**Table Mountain** — cableway, or walk up Platteklip Gorge (steep, two to three hours, no
shade). Rotating cable cars; take the first one of the day.

**Lion's Head** — a 45–60 minute walk with the best view of the city and the mountain
together. Sunrise or sunset; take a torch.

**The Cape Peninsula drive** — a full day. Chapman's Peak Drive (a toll road, and one of the
world's great coastal roads), Boulders Beach for the penguins, Cape Point, and Simon's Town.
Do it clockwise.

**The Winelands** — Stellenbosch, Franschhoek and Constantia. Constantia is 20 minutes from
the city; Stellenbosch and Franschhoek are 45–60 and deserve a full day or an overnight.
**Nominate a driver or book one** — tastings add up faster than people plan for.

**Robben Island** — book well ahead. Tours are led by former political prisoners, which is
what makes it what it is. Ferries cancel in rough seas.

**Bo-Kaap** — the Cape Malay quarter. Visit respectfully; these are people's homes, and
photographing doorways without asking has become a genuine local grievance.

**Zeitz MOCAA** — contemporary African art in a converted grain silo. Excellent, and indoors.

**Kirstenbosch** — botanical gardens on the mountain's eastern slope, among the finest in the
world. Summer sunset concerts.

## Safety, Honestly

Cape Town has a high crime rate, and it is also visited by large numbers of people who have
no trouble at all. Both are true, and the reconciliation is that crime is heavily
concentrated in areas visitors do not go.

**Practical measures that experienced visitors take as standard:**

- **Do not walk at night**, including short distances in central areas. Use a car or a
  ride-hailing app.
- **Do not leave anything visible in a parked car**, ever, anywhere.
- **Use ride-hailing apps** rather than street taxis.
- **Do not hike alone**, especially on Lion's Head or Table Mountain trails — muggings on
  quiet paths do occur. Go in groups, in daylight, on busy routes.
- **Be aware at ATMs**, and never accept help from anyone who approaches you at one.
- **Ask your accommodation** which specific streets are fine and which are not. Local
  knowledge is granular and accurate.

**Township tours:** these exist and opinions on them are genuinely divided. If you go, choose
an operator that is community-owned or clearly channels revenue locally, and treat it as a
visit to a place people live rather than a viewing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Cape Town safe for tourists?**
Cape Town has a high crime rate that is heavily concentrated in areas visitors do not go, and
most visitors have no trouble. The standard precautions are to avoid walking at night even
short distances, never leave anything visible in a parked car, use ride-hailing apps rather
than street taxis, and never hike alone on the mountain trails.

**How many days do you need in Cape Town?**
Five minimum — the city, Table Mountain, the peninsula drive and Robben Island. A week lets
you add the winelands properly and gives you spare days for weather.

**When should you go up Table Mountain?**
On the first clear, calm morning of your trip, whenever that falls. The cableway closes
frequently in wind and cloud, and visitors who save it for a fixed later day often miss it
entirely. Check the cableway's status page each morning.

**Where is the best place to stay in Cape Town?**
The City Bowl or Gardens for a first visit — walkable, central, close to the restaurants. Sea
Point for value and the promenade, Camps Bay for beaches at a premium, and the V&A Waterfront
for convenience if you want to be insulated.

---

## The Bottom Line

Keep two lists — weather-dependent and weather-proof — and decide each morning, hire a car,
don't walk at night, and go in February or March.
