# Rwanda Gorilla Trekking: Permits, Costs, Logistics & What to Expect

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

Gorilla trekking is the most expensive single hour in African travel, and the permit price is
the whole decision: **a Rwandan permit costs $1,500 and a Ugandan one costs $800** — falling
to **$600 in Uganda's low season** of April, May and November.

That $700 gap is worth understanding properly, because the cheaper option is not obviously
the worse one.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Rwanda:** $1,500 per permit. Volcanoes National Park, 2–3 hours from Kigali.
- **Uganda:** $800, or **$600 in April, May and November**. Bwindi, 8–10 hours from Entebbe
  or a domestic flight.
- **One hour with the gorillas.** That is the rule in both countries, strictly enforced.
- **Book months ahead.** Permits are capped by the number of habituated family groups.
- **Minimum age 15** in both countries.

---

## The Permit Comparison

| | Rwanda | Uganda |
|---|---|---|
| Permit cost | **$1,500** | **$800** (**$600** Apr, May, Nov) |
| Park | Volcanoes NP | Bwindi Impenetrable NP, Mgahinga NP |
| Access from capital | 2–3 hours by road from Kigali | 8–10 hours by road, or a domestic flight |
| Terrain | Steep, bamboo and open slopes | Dense forest — genuinely "impenetrable" |
| Trek difficulty | Moderate to hard | Often harder underfoot |
| Lodging | More high-end options | Broader range, more budget choices |
| Combine with | Kigali, Nyungwe, Akagera | Queen Elizabeth NP, chimps at Kibale, the Nile |

**What the extra $700 actually buys in Rwanda:** proximity. Kigali is a short, easy flight
from many hubs, and the park is two to three hours away on good roads, so a trek is doable in
a compressed three-night trip. Uganda's Bwindi requires either a long drive or an extra
domestic flight, and realistically a longer trip.

**What Uganda offers instead:** the same experience for roughly half, in denser and arguably
more atmospheric forest, plus a much stronger wider safari circuit — chimpanzee tracking at
Kibale, tree-climbing lions in Ishasha, and the Nile at Jinja. If you have the days, Uganda
is the better-value trip by a clear margin.

**The honest summary:** Rwanda if time is scarcer than money. Uganda if it is the other way
round, or if you want a fuller trip than a single hour.

## What Actually Happens

- **Early start.** Briefing at park headquarters around 7:00, group allocation, then transfer
  to the trailhead.
- **Groups of eight** maximum per gorilla family, with a guide, trackers and armed escorts —
  the escorts are for forest buffalo and elephant, not gorillas.
- **The trek varies enormously** — 30 minutes to six hours each way, depending on where the
  family moved overnight. Trackers locate them first and radio the position.
- **One hour with them.** Non-negotiable, timed from the moment you make contact.
- **Distance rule:** you are asked to stay several metres back. The gorillas frequently
  ignore this and approach; you are expected to move away.
- **No trekking if you are ill.** Gorillas share our susceptibility to human respiratory
  infections, and this rule protects them. Masks are commonly required.
- **Porters** are available for around $20 and are worth every cent, both for your knees and
  because it directs income to the local community.

---

## Fitness and Terrain

**Be honest with yourself.** This is altitude — roughly 2,300–3,000 m — on steep, muddy,
often trackless ground, sometimes for hours in each direction.

**It is manageable for most reasonably fit people**, and the guides adjust: tell them at the
briefing if you want an easier family, and they will allocate accordingly. Groups nearer the
park edge involve shorter treks.

**What you need:** waterproof hiking boots with ankle support, gaiters, long sleeves and
trousers (stinging nettles are constant), gardening-style gloves for grabbing vegetation,
rain gear, and more water than feels necessary.

## Booking

- **Book several months ahead**, and further for peak season — permit numbers are capped by
  the number of habituated families, so supply is genuinely fixed.
- **Book through a tour operator or directly** with the Rwanda Development Board or Uganda
  Wildlife Authority. Operators bundle permits with transfers and lodging, which is usually
  simpler.
- **Two treks are better than one** if the budget stretches — the experience varies hugely by
  family, weather and how far they have moved.
- **Permits are date- and park-specific** and generally non-transferable. Build in a buffer
  day for flight delays; missing a $1,500 permit because of a missed connection is a real
  risk.

## What It Costs, All In

| Item | Rwanda | Uganda |
|---|---|---|
| Permit | $1,500 | $800 ($600 low season) |
| Lodging, per night | $150–$800+ | $80–$600 |
| Transfers | $200–$400 | $300–$600 (or a domestic flight) |
| Porter, tips | $50–$100 | $50–$100 |
| **Typical 3-night trip** | **$2,500–$4,000** | **$1,600–$2,800** |

**Where the permit money goes:** a share is allocated to surrounding communities under
revenue-sharing schemes in both countries, and the trekking economy is a substantial part of
why mountain gorilla numbers have risen. This is one of the clearer cases in travel where the
high price is doing the conservation work rather than just capturing demand.

## When to Go

| Period | Conditions |
|---|---|
| **Jun–Sept, Dec–Feb** | Dry seasons. Easier trekking, peak demand |
| **Mar–May, Oct–Nov** | Wet. Muddier and harder, fewer people — and **Uganda discounts permits in April, May and November** |

**The wet season is genuinely harder**, not just wetter — steep mud at altitude is
significant work. The trade-off is a $200 permit saving in Uganda and much lower lodge rates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does a gorilla trekking permit cost?**
$1,500 per person in Rwanda and $800 in Uganda, with Uganda dropping to $600 in its low-season
months of April, May and November. The permit buys one hour with a habituated gorilla family.

**Is Rwanda or Uganda better for gorilla trekking?**
Rwanda if your time is limited — Volcanoes National Park is two to three hours from Kigali on
good roads. Uganda if budget matters more or you want a fuller trip: permits are roughly half
the price and the wider safari circuit, including chimpanzee tracking at Kibale, is
considerably stronger.

**How difficult is gorilla trekking?**
Moderate to hard. It takes place at 2,300–3,000 m on steep, muddy, often trackless ground, and
the trek can be anywhere from 30 minutes to six hours each way. Guides can allocate an easier
family group if you ask at the morning briefing.

**How far in advance should you book gorilla permits?**
Several months, and more for peak dry-season dates. Permit numbers are capped by the number of
habituated gorilla families, so availability is genuinely fixed rather than demand-driven.

---

## The Bottom Line

Rwanda if time is the constraint, Uganda if money is — and in Uganda, going in April, May or
November saves $200 on the permit and a great deal more on lodging.
