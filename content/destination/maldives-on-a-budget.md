# The Maldives on a Budget: Guesthouses, Local Islands & How to Cut Costs

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

The Maldives stopped being exclusively a honeymoon destination in 2009, when a change in the
law allowed guesthouses to operate on inhabited islands. That single regulatory change created
a version of the country you can visit for under $100 a day.

The water is the same water. Almost everything else is different, and you should know exactly
what you are trading.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Stay on a local (inhabited) island**, not a resort island. Guesthouses run $50–$120 a
  night.
- **Take the public ferry** where you can — a few dollars against $250+ for a seaplane.
- **Local islands are Muslim communities.** Modest dress away from designated "bikini
  beaches", and no alcohol on the island.
- **Budget $70–$130 per person per day** all in.
- **Best months:** November to April, the dry season.

---

## Resort Island vs Local Island

| | Resort island | Local island |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per night | $400–$2,000+ | $50–$120 |
| Who else is there | Only guests | A working community |
| Alcohol | Yes | **No**, except on offshore "safari" boats or nearby resorts |
| Dress | Anything | Modest in the village; swimwear on designated beaches |
| Transfer | Seaplane or speedboat, $200–$600 | Public ferry, $2–$5, or speedboat $25–$50 |
| Food | Resort restaurants | Local cafés and guesthouse meals |
| Snorkelling and diving | On site | Excursions, often better value |

**One island resort = one island.** That is what the money buys: total privacy, no
community, and no reason to leave.

**The honest trade:** a local island gives you the same reefs, the same water and a real
place with people living in it, for a fifth of the price. It does not give you an overwater
villa, a beach to yourself, or a cocktail at sunset.

---

## Which Local Islands

- **Maafushi** — the most developed for tourism, closest to Malé, most guesthouses and
  excursions, and the easiest first choice. Also the busiest.
- **Thulusdhoo** — surf, and a more relaxed feel.
- **Dhigurah** — long sandbank, and one of the best places in the country for whale sharks.
- **Fulidhoo** — small, quiet, good for a slower stay.
- **Ukulhas** — well regarded for cleanliness and beaches.

**Combine two or three.** Ferries connect them cheaply, and a week on one small island is a
long time.

---

## Getting Around Cheaply

**Public ferries** are the key to a budget trip: a few dollars per hop, run by the state
operator, and they connect Malé to most inhabited islands. **The catch is the schedule** —
many routes run only a few times a week and **most do not run on Fridays**, the day of
congregational prayer. Build the itinerary around the timetable, not the other way round.

**Speedboat transfers** cost $25–$50 and run daily to the popular islands. Faster, still a
fraction of a seaplane.

**Seaplanes** ($200–$600 return) serve resorts and distant atolls only, and they do not fly
after dark.

## The Rules on Local Islands

These are inhabited Muslim communities, not resorts. The rules are straightforward and taken
seriously.

- **Modest dress in the village** — shoulders and knees covered for everyone.
- **Swimwear only on designated "bikini beaches"**, which most tourist-facing islands now
  have. Not on the village beach.
- **No alcohol on inhabited islands.** Guesthouses often arrange trips to a floating bar or a
  nearby resort day-pass for those who want it.
- **No pork.**
- **Ramadan** changes daytime eating and opening hours substantially — check dates before
  booking.

None of this is onerous. It does mean a local-island trip is not a resort trip with a
discount.

## What It Costs

Per person per day.

| Style | Daily cost |
|---|---|
| Budget local island | $70–$100 |
| Comfortable local island | $120–$180 |
| Mid-range resort | $400+ |
| Luxury resort | $1,000+ |

**Typical breakdown on a local island:** guesthouse $60–$100 per room, meals $20–$30 per
person, one excursion $40–$80.

**Excursions are where a budget trip spends.** Snorkelling with manta rays or whale sharks,
sandbank picnics, dolphin trips — $40–$80 each and genuinely the reason to be there. Budget
for two or three rather than one a day.

**Note:** a green tax and goods and services tax apply to tourist accommodation, and rates
have been revised in recent years. Confirm current rates when booking rather than assuming
the quoted room price is final.

## When to Go

| Period | Conditions |
|---|---|
| **Nov–Apr** | Dry season. Best weather, highest prices, peak Dec–Feb |
| **May–Oct** | Wet season — rain in bursts, cheaper, better for surf and whale sharks |

**The wet season is more usable than the label suggests.** Rain tends to come in short heavy
spells rather than all day, and prices fall substantially.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can you visit the Maldives on a budget?**
Yes. Guesthouses on inhabited local islands, legal since 2009, run $50–$120 a night against
$400+ for resort islands. With public ferries and local meals, $70–$130 per person per day
covers a comfortable trip.

**What is the difference between a local island and a resort island?**
A resort occupies its own private island with no permanent community; a local island is an
inhabited Maldivian village where guesthouses operate alongside residents. Local islands are
far cheaper and have alcohol restrictions and modest dress requirements away from designated
beaches.

**Can you drink alcohol in the Maldives?**
Not on inhabited islands, which are Muslim communities. Alcohol is served on resort islands
and on offshore boats, and many guesthouses arrange trips to a floating bar or a resort day
pass.

**How do you get between islands in the Maldives?**
Public ferries are cheapest at a few dollars per hop, but many routes run only a few times a
week and most do not operate on Fridays. Speedboat transfers cost $25–$50 and run daily to
popular islands. Seaplanes serve resorts and distant atolls at $200–$600 return.

---

## The Bottom Line

Stay in guesthouses on two or three local islands, move by public ferry around the Friday
gap, spend the savings on excursions, and accept that you are visiting a country rather than
a resort.
