# Machu Picchu: The Complete Guide to Getting There Without a Tour Group

[BOOKING WARNING: this article's core facts change more often than any other on the site.
Verify the circuit structure and capacity figures before each republish.]

*By [Author] · [Date] · 9 min read*

You do not need a tour to visit Machu Picchu. You do need to understand the ticket system,
which has been rebuilt since 2024 and is now the single thing that determines what you
actually get to see.

**Three circuits, ten routes between them, hourly entry slots, one-way flows, and a hard
daily cap.** Buy the wrong circuit and you will not see the view you came for.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Three circuits** — Panorámico, Clásico, Realeza — bundling **ten routes** in total.
- **Circuit 2 (Clásico)** contains the classic postcard view. This is the one most people
  want.
- **Hourly entry slots**, enforced one-way routes, no re-entry, no backtracking.
- **Daily cap: 4,500**, rising to **5,600** on designated high-season dates.
- **Book as early as possible.** Peak dates and Huayna Picchu add-ons sell out months ahead.

---

## The Ticket System

Since the 2024 restructure, tickets are sold by circuit rather than as general admission.

| Circuit | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **1 — Panorámico** | Upper routes and the high viewpoints, including the Inca Bridge and the Sun Gate on some routes |
| **2 — Clásico** | **The classic view**, plus the urban sector — the route most visitors picture |
| **3 — Realeza** | Lower routes through the royal sector; the least elevation |

**Ten routes are distributed across those three circuits**, each with its own entry time and
fixed one-way path.

**Capacity for 2026:** 4,500 per day on ordinary dates, rising to **5,600** on designated
high-season dates — 1 January, 2–5 April, **19 June–2 November**, and 30–31 December.

**What this means in practice:**

- **Buy Circuit 2** unless you have a specific reason not to. It has the view.
- **You cannot wander.** Routes are one-way and monitored; you cannot double back for a photo.
- **You cannot combine circuits** on one ticket. Two circuits means two tickets on two entries,
  usually two days.
- **Add-ons** — Huayna Picchu and Machu Picchu Mountain — attach to specific circuits and have
  their own tiny quotas. These sell out first, often months ahead.

**Buy from the official channel.** The resale market is large and marks up heavily.

## Getting There

**The route:** Cusco → Ollantaytambo (by road, ~2 hours) → Aguas Calientes (by train, ~1.5
hours) → the site (by bus, 30 minutes, or on foot, 1.5 hours uphill).

**Trains** are run by two operators and are the only practical way in. **Book ahead**; they
sell out and prices vary considerably by service level and time.

**The bus** from Aguas Calientes runs frequently from early morning. The queue at 5am is long
and real. Walking up is free, steep, and takes about 90 minutes.

**Altitude:** Cusco is at 3,400 m, considerably higher than Machu Picchu at 2,430 m.
**Acclimatise in Cusco or the Sacred Valley for two days before** — the Sacred Valley is
lower and is the better place to do it.

## The Inca Trail and the Alternatives

**The classic Inca Trail** — four days, ending at the Sun Gate. **Permits are capped and must
be booked through a licensed operator**, typically **five to six months ahead**, and it is
closed each February for maintenance. You cannot walk it independently.

**Alternatives when permits are gone:**

- **Salkantay** — five days, higher and harder, no permit cap, arguably more spectacular
  scenery.
- **Lares** — gentler, more cultural, communities rather than ruins.
- **The short Inca Trail** — one or two days, entering at km104 with the Sun Gate arrival,
  and far easier to secure.

## Practical Rules at the Site

- **No re-entry.** Once out, out. Use the toilets before entering — there are none inside.
- **No large backpacks**, tripods, drones or selfie sticks.
- **Bring water and a snack** discreetly; food is officially restricted.
- **A guide is required for some circuits** and strongly worth it regardless — the site is
  substantially more interesting explained.
- **Rain is likely** in any season. Bring a poncho; umbrellas are restricted.
- **Passport required** for entry, and get it stamped at the exit if you want the souvenir.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which Machu Picchu circuit should I buy?**
Circuit 2, the Clásico, for most visitors — it contains the classic postcard view along with
the urban sector. Circuit 1 covers the upper viewpoints and Circuit 3 the lower royal sector.
You cannot combine circuits on one ticket, and routes are one-way with no backtracking.

**Can you visit Machu Picchu without a tour?**
Yes. Book the entry ticket through the official channel, the train to Aguas Calientes, and
the bus up, and you are done. It costs roughly $300–$500 per person for a two-day independent
trip against $600–$1,000 for a packaged equivalent. Note that some circuits require a guide.

**How far in advance should you book Machu Picchu?**
As early as you can. Daily capacity is capped at 4,500, rising to 5,600 on designated
high-season dates, and Huayna Picchu add-ons have small separate quotas that sell out months
ahead. Classic Inca Trail permits typically need booking five to six months in advance.

**Do you need to acclimatise before Machu Picchu?**
Yes, but for Cusco rather than the site itself — Cusco sits at 3,400 m against Machu Picchu's
2,430 m. Spend two days acclimatising first, ideally in the Sacred Valley, which is lower than
Cusco.

---

## The Bottom Line

Buy Circuit 2 as early as you can through the official channel, acclimatise in the Sacred
Valley rather than Cusco, and skip the tour package — the independent version is cheaper and
identical.
