# First Time in India: What to Know Before You Go

*By [Author] · [Date] · 9 min read*

India is the destination people most often love and most often find harder than they
expected, and the two are related. The intensity that makes it worth going is the same
intensity that makes the first three days difficult.

The single most useful piece of advice for a first trip is to attempt far less than you
think you can.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Go slower and smaller.** Three places in two weeks, not eight.
- **The Golden Triangle** — Delhi, Agra, Jaipur — is the standard first trip for good reason.
- **Rajasthan or Kerala** are the two best second legs, and they are very different countries.
- **Most nationalities need an e-visa**, applied for in advance online.
- **Trains are excellent and book out weeks ahead.**

---

## Set Expectations Honestly

**The first 48 hours are the hardest.** Noise, crowds, traffic, and constant attention if you
look foreign. This is normal, it is not a sign the trip was a mistake, and it passes.

**What genuinely helps:**

- **Land somewhere gentle.** Arriving into Delhi and going straight to Old Delhi on day one is
  a choice. Kerala, Udaipur or Rishikesh are far softer landings.
- **Book the first two nights somewhere comfortable**, and pre-arrange the airport pickup.
- **Give yourself a slow first day.** Jetlag plus sensory intensity is a poor combination.
- **Accept that you will be quoted tourist prices.** Negotiating is normal and expected; being
  angry about it is not useful.

---

## Where to Go First

### The Golden Triangle — 7–10 days

**Delhi** (2–3 days) — Old Delhi and Jama Masjid, Humayun's Tomb, Qutub Minar, and Lodhi
Gardens for the calm.

**Agra** (1–2 days) — the Taj Mahal at sunrise, which is the correct time and not a cliché.
Agra Fort is genuinely worth the extra half day; most people skip it.

**Jaipur** (2–3 days) — Amber Fort, the City Palace, Hawa Mahal, and the best shopping of the
three.

**Connected by train and by good roads**, and the standard first trip.

### Then pick one

**Rajasthan** — Udaipur (lakes, the softest city in the state), Jodhpur (the blue city),
Jaisalmer (desert fort). Add a week.

**Kerala** — completely different: backwaters, tea plantations at Munnar, Fort Kochi, ayurveda,
and a far gentler pace. **The best choice for a nervous first-timer.**

**Varanasi** — the most intense place in India and one of the most extraordinary. Not a first
stop; a superb second-week one if the trip is going well.

**Goa or Rishikesh** — good decompression stops rather than destinations in themselves.

**What not to do:** north and south in two weeks. India is the size of Europe and internal
flights eat days.

## Practicalities

**Visa** — most nationalities need an **e-visa**, applied for in advance through the official
Indian government portal. Terms, durations and eligible nationalities change; check the
current official position and use the government site, not the many lookalike agencies.

**Trains** — the network is excellent and the classes are clearly graded. **AC2 or AC3 tier**
for overnight journeys. Book weeks ahead through the official system; popular routes fill
fast. A day train in a good class is one of the better experiences in the country.

**Domestic flights** are cheap and save whole days on long hops.

**Money** — ATMs are widespread. Carry small notes; change is a genuine and constant problem.

**Connectivity** — buy a local SIM or eSIM. Coverage is good and data is very cheap.

## Health and Food

**The food is the reason to go** and also the most common source of trouble. Sensible practice:

- **Eat where it is busy and hot.** High turnover, freshly cooked.
- **Vegetarian is the default** in much of the country and is generally the safer choice.
- **Bottled or filtered water only.** Check the seal. Avoid ice early in the trip.
- **Street food is often safer than a mid-range restaurant** — you can see it cooked.
- **Build up gradually.** Your digestive system adjusts; give it a week before the hottest
  things on the menu.

**Before you go:** see a travel clinic well ahead about routine vaccinations, hepatitis A and
typhoid, and malaria prophylaxis depending on where you are going. **Carry rehydration salts.**

## Safety, Honestly

**General:** violent crime against tourists is rare. Scams, overcharging and persistent touts
are common and are the day-to-day reality rather than a danger.

**Common scams:** the "your hotel is closed" taxi diversion, the unsolicited helpful stranger
at a station, gem and carpet schemes, and rickshaws with a mysteriously broken meter.
**Pre-book airport transfers** and use ride apps where available.

**For women travellers:** India requires more caution than most destinations, and this is
well documented rather than a matter of opinion. Practical measures that experienced
travellers consistently recommend: dress conservatively, avoid travelling alone at night,
book women-only train compartments where available, and choose accommodation on location
rather than price. Many women travel India solo and have a superb time; it takes more
planning than Southeast Asia does.

## What It Costs

Per person per day.

| Style | Daily cost |
|---|---|
| Backpacker | $20–$35 |
| Mid-range | $50–$90 |
| Comfortable | $150–$250 |
| Heritage hotels and palaces | $400+ |

India offers among the widest ranges in the world — you can travel very cheaply, and the
top-end heritage properties are extraordinary and still cost less than their European
equivalents.

**Hiring a car with a driver** for a Rajasthan loop runs roughly $50–$90 a day and is standard
practice rather than an indulgence.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is India good for first-time travellers?**
Yes, with realistic expectations and a slower itinerary than you would plan elsewhere. Three
places in two weeks rather than eight. Kerala or Udaipur are far gentler first landings than
Delhi if the intensity concerns you.

**How long should a first trip to India be?**
Two weeks minimum — the Golden Triangle plus one region. Three weeks is considerably more
comfortable. Attempting both north and south in two weeks means spending the trip in airports.

**Do I need a visa for India?**
Most nationalities need an e-visa, applied for in advance through the official Indian
government portal. Terms and eligible nationalities change periodically, so check the current
official position and avoid the many lookalike agency sites.

**Is it safe to travel in India?**
Violent crime against tourists is rare; scams and persistent touting are the everyday
reality. Women travellers should take more precautions than in most destinations — dress
conservatively, avoid travelling alone at night, and use women-only train compartments where
available.

---

## The Bottom Line

Do three places in two weeks, land somewhere gentle, book trains weeks ahead, and give
yourself the first 48 hours to adjust before judging the trip.
