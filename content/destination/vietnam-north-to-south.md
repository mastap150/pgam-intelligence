# Vietnam North to South: The Complete Route, Timing & Transport Guide

*By [Author] · [Date] · 9 min read*

Vietnam is 1,650 km long and narrow, and that shape creates the one planning problem that
matters: **the north, centre and south have different climates, and they are rarely all good
at once.** There is no single best month to visit Vietnam. There is only a best month for
the part you care about most.

This guide covers the route, how to move between regions, and how to time a trip so you are
not in the wrong third of the country in the wrong season.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Direction:** north to south is the conventional flow — Hanoi in, Ho Chi Minh City out.
- **Time needed:** 2 weeks minimum, 3 for comfort.
- **Visa:** most nationalities use the **e-visa at evisa.gov.vn** — up to 90 days, single or
  multiple entry, around $25 and $50 respectively, roughly 3 working days to process.
- **Best compromise months:** **March–April** and **October–November**.
- **Internal flights are cheap** and save days. The train is the experience.

---

## Before You Go: the E-Visa

Vietnam runs an electronic visa system covering most nationalities, applied for online at
the official portal **evisa.gov.vn**.

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| Duration | Up to **90 days** |
| Entry | Single or multiple |
| Cost | Roughly **$25** single, **$50** multiple |
| Processing | About **3 working days** |
| At the border | Carry proof of onward travel and accommodation |

**Two warnings.** First, **apply through the official government portal** — there is a large
ecosystem of lookalike sites charging several times the fee for the same application.
Second, the application is unforgiving about **exact passport details and photo format**; a
mismatch causes rejection or denied boarding.

Some nationalities have visa exemptions for shorter stays and terms change periodically —
check your own country's current position rather than relying on any article, this one
included.

## The Climate Problem

Three regions, three patterns, running on different calendars.

| Region | Dry / best | Wet / difficult |
|---|---|---|
| **North** (Hanoi, Sapa, Ha Long) | Oct–Dec, Mar–Apr | Cold and drizzly Jan–Feb; hot and wet Jun–Aug |
| **Centre** (Hue, Hoi An, Da Nang) | Feb–Aug | **Sept–Nov — typhoon and flood season** |
| **South** (HCMC, Mekong, Phu Quoc) | Dec–Apr | May–Nov wet season, afternoon downpours |

**The conflict:** the centre's worst months are the north's best. September to November is
excellent in Hanoi and genuinely risky in Hoi An, where flooding closes the old town most
years.

**The compromises that work:**

- **March–April** — the best all-round window. Decent everywhere, hot in the south by April.
- **October–November** — excellent north and south, but you are gambling on the centre.
  Build in flexibility.
- **December–February** — great in the south, cold and grey in the north. Fine if the south
  and centre are the priority.

## The Route

### North: Hanoi, Ha Long, Sapa — 5–6 days

**Hanoi** (2–3 days). The old quarter, the street food, the coffee. Denser and older-feeling
than Ho Chi Minh City, and the better city of the two for most visitors.

**Ha Long Bay or Lan Ha Bay** (1–2 nights). Ha Long is spectacular and heavily trafficked.
**Lan Ha Bay**, adjoining it via Cat Ba island, is the same limestone karst with a fraction
of the boats — take it instead.

**Sapa** (2–3 days). Rice terraces and hill-tribe villages in the far north. Overnight train
or bus from Hanoi. Best May–September when the terraces are green and flooded, though that
is the north's wet season — a genuine trade-off.

### Centre: Hue, Hoi An — 4–5 days

**Hue** (1–2 days). The imperial citadel, royal tombs along the river, and the country's most
distinctive regional cuisine.

**The Hai Van Pass** between Hue and Da Nang is one of the great coastal roads. Ride it,
drive it, or take the train, which runs the same coastline.

**Hoi An** (3 days). A preserved trading port, lantern-lit at night, with tailoring, a good
beach nearby and the best food in Vietnam. Very touristed and still worth it. Go early
morning and late evening.

### South: Ho Chi Minh City, Mekong — 4–5 days

**Ho Chi Minh City** (2–3 days). Faster, newer, louder. The War Remnants Museum is
confronting and essential. Cu Chi tunnels as a half-day.

**The Mekong Delta** (1–2 days). Skip the day trip from HCMC — it is a coach and a
performance. Stay a night in **Can Tho** and see the floating markets at dawn, which is when
they actually happen.

**Optional endings:** **Phu Quoc** for beaches, or **Da Lat** in the highlands for cool air
and coffee farms.

## Getting Between Regions

| Mode | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Domestic flights** | Saving days | 2 hours end to end, often $40–$90. The pragmatic choice |
| **Reunification Express train** | The experience | Hanoi–HCMC is ~35 hours total; do it in legs |
| **The Da Nang–Hue leg specifically** | Everyone | ~3 hours along the coast. The best short train ride in Southeast Asia |
| **Sleeper buses** | Budget | Cheap, widespread, variable comfort |
| **Motorbike, end to end** | Experienced riders only | Popular and genuinely risky — see below |

**The single best transport decision:** fly the long legs, take the train for the
Hue–Da Nang coastal stretch, and use Grab within cities.

**On motorbikes:** riding Vietnam north to south is a well-established backpacker trip and
also a leading cause of serious injury to travellers. Most travel insurance will not cover a
motorbike accident without the correct licence category. Check the policy wording before you
buy the bike.

## What It Costs

Per person per day.

| Style | Daily cost | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Backpacker | $25–$40 | Hostels, street food, sleeper buses |
| Mid-range | $50–$90 | Good hotels, restaurants, internal flights, some tours |
| Comfortable | $150+ | Boutique hotels, private guides, cruises |

Vietnam is among the best-value countries in Southeast Asia. **Street food at $2–$4 a meal is
consistently better than restaurant food at ten times that.**

**Budget separately for:** internal flights, a Ha Long or Lan Ha cruise ($100–$250 for two
days), and tailoring in Hoi An if you intend to.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long do you need in Vietnam?**
Two weeks covers the north, centre and south at a brisk pace with internal flights. Three
weeks lets you take the train for the good stretches, add Sapa or the Mekong properly, and
stop moving every second day.

**Do I need a visa for Vietnam?**
Most nationalities need one, and the standard route is the e-visa applied for at the official
portal evisa.gov.vn — up to 90 days, single or multiple entry, roughly $25 or $50, processed
in about three working days. Some nationalities have exemptions for shorter stays, and terms
change; check your own country's current rules.

**What is the best time to visit Vietnam?**
March–April or October–November are the best compromises, because the country's three regions
have different climates. Note that September to November is typhoon and flood season in the
centre, which is when Hoi An is most at risk, even though those months are excellent in the
north.

**Should you travel Vietnam north to south or south to north?**
Either works. North to south is more common — it ends in Ho Chi Minh City, which has better
onward connections to Cambodia and the Mekong — and it means starting with Hanoi, which most
people find the more rewarding city.

---

## The Bottom Line

Give it two weeks minimum, fly the long legs and take the train from Hue to Da Nang, and pick
March–April or October–November while accepting that no month is good everywhere at once.
