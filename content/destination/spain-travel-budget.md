# How Much Does It Cost to Travel Spain? A Realistic Budget Breakdown

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

Spain is one of the better-value countries in Western Europe, and the gap between what a
careless trip costs and a well-organised one costs is larger here than almost anywhere — not
because of accommodation, but because of how you eat.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Budget:** €50–€75/day. **Mid-range:** €110–€170. **Comfortable:** €250+.
- **The menú del día** — a set lunch, €12–€18 with wine and coffee — is the single biggest
  lever on a Spain budget.
- **Regional variation is large.** Barcelona and San Sebastián cost far more than Andalusia
  or Galicia.
- **Trains are excellent and worth booking early**; advance AVE fares are a fraction of
  walk-up prices.
- **Some regions charge a tourist tax**, Catalonia and the Balearics most notably.

## The Food Lever

This is where a Spain budget is won or lost.

**The menú del día** is a legally-rooted institution: a set lunch, typically two courses plus
bread, wine or water, and often coffee, for **€12–€18**. It is served on weekdays at
essentially every ordinary restaurant, and it is the same kitchen that charges three times as
much at dinner.

**Eat your main meal at lunch.** Do that consistently and your food spend roughly halves,
which on a mid-range trip is €15–€25 a day.

**Other habits that matter:**

- **Stand at the bar.** Many bars charge less at the bar than at a table, and less again than
  on the terrace. The price list usually shows all three.
- **In Granada**, a free tapa still comes with each drink. It is genuinely possible to eat an
  evening meal on three drinks.
- **Avoid restaurants with photographs on the menu** and anyone touting outside. Standard
  advice, unusually true in Spain's tourist centres.
- **Supermarket jamón, cheese and bread** are excellent and cheap.

## Transport

**Trains:** the AVE high-speed network is excellent. **Book early** — advance fares are
dramatically cheaper than walk-up, and competition on the Madrid–Barcelona corridor from
newer operators has pushed prices down further.

**Buses:** slower, notably cheaper, and comprehensive. Worth it for shorter hops and rural
routes.

**Flights:** domestic budget flights compete with trains on longer routes; count the transfers
before assuming they win.

**Car hire:** only worth it for Andalusian white villages, rural Galicia, or the Pyrenees.
Cities are a liability.

## When You Go Changes What It Costs

| Period | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Apr–May, Sept–Oct** | Moderate | **Best value and best weather** |
| Jun | Rising | Good |
| Jul–Aug | Highest on the coast | Inland Andalusia hits 40 °C+ and is cheap for that reason |
| Nov–Mar | Lowest | Cities excellent; coast largely shut |

**Note the inversion:** in August the coast is expensive and packed while Seville and Córdoba
are cheap and nearly empty — because they are genuinely too hot.

---

## Tourist Taxes

Not national. Applied regionally:

- **Catalonia** — a regional tourist tax plus a Barcelona city surcharge, varying by
  accommodation class.
- **Balearics** — a sustainable tourism tax, seasonally varied.
- Most other regions currently charge nothing, though several have debated introducing it.

Check the specific region before budgeting; the amounts are small but they are per person,
per night.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much money do you need per day in Spain?**
Roughly €50–€75 per person per day travelling on a budget, €110–€170 mid-range, and €250+ for
comfortable travel. Andalusia and Galicia run well below those figures; Barcelona, San
Sebastián and the Balearics in summer run above them.

**What is the menú del día?**
A set weekday lunch — usually two courses with bread, wine or water and often coffee — for
about €12–€18. It is served at ordinary restaurants across Spain and is the single most
effective way to cut a food budget, because it is the same kitchen charging far more at
dinner.

**Is Spain expensive compared to the rest of Europe?**
No. It is among the better-value countries in Western Europe, particularly for food and rail
travel. Barcelona and the Basque Country are the exceptions and sit closer to French or
Italian pricing.

**Do you pay tourist tax in Spain?**
In some regions. Catalonia charges a regional tax plus a Barcelona city surcharge, and the
Balearics apply a seasonal sustainable tourism tax. Most other regions currently do not,
though several have debated it.

---

## The Bottom Line

Eat your main meal at lunch on the menú del día, book AVE tickets early, and travel in spring
or autumn — those three decisions do more for a Spain budget than anything else.
