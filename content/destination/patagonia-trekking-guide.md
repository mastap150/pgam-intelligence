# Patagonia Trekking Guide: W Trek vs O Trek — Which Route Is Right for You?

*By [Author] · [Date] · 10 min read*

Torres del Paine is not a park you can turn up and walk. Accommodation inside it is limited,
run by two private operators plus the park authority, and **you must hold confirmed bookings
for every night before you are allowed on the trail.** The most popular dates sell out
months before the season opens.

So the real question is not just W or O. It is whether you can still get the beds.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **The W:** roughly 4 nights (3 at a push), the three famous valleys, most popular.
- **The O (Circuit):** at least 7 nights, includes the whole W plus the remote back side.
- **You must book every night in advance.** No booking, no entry.
- **Two operators inside the park:** Vertice (Paine Grande, Grey, Dickson, Los Perros) and
  Las Torres (Central, Chileno, Cuernos, Francés, Serón).
- **Season:** roughly October to April. December–February is peak.

---

## The Booking Problem — Solve This First

This is the part that catches people, and it is worth understanding before you choose a
route.

**How it works:**

- Accommodation inside the park is run by **Vertice Patagonia** and **Las Torres
  Patagonia**, each with their own booking systems, plus CONAF-run free campsites on some
  sections.
- **The full W requires 4 nights' accommodation** (3 is possible with long days). **The O
  Circuit requires at least 7.**
- Every night must be confirmed. Rangers check.
- **Beds in refugios are the scarcest resource.** Camping with your own tent, or renting a
  pre-pitched tent, opens up availability considerably.
- Aggregator sites now show real-time availability across both operators in one place,
  which makes building a viable chain far less painful than booking each separately.

**When to book:** reservations for a season typically open around the middle of the
preceding year. Popular dates sell out months before the season begins.

**If everything is booked:** take a guided trip — operators hold allocations — or walk the
W as day hikes from outside the park, or go in the shoulder months of October and April.

## The W Trek

**Shape:** a W-shaped route along the southern face of the massif, hitting three valleys —
Grey Glacier in the west, the French Valley in the middle, and the Torres themselves in the
east.

**Length:** 4 nights / 5 days typically; 3 nights with long days.

**Difficulty:** moderate. Days run 6–9 hours with meaningful ascent, particularly the final
climb to the Torres viewpoint, but there is no technical ground and you can sleep indoors
and eat cooked meals throughout.

**Direction:** **east to west** (Torres first) or **west to east** (Grey first). West-to-east
saves the Torres for the finish and tends to have better light on the towers at sunrise from
Chileno. East-to-west gets the hardest climb done while you are freshest.

**The three highlights:**

1. **Base of the Torres** — the three granite towers above a glacial lake. The climb is
   steep and the final section is boulder scrambling. Sunrise is famous and requires a
   pre-dawn start from Chileno.
2. **French Valley** — a hanging glacier that calves audibly across the valley. The upper
   viewpoint at Británico is worth the extra time if weather allows.
3. **Grey Glacier** — the glacier face and icebergs on the lake. Boat options exist.

## The O Circuit

**Shape:** the full loop around the massif — the entire W plus the remote northern back
side.

**Length:** 7–9 nights.

**Difficulty:** significantly harder. The **John Garner Pass** is the crux: a steep climb
in exposed, frequently brutal weather, with a long descent to Grey.

**Direction:** **anticlockwise only.** The back section is one-way by park rule.

**Why do it:** the back side is genuinely empty. You go from the W's queues to seeing a
handful of people a day, and the descent from the pass toward the Grey glacier is one of the
great views in South America.

**Who shouldn't:** anyone not comfortable with multi-day walking in poor weather, or
carrying more gear — the back-side huts are more basic and further apart.

## W or O?

| | W Trek | O Circuit |
|---|---|---|
| Nights | 4 (3 possible) | 7–9 |
| Difficulty | Moderate | Hard |
| Crowds | Busy | Busy on the W section, empty behind |
| Booking difficulty | Very hard in peak | Hard, but less competition |
| Refugio comfort | Good throughout | Basic on the back side |
| Best for | First-time Patagonia, limited time | Experienced walkers with 10+ days |

**Pick the W if** you have under a week in the park, want beds and cooked meals, or this is
your first multi-day trek. **Pick the O if** you have the time, the fitness and the
tolerance for weather — and book earlier, because it needs seven consecutive nights to line
up.

## The Weather Is the Real Difficulty

Patagonia's difficulty is not gradient. It is wind.

- **Gusts regularly exceed 100 km/h** in the mountains, and can knock a walker over on
  exposed sections.
- **Four seasons in a day is literal.** Sun, sleet, and back to sun within an hour.
- **Waterproofs must actually be waterproof.** A shower-resistant jacket will fail.
- **Trekking poles are close to essential** — as much for wind stability as for knees.
- **Weather cancels things.** The Britanico viewpoint and the Torres viewpoint both close in
  high wind. Build slack into the itinerary.

**Season by month:**

| Period | Conditions |
|---|---|
| Oct–Nov | Spring — fewer people, colder, less wind. Underrated |
| **Dec–Feb** | Summer — warmest, longest days, **windiest**, busiest, most expensive |
| **Mar–Apr** | Autumn — colour, less wind, thinning crowds. **Arguably the best window** |
| May–Sep | Winter — most services closed; limited guided options only |

**March is the insider answer:** the wind drops noticeably, the crowds go home, and the
lenga forests turn red.

## What It Costs

Per person, excluding international flights.

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Park entrance | Roughly $35–$50 for multi-day foreign visitors |
| Refugio bed, half board | $100–$180 per night |
| Camping, own tent | $10–$25 per night |
| Pre-pitched tent rental | $40–$80 per night |
| Meals if not half board | $20–$40 per day |
| Guided W trek | $1,500–$3,000 |
| Guided O circuit | $2,500–$4,500 |

**Puerto Natales** is the base town — hostels, gear rental and supermarkets. Buses run to
the park in about two hours.

**The big saving is camping.** Carrying a tent roughly halves the accommodation cost and
substantially improves availability.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do you need to book Torres del Paine in advance?**
Yes, and this is non-negotiable. You must hold confirmed accommodation for every night of
your trek before being allowed on the trail, and rangers check. Reservations for a season
typically open around the middle of the preceding year, and popular dates sell out months
before the season starts.

**What is the difference between the W and the O in Torres del Paine?**
The W is a roughly 4-night route along the southern face covering the three main valleys —
Grey Glacier, the French Valley and the Torres. The O Circuit includes the entire W plus the
remote northern back side, needs at least 7 nights, must be walked anticlockwise, and crosses
the demanding John Garner Pass.

**How hard is the W Trek?**
Moderate. Days run 6 to 9 hours with real ascent, particularly the final climb to the Torres
viewpoint, but nothing is technical and you can sleep in refugios with cooked meals
throughout. The genuine difficulty is the weather — wind gusts regularly exceed 100 km/h.

**When is the best time to trek in Patagonia?**
March and early April. The wind drops noticeably from the summer peak, the crowds thin, and
the forests turn. December to February has the longest days and warmest temperatures but the
worst wind, the biggest crowds and the highest prices.

---

## The Bottom Line

Book your beds the moment the season opens or plan to camp, choose the W unless you have ten
days and real hill fitness, and go in March.
