# Tour de France 2027: The Grand Départ Comes to Britain

*By [Author] · [Date] · 9 min read*

For three days in July 2027, the biggest bike race in the world will not be in France. The
Tour de France 2027 starts in Edinburgh on Friday 2 July and spends its opening weekend
crossing Scotland, England and Wales before the race transfers to the continent. It is the
first time the men's Tour and the Tour de France Femmes have both launched from the same
country outside France in the same year.

That makes 2027 the most accessible Tour in a generation for anyone in Britain, Ireland or
North America — and it creates a planning problem, because most of the route is still
unknown. Here is what has been confirmed, what lands in October, and how to decide when to
commit money.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Men's Grand Départ:** Edinburgh, Friday 2 July 2027. Three UK stages, finishing in
  Cardiff on Sunday 4 July.
- **Tour de France Femmes:** Starts in Leeds, Friday 30 July 2027. Finishes in London.
- **The rest of the route:** Announced at the Palais des Congrès in Paris in late October
  2026. Alps, Pyrenees and a Paris finish are expected but not confirmed.
- **Watching roadside is free.** It has always been free. You do not need a ticket.
- **Book accommodation now if you want a UK stage town.** Book tours after October if you
  want a French stage.

## What's Actually Been Announced — and What Hasn't

This is the part most coverage gets muddled, so be precise about it.

**Confirmed:** the three opening stages in Britain, for both the men's and women's races.
Host towns, dates and broad routing are locked in. Local councils along the way — Midlothian,
Scottish Borders, Rhondda Cynon Taf, Merthyr Tydfil — have already begun announcing their
involvement.

**Not confirmed:** everything after the Channel. Stages 4 through 21 of the men's race, the
mountain days, the time trials, the finish. ASO publishes the full route at a presentation
in Paris at the end of October each year, and 2027 follows that pattern. Expect traditional
Alpine and Pyrenean blocks and a Paris finale, because that is the shape of nearly every
modern Tour — but "expect" is doing real work in that sentence. Nobody selling you a French
stage package today knows which mountain you will be standing on.

That gap between now and late October is the single most important planning fact in this
article. It determines what you can safely book.

## The 2027 Grand Départ, Stage by Stage

### Stage 1 — Edinburgh to Carlisle (184 km), Friday 2 July

A rural, hilly opener rather than the flat sprint finish Grand Départs usually serve up.
184 km from the Scottish capital down through the Borders into Cumbria. A lumpy first day
tends to produce an aggressive race — the sprinters' teams cannot control it as easily, and
the yellow jersey often lands on someone unexpected.

For spectators, the Borders section is the interesting call. Edinburgh will be
overwhelmed; the crowds thin considerably once the race is an hour south, and the roads
through the Borders offer the kind of hedgerow-and-hillside viewing that photographs far
better than a city-centre barrier.

### Stage 2 — Keswick to Lancaster, Saturday 3 July

Starting in the heart of the Lake District and running down the west coast. The Lakes are
the most scenically dramatic terrain the race will touch in Britain, and Keswick on a
Saturday morning in July will be busy regardless of a bike race. If you want this stage,
accommodation is the constraint, not access — the Lake District books out for ordinary
summer weekends, let alone this one.

### Stage 3 — Welshpool to Cardiff, Sunday 4 July

Mid-Wales to the capital, over the Côte de Rhigos and the Côte de Caerffili. These are the
two named climbs of the British section and therefore the best free viewing on the whole
Grand Départ. Categorised climbs are where the race slows, splinters and becomes legible to
a roadside spectator — on the flat, the peloton is a 12-second blur.

If you only watch one UK stage, make it this one, and stand on a climb.

### The Tour de France Femmes, 30 July–early August

A separate race, four weeks later, with its own British opening: Leeds to Manchester across
the Pennines, then back to Sheffield through the Peak District climbs, and a showpiece
finish in London. The Peak District stage is the one to target — same logic as Caerphilly.
The Femmes race also runs smaller crowds than the men's, which in practice means you can
get to the barriers an hour before the race rather than four.

## Britain or France? An Honest Comparison

The romantic answer is France. The practical answer, for 2027, is more interesting.

**Watch in Britain if** you are already in the UK or Ireland, you want to see the race
without a flight, or you want the novelty — a home Grand Départ happens roughly once a
decade, and the last British one (Yorkshire, 2014) is still talked about for the crowds it
drew. Costs are lower, logistics are simpler, and you can watch three consecutive stages
without moving more than 300 miles.

**Watch in France if** you want mountains. This is the real trade-off. The British stages
are hilly; they are not the Tour. The images that made you want to go — switchbacks packed
ten deep, riders climbing at walking pace through a corridor of noise — happen on Alpe
d'Huez and the Tourmalet, not the Côte de Caerffili. If the mountains are the point, the
UK stages are a warm-up act.

**The honest recommendation:** if you can only do one and you have never seen the race
live, go to a mountain stage in France. If you are in Britain, do the Grand Départ too — it
is three days of the world's biggest race on your own roads, and it is free.

## How to Actually See a Stage

Roadside spectating is free and unticketed. That is the single best thing about this sport
and it surprises people every time.

**The practical version:**

1. **Pick a climb, not a flat section.** On a categorised climb the race passes at 12–20
   km/h and lasts several minutes. On the flat it is gone in seconds.
2. **Arrive early — much earlier than feels reasonable.** Roads close 2–4 hours before the
   race. On a famous mountain, people camp for days. For a British stage, 3–4 hours before
   the expected passing time is sensible for a climb; less for a rural section.
3. **The publicity caravan comes first**, roughly two hours ahead of the riders. It is a
   procession of sponsor floats throwing merchandise into the crowd. Children love it. It
   is also your signal that the road is properly closed and the wait is nearly over.
4. **Stand on the outside of a corner near the top of a climb.** Best sightlines, longest
   view, and the riders are at their most spread out.
5. **Do not run alongside the riders.** Do not hold a flag over the road. Do not step back
   into the road to photograph the rider behind. Every year someone causes a crash this way.

**Getting to a stage without a car:** the UK stages are unusually well served — Edinburgh,
Carlisle, Lancaster, Cardiff and Leeds all have mainline stations. Book rail now. Rail
capacity, not road access, will be the binding constraint on the British Grand Départ, and
advance fares for July 2027 will be released roughly 12 weeks ahead.

## Guided Tours: What They Cost and Who They're For

You do not need a tour to watch the Tour. You need one if you want to *ride* the route —
the same climbs, closed or quiet roads, a mechanic, bikes waiting for you, and access to
areas the public cannot reach.

This is a genuinely expensive category and it is worth being blunt about the numbers.
**EF Adventures**, which runs the official travel programme tied to the EF Pro Cycling team,
prices its 2026 Tour de France itineraries from **$9,499 per person**. That buys 4- and
5-star hotels, a Cannondale road bike or e-bike, two tour directors, transfers, and VIP
access as guests of the team — stage-finish viewpoints inside the barriers, the Stage 1
starting village, the team's end-of-tour party. Eight itineraries ran in 2026 and two sold
out.

For 2027, itineraries have not been released. EF is holding places against a **$1,000 fully
refundable deposit**, with a 10-day window to review and withdraw once the routes are
published. In practice that is a hedge against the October announcement: you reserve a spot
before the route is known, then decide when it is.

**Who this is for:** riders, not spectators. If you want to climb Ventoux on a good bike
with your luggage moved for you and a mechanic on hand, this is what that costs. If you
want to stand at a barrier and watch a bike race, you are paying roughly $9,500 for a hotel
booking you could make yourself.

**Who it isn't for:** anyone whose main goal is seeing the race. Roadside is free. A
self-organised week in the Alps — rental car, gîte, your own bike or a local hire — runs
$1,500–$3,000 per person and puts you on the same mountains.

## What to Bring Roadside

Not a generic packing list — this is a specific day.

- **Layers and a waterproof.** July in the Lake District and the Borders is not July in
  Provence. You will be stationary for hours.
- **More water and food than you think.** Roads are closed; nothing is open; you cannot
  leave and come back.
- **A power bank.** Cell networks collapse under crowd load at the finish. Download your
  maps and tickets in advance.
- **Cash.** Same reason.
- **A folding stool** if you are committing to a climb for four hours.
- **Sunscreen**, which sounds absurd in Cumbria until it isn't.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a ticket to watch the Tour de France 2027?**
No. Roadside viewing is free and unticketed along the entire route, in Britain and in
France. Tickets only exist for hospitality areas — VIP enclosures at stage finishes, which
are sold through official partners and tour operators.

**When will the full Tour de France 2027 route be announced?**
Late October 2026, at a presentation at the Palais des Congrès in Paris. The three British
opening stages are already confirmed; everything after them is announced then.

**Where's the best place to watch the British stages?**
A categorised climb on Stage 3 — the Côte de Rhigos or Côte de Caerffili, between Welshpool
and Cardiff. The race is slowest and most spread out on a climb, and these are the only
named climbs of the UK section.

**Is the Tour de France Femmes worth travelling for?**
Yes, and it is easier. It starts in Leeds on 30 July 2027 and finishes in London, draws
smaller crowds than the men's race, and the Peak District stage offers the same climb-side
viewing with a fraction of the queueing.

## The Bottom Line

The 2027 Grand Départ is the most accessible Tour de France in years for an
English-speaking audience, and the British stages are free to watch, well served by rail
and confirmed enough to book around today. For anything on French soil, wait for the
October route announcement before you spend a pound.
