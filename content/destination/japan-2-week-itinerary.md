# The Best 2-Week Japan Itinerary — Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka & Beyond

*By [Author] · [Date] · 10 min read*

Two weeks is the right length for a first trip to Japan, and the itinerary below is built
around a specific financial fact: **the 7-day Japan Rail Pass costs ¥50,000 and only pays
for itself if you go west of Kyoto.** A Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka loop does not earn it back. Add
Hiroshima and it does.

**Prices rise on 1 October 2026** — the nationwide pass bought through overseas agencies
goes from ¥50,000 to ¥53,000 — so if you are booking now, buy before then.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Route:** Tokyo (4) → Hakone (1) → Kyoto (4) → Osaka (2) → Hiroshima & Miyajima (2) →
  Tokyo (1).
- **JR Pass:** ¥50,000 for 7 days, ¥80,000 for 14, ¥100,000 for 21 (Ordinary). Rising
  1 Oct 2026.
- **Activate the 7-day pass on day 5**, not day 1. The long legs are in the second week.
- **Best months:** late March–May, and October–November.
- **Budget:** ¥15,000–¥25,000 (about $100–$170) per person per day, mid-range.

---

## The JR Pass Maths — Read This First

The pass stopped being an automatic purchase after its 2023 price rise, and most itineraries
online still assume otherwise.

| Pass | 2026 price (Ordinary) |
|---|---|
| 7 days | ¥50,000 → **¥53,000 from 1 Oct 2026** |
| 14 days | ¥80,000 |
| 21 days | ¥100,000 |

**The break-even test:** a Tokyo–Osaka round trip in single tickets is roughly
¥29,000–¥37,000. That loses against a ¥50,000 pass. Add Hiroshima or Hakata and the long
legs push you well past it.

**So:**

- **Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka only?** Skip the pass. Buy singles.
- **Adding Hiroshima or further west?** The 7-day pass wins.
- **Two weeks?** The **7-day pass, activated for the second week**, almost always beats the
  14-day pass — the first week is city travel on metros the pass doesn't cover anyway.

That last point is the single most useful thing in this article and the itinerary is built
around it.

## The Itinerary

### Days 1–4: Tokyo

Land at Haneda if you can — it is far closer to the centre than Narita.

- **Day 1:** Arrive, stay awake. Shibuya and Shinjuku in the evening.
- **Day 2:** Asakusa and Senso-ji early, Ueno, then Akihabara.
- **Day 3:** Meiji Jingu, Harajuku, Omotesando, Shimokitazawa in the evening.
- **Day 4:** Tsukiji outer market in the morning, teamLab, then Shimbashi or Ebisu for
  yakitori under the tracks.

**Stay:** Shinjuku or Shibuya for first-timers. Yanaka or Kuramae if you want quieter.

### Day 5: Hakone

Activate the JR Pass here if you are following the plan. Hakone is the classic Fuji-view
detour — a loop of mountain railway, ropeway, pirate ship across Lake Ashi, and an onsen at
the end.

**Fuji is visible about half the time.** Winter has the best odds; summer the worst.

**If you want to climb Fuji instead:** the season is short and the rules are now strict.
There is a **¥4,000 permit per climber**, daily caps, and mandatory online pre-registration
for a QR code before you can pass the gate. It is a July–early September activity and needs
booking well ahead.

### Days 6–9: Kyoto

- **Day 6:** Fushimi Inari at 7am — genuinely early, it is unbearable later. Then Tofuku-ji
  and the southern temples.
- **Day 7:** Arashiyama early for the bamboo, the monkey park, then Kinkaku-ji.
- **Day 8:** Higashiyama — Kiyomizu-dera, the Philosopher's Path, Ginkaku-ji. Gion in the
  evening.
- **Day 9:** **Nara day trip** (45 minutes, JR Pass covers it). Todai-ji and the deer.

**The one rule for Kyoto:** start at dawn. Every major site is transformed by arriving
before 8am, and there is no other trick that works as well.

**Stay:** near Kyoto Station for transport, or Gion for atmosphere at a price.

### Days 10–11: Osaka

The counterweight to Kyoto — loud, modern, and the best eating in Japan.

- **Day 10:** Dotonbori, Shinsekai, Kuromon market. Okonomiyaki and kushikatsu.
- **Day 11:** Osaka Castle, then **Himeji Castle** on the way west (JR Pass covers it) —
  the finest surviving castle in Japan and better than Osaka's reconstruction.

### Days 12–13: Hiroshima and Miyajima

This is the leg that earns the pass back.

- **Day 12:** Peace Memorial Park and Museum. Give it the full morning; it is not a quick
  visit.
- **Day 13:** **Miyajima** — the floating torii, and the walk or ropeway up Mount Misen.
  Stay overnight on the island if you can; it empties completely after the day-trippers
  leave.

### Day 14: Back to Tokyo

Four hours on the shinkansen from Hiroshima. Last night near the airport, or one more
evening in the city.

## What to Change, and When

**If you are going in winter:** swap Hiroshima for the **Japanese Alps** — Takayama,
Shirakawa-go and Kanazawa. Snow country is at its best and the pass covers most of it.

**If it is cherry blossom season:** add days, not destinations. Everything takes longer and
you will want time to sit in parks.

**If you have three weeks:** add Kanazawa and Takayama between Tokyo and Kyoto, or Kyushu
beyond Hiroshima.

**If you have ten days:** cut Osaka to a day trip from Kyoto and drop Hakone.

## When to Go

| Period | Conditions | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Late Mar–early Apr | Cherry blossom; crowded and expensive | Spectacular, book far ahead |
| Late Apr–May | Warm, dry, green. **Golden Week** (late Apr–early May) is a domestic travel peak | **Best overall**, avoid Golden Week |
| Jun–mid Jul | Rainy season, humid | Cheapest, genuinely wet |
| Late Jul–Aug | Hot and very humid; Fuji climbing season | Mountains only |
| Sept | Typhoon risk, still humid | Improving through the month |
| **Oct–Nov** | Dry, mild, autumn colour | **Best overall, joint with May** |
| Dec–Feb | Cold, dry, clear. Best Fuji visibility | Excellent value; snow country |

## What It Costs

Per person per day, excluding international flights.

| Style | Daily cost | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | ¥8,000–¥12,000 (~$55–$80) | Hostels, convenience-store and chain-restaurant meals |
| Mid-range | ¥15,000–¥25,000 (~$100–$170) | Business hotels, restaurant meals, one ryokan night |
| Comfortable | ¥35,000+ (~$235+) | Good hotels, kaiseki, ryokan with private onsen |

**Add:** the JR Pass, and roughly ¥1,000–¥2,000/day in city metro fares the pass does not
cover.

**Japan is cheaper than people expect** for food and transport, and more expensive than
expected for accommodation. A superb bowl of ramen is ¥1,000. A mediocre hotel room in
Tokyo is ¥15,000.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the Japan Rail Pass worth it in 2026?**
Only if you travel west of Kyoto. The 7-day Ordinary pass is ¥50,000, rising to ¥53,000 from
1 October 2026, and a Tokyo–Osaka round trip in single tickets runs roughly ¥29,000–¥37,000
— so the classic Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka loop loses money on the pass. Adding Hiroshima or Hakata
pushes you past break-even.

**How many days do you need in Japan?**
Two weeks is the sweet spot for a first trip: four days in Tokyo, four in Kyoto, and enough
left for Osaka, Hiroshima and one mountain or onsen stop. Ten days works if you cut Hakone
and treat Osaka as a day trip.

**Do you need a permit to climb Mount Fuji?**
Yes. A ¥4,000 per-person climbing fee applies, daily caps are in force, and climbers must
pre-register online to obtain a permit QR code before entering the mountain. The season runs
roughly July to early September.

**What is the best time to visit Japan?**
October and November for dry, mild weather and autumn colour, or late April into May —
avoiding Golden Week, when domestic travel peaks. Cherry blossom in late March and early
April is spectacular but crowded and expensive.
