# The Best Time to Visit Italy — Month-by-Month Guide for Every Region

*By [Author] · [Date] · 9 min read*

Italy is not one climate. In April you can ski in the Dolomites and swim in Sicily on the
same day, and the best time to visit Italy depends far more on which Italy you mean than on
any general rule.

This guide breaks it down by month and by region, and covers the two practical things that
now shape timing as much as weather: Venice's access fee, and the fact that much of the
country closes in August.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Best overall: late April to early June, and mid-September to late October.**
- **Worst overall: August** — hottest, most crowded at the coast, and much of urban Italy
  shut for *Ferragosto*.
- **Venice charged a day-tripper access fee on selected dates in 2026** (3 April–26 July).
  The trial has ended; 2027 dates are not yet announced.
- **The south runs two months ahead** of the north. Sicily in April is Tuscany in June.

---

## The Two Things That Actually Decide Your Trip

### Ferragosto and the August shutdown

Italians take their holiday in August, centred on **Ferragosto on 15 August**. Cities empty
out; family-run restaurants, workshops and small shops close for one to three weeks. The
coast and the lakes fill with the people who left the cities.

The practical result: **August is the worst of both worlds** for most itineraries. Rome and
Florence are hot and stripped of the local life that makes them worth visiting, while
Sorrento and Positano are at maximum crowding and maximum price.

### The Venice access fee

Venice ran an access fee for day visitors on selected 2026 dates — every Friday, Saturday
and Sunday from 3 April to 26 July, plus 6 April, the whole week of 27 April, and the whole
week of 1 June.

| Detail | 2026 terms |
|---|---|
| Cost | **€5** if registered more than four days ahead; **€10** closer in |
| Who pays | Day visitors aged 14+ entering the historic centre, 08:30–16:00 |
| Overnight guests | **Exempt from the fee** — but still must pre-register |
| Status | The 2026 trial ended 27 July; entry is currently free |

**What to do:** check the official calendar before booking spring travel — the scheme has
run in some form for several years and 2027 dates are likely to be announced in late
winter. The simplest way to avoid it entirely is to stay the night, which you should be
doing in Venice anyway.

## Month by Month

### January–February — cold, cheap, uncrowded

Northern cities are genuinely cold and often foggy; the south is mild and wet. Ski season is
in full swing in the Dolomites and the Alps.

**Go for:** Venice without crowds, Rome at half the summer price, **Carnevale** in Venice
(February), skiing.
**Avoid:** the coast and the lakes — largely closed.

### March — the turn

Unpredictable. Can be spring in Naples and winter in Milan in the same week. Prices are
still low and the cities are still quiet.

**Go for:** cities on a budget, early wildflowers in Sicily.

### April — one of the two best months

Warm in the south, pleasant in the centre, still cool in the north. Everything reopens.
**Easter** is the exception — Rome in particular is extremely busy, and prices spike.

**Go for:** Rome, Florence, Puglia, Sicily. Gardens at Lake Como start opening.
**Watch:** Venice access-fee dates cluster here.

### May — arguably the single best month

Warm without heat, long days, everything open, sea starting to be swimmable in the south.
Crowds are building but nowhere near summer.

**Go for:** everything. The Amalfi Coast before the driving restrictions bite hardest,
Tuscany before the tour buses, the Dolomites for lower-altitude walking.

### June — very good, getting busy

Hot in the south, ideal in the north. Sea is warm. The Dolomites hut season opens mid-month.

**Go for:** the lakes, the Dolomites, the northern coast.
**Watch:** prices climb sharply through the month.

### July — hot and full

30–35 °C across most of the country and higher inland in the south. The coast is packed and
priced accordingly. Cities are hard work in the middle of the day.

**Go for:** mountains and the far north. The Dolomites are at their best.
**Avoid:** Rome, Florence and Naples unless you are prepared to work around the heat.

### August — the month to avoid

Peak heat, peak coastal crowds, and the Ferragosto shutdown in the cities.

**If you must go:** head for altitude. The Alps and Dolomites are excellent and the
restaurants stay open because that is their season.

### September — the other best month

The sea is at its warmest after a summer of heating, the crowds thin from mid-month, and
the light is better than at any other time of year. Harvest season across the wine regions.

**Go for:** all of it. This is the month to book if you can only take one trip.

### October — excellent and underrated

Mild, quiet, cheap by comparison. Truffle season in Piedmont and Umbria. Some coastal
resorts start closing late in the month.

**Go for:** Tuscany, Umbria, Piedmont, the cities.

### November–December — low season, with an exception

Wet and grey in the north; mild in the south. Most coastal infrastructure is shut.
**December** is the exception: Christmas markets in the Alto Adige, and the cities are
lovely in the run-up to Christmas before the holiday-week price spike.

## By Region

| Region | Best months | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Rome, Florence, cities | Apr–May, Sept–Oct | Jul–Aug |
| Venice | Apr–May, Sept–Oct; Feb for Carnevale | Jul–Aug |
| Amalfi Coast, Capri | May–Jun, Sept | Aug |
| Tuscany, Umbria | May, Sept–Oct | Jul–Aug |
| Dolomites, Alps (walking) | Late Jun–mid Sept | Nov, Apr (huts shut) |
| Dolomites, Alps (skiing) | Jan–Mar | Summer, for skiing |
| Sicily, Puglia | Apr–Jun, Sept–Oct | Jul–Aug |
| Lakes (Como, Garda) | May–Jun, Sept | Nov–Mar (much closed) |

**The rule of thumb:** the south runs roughly two months ahead of the north. April in Sicily
feels like June in Tuscany.

## What It Costs by Season

Indicative, per person per day, mid-range, excluding flights.

| Season | Daily cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Low (Nov–Mar, excl. Christmas) | €90–€140 | Cities only; coast largely shut |
| Shoulder (Apr–May, Sept–Oct) | €130–€190 | Best value-to-experience ratio |
| Peak (Jun–Aug) | €200–€300+ | Coastal accommodation doubles or worse |

**Where the money actually goes:** accommodation swings hardest by season, food barely
moves. A shoulder-season trip is not a cheaper version of a summer trip — it is a better one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best month to visit Italy?**
May or September. Both give warm weather without July and August heat, everything open, and
crowds well below peak. September has the warmer sea and better light; May has the
wildflowers and slightly lower prices.

**Do I have to pay a fee to enter Venice?**
Only as a day visitor, on specific announced dates. In 2026 the fee applied on selected days
between 3 April and 26 July at €5 per person if registered more than four days ahead, or €10
closer in, for visitors aged 14+ entering the historic centre between 08:30 and 16:00.
Overnight guests are exempt but must still pre-register. The 2026 trial has ended and 2027
dates have not yet been published.

**Is August a bad time to visit Italy?**
For most itineraries, yes. It is the hottest month, the coast is at peak crowding and price,
and many city businesses close for Ferragosto around 15 August. The exception is the
mountains — the Alps and Dolomites are at their best and stay fully open.

**When is the cheapest time to visit Italy?**
November to March, excluding the Christmas and New Year weeks. City accommodation can be
half the summer rate. The trade-off is that coastal and lake destinations largely shut down
for the season.

---

## The Bottom Line

Book May or September if you can, treat August as mountains-only, and check Venice's
access-fee calendar before committing to spring dates.
