# Bangkok Street Food: The 20 Dishes You Must Eat and Where to Find Them

*By [Author] · [Date] · 9 min read*

The mistake most visitors make with Bangkok street food is ordering from a list of dishes
they already know. Pad thai and green curry are fine and they are not why this is one of
the great eating cities.

This guide is organised by what to order and which neighbourhood to order it in — plus how
to choose a stall, which is the skill that actually matters.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **Eat where the queue is Thai**, and where the stall does one thing.
- **Yaowarat (Chinatown)** after dark is the single best eating area in the city.
- **Point at what someone else is eating.** It works better than any phrasebook.
- **Order "mai phet"** for not spicy — and expect it to still be spicy.
- **Budget 40–80 baht a dish.** A full evening of eating costs less than one restaurant
  meal at home.

---

## How to Pick a Stall

This matters more than any specific recommendation, because vendors move and the good ones
change pitch.

1. **One stall, one dish.** Specialists have made the same thing thousands of times. A stall
   with a laminated 40-item menu in English is aiming at you, not at cooks.
2. **Follow the Thai queue**, especially the office-worker queue at lunch.
3. **High turnover means fresh.** Busy is safer than quiet, not less safe.
4. **Watch it cooked.** Wok and grill in front of you is the norm and the point.
5. **Eat where locals sit down.** Plastic stools and a folding table are a good sign.
6. **Be sensible about pre-cut fruit and ice** early in a trip while your stomach adjusts —
   though ice in Bangkok is generally commercially produced and fine.

## Where to Eat

### Yaowarat (Chinatown) — the essential one

Comes alive after about 6pm and runs late. Thai-Chinese specialities: grilled seafood,
guay jub, oyster omelettes, braised goose, bird's nest and dessert stalls.

**Do it as a walk**, not a destination — start at the Ratchawong end and work east along
Yaowarat Road, eating one dish per stop.

### Bang Rak — the underrated one

South of the river bend near the old Oriental. Excellent roast pork and duck, Muslim-Thai
food, and far fewer visitors than Chinatown.

### Ari — where young Bangkok eats

Northern, residential, increasingly fashionable. A mix of old-school stalls and modern
open-front places. Good for a quieter evening.

### Victory Monument — boat noodles

The boat noodle alley here serves tiny, intense bowls designed to be eaten several at a
time. Stacking the empty bowls is part of it.

### Or Tor Kor Market — the produce one

A covered fresh market near Chatuchak, cleaner and pricier than the street, with excellent
prepared food and the best fruit in the city.

### Chatuchak Weekend Market

Enormous, hot, and full of good food if you know to look past the first stalls. Weekends
only.

## The 20 Dishes

**Noodles and rice**

1. **Boat noodles** (*kuay teow reua*) — small, dark, intensely savoury pork or beef broth.
   Order three.
2. **Pad see ew** — wide rice noodles, dark soy, egg, Chinese broccoli. The one to order
   instead of pad thai.
3. **Rad na** — the same noodles under a thick gravy.
4. **Guay jub** — peppery broth with rolled rice noodles and crisp pork belly. Chinatown.
5. **Khao man gai** — poached chicken on rice with ginger-chilli sauce. Deceptively simple,
   endlessly variable.
6. **Khao kha moo** — braised pork leg on rice with mustard greens and egg.
7. **Khao moo daeng** — red barbecue pork on rice with a sweet gravy.
8. **Pad kra pao** — minced pork or chicken with holy basil and chilli, fried egg on top.
   The default Thai lunch and the thing to order when nothing else appeals.

**Grilled and fried**

9. **Moo ping** — grilled pork skewers, eaten with sticky rice. The great Bangkok breakfast.
10. **Gai yang** — grilled chicken, Isaan style.
11. **Sai krok Isaan** — fermented sour sausage, eaten with raw cabbage and chillies.
12. **Hoy tod** — crisp oyster omelette. Chinatown does it best.
13. **Pla pao** — whole salt-crusted grilled fish.

**Salads and soups**

14. **Som tam** — green papaya salad. Order *som tam Thai* to start; *som tam pu pla ra*
    is the fermented, funkier version.
15. **Larb** — minced meat salad with toasted rice powder, lime and mint.
16. **Tom yum goong** — the famous one, and better on the street than in most restaurants.
17. **Kuay teow tom yum** — tom yum as a noodle soup. Under-ordered by visitors.

**Sweet**

18. **Mango sticky rice** (*khao niao mamuang*) — seasonal at its best, March to May.
19. **Kanom krok** — coconut-rice pancakes cooked in a dimpled pan.
20. **Roti** — the banana-and-condensed-milk street version, sold late at night.

## Ordering Without Thai

- **Point.** At the food, or at what the person ahead of you is eating. Universally
  understood, never rude.
- **"Ao nee"** — I'll have this.
- **"Mai phet"** — not spicy. Treat as a direction of travel rather than a promise.
- **"Mai sai nam pla"** — no fish sauce, useful for vegetarians, though stock is often
  meat-based regardless.
- **"Gep tang"** — the bill.
- **Vegetarians:** look for the yellow-and-red **"เจ" (jay)** sign, which indicates strictly
  vegan Buddhist food. Otherwise assume fish sauce.

## When to Go

| Period | Conditions | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Nov–Feb** | Cool season — 25–32 °C, dry | **Best**, and peak |
| Mar–May | Hot, 35 °C+ and humid. Mango season | Punishing midday; best mangoes |
| Jun–Oct | Rainy season — heavy short downpours | Fewer crowds, cheaper, still very workable |

Street food is a year-round activity; the rain arrives in bursts and stalls are covered.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is street food in Bangkok safe to eat?**
Broadly yes, and it is where most Thais eat. The practical rules are to choose stalls with
high turnover, prefer specialists cooking one dish to order in front of you, and follow
local queues. Being cautious with pre-cut fruit in the first days while your stomach adjusts
is sensible, though commercially produced ice is standard in the city.

**Where is the best street food area in Bangkok?**
Yaowarat, the Chinatown district, after dark — best approached as a walk along Yaowarat Road
eating one dish per stop. Bang Rak and the Victory Monument boat-noodle alley are strong
alternatives with far fewer visitors.

**What should I order instead of pad thai?**
Pad see ew, pad kra pao with a fried egg, boat noodles, or khao man gai. All are more
commonly eaten by Thais than pad thai and are usually better executed at street stalls.

**How much does street food cost in Bangkok?**
Most dishes run 40–80 baht, with boat noodles as low as 15–25 baht a bowl. An evening of
eating your way across Yaowarat costs roughly 300–500 baht per person.

---

## The Bottom Line

Walk Yaowarat after dark, order one dish per stop from stalls that only make that dish, and
skip pad thai entirely.
