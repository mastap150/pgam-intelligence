# The Tuscany Road Trip Route: Vineyards, Hill Towns & Where to Stay

*By [Author] · [Date] · 8 min read*

Tuscany is one of the few places where the driving is genuinely part of the point. It is also
where a large number of visitors collect fines they only discover months later, because of a
camera system that is easy to explain and easy to ignore.

Start with the ZTLs. Then the route.

---

## The Quick Answer

- **ZTL cameras.** Restricted traffic zones in every historic centre, camera-enforced, fines
  arriving months later via your hire company. **Park outside the walls, always.**
- **Base in two places**, don't day-trip everything from Florence.
- **The route:** Florence → Chianti → Siena → Val d'Orcia → San Gimignano/Volterra.
- **A week** is right. Ten days is better.
- **Best months:** May, June, September, October.

---

## ZTLs: Read This Before You Drive

**Zona a Traffico Limitato** — restricted traffic zones covering the historic centre of
essentially every Tuscan town, including Florence, Siena, Lucca and San Gimignano.

**How it works:** cameras photograph plates entering the zone. There is no barrier and no
warning beyond a sign that is easy to miss. The fine reaches your hire company, which charges
an administration fee and passes your details on. **The fine itself typically arrives months
after your trip**, often more than one, and people frequently receive several before
realising what happened.

**How to avoid it entirely:**

- **Never drive into a historic centre.** Park in a signed car park outside the walls and
  walk in. Every town has one.
- **Watch for the ZTL sign** — a white circle with a red border, usually with active hours
  beneath it.
- **If your hotel is inside a ZTL**, they can register your plate for temporary access — but
  you must give them the plate number and they must do it. Confirm it was actually done.
- **GPS will route you straight through one.** It does not know or care.

## The Route

### Florence — 2 nights, no car

Collect the car **when you leave**, not when you arrive. Florence is a walking city with a
large ZTL and expensive parking.

### Chianti — 2 nights

The classic drive: the **SR222 (Via Chiantigiana)** between Florence and Siena, through
Greve, Panzano and Castellina. Vineyards, cypresses, and the road most people picture when
they picture Tuscany.

**Wine:** Chianti Classico, marked with the black rooster. Book tastings ahead — the days of
turning up at a cantina are largely gone at the good producers.

### Siena — 1–2 nights

The Piazza del Campo is the finest medieval square in Italy, and the cathedral's inlaid marble
floor is extraordinary. **The Palio** runs on 2 July and 16 August — spectacular, and the city
is impossible to book or move around for days either side.

### Val d'Orcia — 2–3 nights

The UNESCO landscape: rolling hills, isolated farmhouses, cypress avenues. The Tuscany of the
photographs is specifically here.

- **Pienza** — a tiny Renaissance ideal-city and the home of pecorino.
- **Montalcino** — Brunello, the region's most serious wine.
- **Montepulciano** — Vino Nobile, and a dramatic hilltop setting.
- **Bagno Vignoni** — a village built around a thermal pool in the main square.
- **San Quirico d'Orcia** and the cypress groves nearby.

**The best light is early morning**, when mist sits in the valleys. This is worth setting an
alarm for.

### San Gimignano and Volterra — 1–2 nights

**San Gimignano's** medieval towers are remarkable and the town is overwhelmed by day
visitors — **stay the night** and it empties completely after 6pm. **Volterra** is larger,
Etruscan in origin, and far less visited.

## Where to Stay

**Agriturismi** — working farms with rooms, often with a pool and a view, usually including
breakfast and sometimes dinner from the farm. This is the correct way to stay in rural
Tuscany and it costs less than hotels in the towns.

| Base | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Chianti** (Greve, Panzano, Radda) | Florence, Siena, the wine road |
| **Val d'Orcia** (Pienza, Montalcino) | The southern hill towns, Montepulciano |
| **Near San Gimignano** | Volterra, the western hills, Pisa |

**Two bases beat five.** Tuscan roads are slow and beautiful; you do not want to spend the
week packing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a ZTL in Italy and how do I avoid a fine?**
A Zona a Traffico Limitato is a camera-enforced restricted traffic zone covering the historic
centre of most Italian towns. Driving into one without authorisation produces a fine that
reaches you months later via your hire company. Park in a signed car park outside the walls
and walk in — and do not trust GPS, which will route you straight through one.

**How many days do you need for a Tuscany road trip?**
Seven is comfortable — two nights in Florence without a car, then two bases in the
countryside. Ten days lets you add Volterra, the coast or Lucca without rushing.

**Where should I stay in Tuscany?**
An agriturismo — a working farm with rooms — in two bases rather than moving constantly.
Chianti covers Florence and Siena; the Val d'Orcia covers the southern hill towns. Both cost
less than town hotels and are the more characteristic stay.

**When is the best time for a Tuscany road trip?**
May and June, or September and October. Spring is green and full of wildflowers; autumn
brings the harvest and the best light of the year. July and August are hot, busy, and the
landscape has already turned gold.

---

## The Bottom Line

Park outside the walls in every single town, base yourself in two agriturismi rather than
five hotels, and go in May or September.
