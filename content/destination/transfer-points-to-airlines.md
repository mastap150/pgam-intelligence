# How to Transfer Credit Card Points to Airlines: The Complete Guide

> **Not financial advice.** Transfer partners, ratios and award pricing change without notice.
> Verify current terms before transferring.

*By [Author] · [Date] · 7 min read*

There is one rule in points transfers and everything else is detail: **find and hold the award
seat first, transfer second, book immediately.**

Transfers are almost always irreversible. Points moved into a programme where you then cannot
find space are stranded there permanently, and this is the single most common expensive
mistake in the hobby.

---

## The Quick Answer

1. **Search for award space** in the partner programme before moving anything.
2. **Confirm the exact points cost** and that the seat is bookable now.
3. **Transfer only what you need**, plus a small buffer for a pricing change.
4. **Book immediately** — usually the same session.
5. **Never transfer speculatively**, and never chase a transfer bonus into a programme you
   cannot use.

---

## The Order of Operations

**Step 1 — Find the seat.** Search the partner airline's own award engine, or a partner
programme that displays the space well. Confirm the date, the routing and the cabin.

**Step 2 — Note the exact cost in miles**, plus taxes and surcharges. Some programmes add
substantial carrier-imposed surcharges that can make a "cheap" award expensive in cash.

**Step 3 — Check your transfer ratio and timing.** Most transfers are 1:1 but not all, and
transfer speed varies from instant to several days — see below.

**Step 4 — Transfer only what you need.** A small buffer is sensible in case pricing moves;
a large one leaves points stranded in a programme you may not use again.

**Step 5 — Book.** Immediately. Award space can disappear during a transfer, and this is
precisely the risk that instant-transfer partners mitigate.

---

## Transfer Speed Is a Real Risk

| Speed | Risk |
|---|---|
| **Instant** (most major partners) | Low — transfer and book in one session |
| **A few hours** | Moderate |
| **1–5 business days** (some partners) | **High — the seat may be gone** |

**For slow partners:** either hold the seat some other way, or accept the risk consciously.
Some programmes allow a temporary hold on an award booking, which resolves this neatly — check
whether yours does before you need it.

**Never assume speed from memory.** Transfer times change and vary by programme pair.

## Transfer Bonuses

Transferable programmes periodically offer bonuses to specific partners — commonly **20–30%**,
occasionally more. Transferring 50,000 points during a 30% bonus yields 65,000 miles.

**Use them properly:**

- **Only into a programme you will actually use.** A 30% bonus on stranded points is worth
  nothing. This is the mistake bonuses cause.
- **Ideally with the seat already identified.** The bonus is a discount, not a reason.
- **Some pairings run bonuses predictably enough** to be worth waiting a few weeks for, if
  your travel is not imminent.

---

## Choosing Which Programme to Transfer To

The same flight can cost very different amounts through different programmes, because each
partner prices partner awards on its own chart.

**What to compare before transferring:**

1. **The award price in miles** across every programme that can book that flight.
2. **Cash surcharges.** Some programmes pass on large carrier-imposed fees; others do not. A
   lower mileage price with $600 of surcharges may be worse.
3. **Routing rules.** Some allow stopovers or open jaws that add real value.
4. **Change and cancellation terms.** Some programmes refund miles cheaply; some do not
   refund at all.

**A worked illustration:** a transatlantic business class seat on the same aircraft might
price around 45,000 through one programme, 63,000 through another and 65,000–75,000 through a
third. Same seat. That spread is why step one is comparing programmes, not defaulting to the
one you know.

---

## What Not To Do

- **Do not transfer speculatively** in the hope of a future use. The single most common way
  people lose value.
- **Do not transfer to "top up" without checking.** Confirm the shortfall precisely first.
- **Do not transfer to hotel programmes from airline miles** — those rates are consistently
  poor.
- **Do not convert points to cash or gift cards** at ~1¢ if you have any realistic premium
  redemption in mind, which is worth 5–10¢.
- **Do not hoard.** Programmes devalue; a redemption you take at 2¢ beats a theoretical 8¢ you
  never book.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can you reverse a credit card points transfer?**
Almost never. Transfers to airline and hotel programmes are treated as final, and points moved
into a programme where you cannot find award space are effectively stranded. This is why you
find and confirm the seat before transferring, not after.

**How long do points transfers take?**
It varies by programme pair — many major partners are instant, some take a few hours, and
others take one to five business days. Slow transfers carry real risk that the award seat
disappears in the meantime, so check the speed for your specific pairing before relying on it.

**Should I wait for a transfer bonus?**
Only if your travel is not imminent and only into a programme you will genuinely use. A 20–30%
bonus is meaningful, but chasing one into a programme where you cannot find award space
converts flexible points into stranded ones.

**How do I know which airline programme to transfer to?**
Compare the award price for your specific flight across every programme that can book it, then
check cash surcharges, routing rules and cancellation terms. The same seat can vary by 20,000
miles or more between programmes, and a lower mileage price with large surcharges can be the
worse deal.

---

## The Bottom Line

Find the seat, price it across every programme that can book it, transfer exactly what you
need, and book in the same session — and never move points anywhere on the assumption that you
will find a use later.
